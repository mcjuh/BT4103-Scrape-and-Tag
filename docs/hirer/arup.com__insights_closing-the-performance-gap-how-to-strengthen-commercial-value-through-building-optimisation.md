#  Closing the performance gap – how to strengthen commercial value through building optimisation 
A few years into their operational life, too many commercial buildings start to fall short of performance expectations. Just 3-5 years into a building’s lifespan, there’s a strong case for targeted building optimisation – particularly given that underperformance leads to enduring problems and erodes asset value over time. For developers and owners, building optimisation needs to become the norm.

At first glance, every new commercial building seems defined by a range of tangible, confidently stated performance metrics. Yet despite promising projections, too many buildings underperform once occupied, leading to avoidable additional costs, greater carbon emissions, a diminished user/tenant experience and for owners/developers, growing pressure on long-term asset value. 
At Arup we have been considering how investors, developers and asset owners can establish, evidence and improve how their buildings perform in use, using verified operational measurements such as NABERS UK. This evidence is fast becoming a commercial differentiator, not a technical nice-to-have. Buildings that can demonstrate efficiency, resilience and occupant comfort with confidence are increasingly better positioned in the market, helping to inform investment, refurbishment and leasing decisions.Many of our clients with existing buildings are also working towards decarbonisation targets and related resource efficiency commitments – another dimension greatly impacted by their buildings’ operational performance, and a context where details really matter. A wide range of smart building technologies are in theory enabling data-driven decision making, but once more, it’s too easy for gaps between systems and insights and real-world performance to emerge. 
## What building optimisation offers
Beyond the more obvious financial benefits of reduced energy costs, optimisation offers other direct commercial and financial advantages: 
  * **Extended plant life and avoided costs for replacement:** Chiller or heat pump equipment which typically lasts for 20 years and costs hundreds of thousands of pounds, could be worn out in less than 5 years if poorly operated. If run optimally they could last for 30 years. Building optimisation can reveal which pathway a building’s plant is on and enable improved lifespan. 
  * **Opportunities to reduce replacement plant costs** : Major equipment such as chillers, heat-pumps, pumps and fans, worth £100ks, are often proved to be larger than actually required once systems are optimised. Once the evidence for this size mismatch is clear, these items can be replaced at end of their life with a smaller and cheaper replacement. On several buildings we’ve worked on, we have found installed equipment to be at least twice the actual required size. 
  * **Reduced decarbonisation upgrade costs:** Similarly, once a building is optimised, and performance data captured, decarbonisation upgrades such as the replacement of boilers with heat-pumps can be specified to match the actual demands of a building, resulting in reduced costs, reduced embodied carbon and improved efficiency. By using actual performance data, Arup was able to replace existing equipment at our London headquarters with systems that have 15% less cooling capacity and 40% less heating capacity as part of our building decarbonisation programme. These reductions were possible even while accounting for redundancy and climate change. 


For asset managers, this isn’t just about energy payback. The value of optimisation is that it creates a practical bridge between day-to-day performance and longer-term asset strategy. It can identify low-cost operational improvements that support decarbonisation aims, while also improving comfort and reducing pressure on future capital expenditure.

## Overcoming market ‘drift’ 
Given a typical development timeline of between 5 and 10 years, many completed commercial buildings take their place in a changed economic and regulatory reality – bearing a specification that has drifted away from today’s priorities. Consider all the buildings that became operational just before the Covid-19 crisis, specified as they were, for the pre-flexible working era. If we look at energy use alone, since 2022 prices all over the world have become increasingly volatile, and in parallel priorities like decarbonisation can reveal the ways in which many buildings were designed with assumptions that no longer hold true.
## The specification question 
As we’ve seen, just 3 to 5 years into a building’s lifespan, the specifications for a building’s core systems (heating, plumbing, air conditioning, power) may benefit from a forensic review. With this review complete it’s possible to work with a building’s facilities management supplier, to make the technical change happen. The key isn’t to undertake unnecessary changes or to replace like with like. This is an opportunity to refresh and redefine specifications and expectations, and deploy better specified technical solutions, with improved monitoring to understand how well it works in future. 
## Answering ‘why now?’ 
Of course, this level of early scrutiny might be questioned – particularly where owners are already investing in decarbonisation. However, this optimisation does not duplicate that effort; it helps direct it – because the analysis reveals how people are using a building now, enabling a clearer understanding of the impact for the future. Buildings’ actual in-practice use always reflects an evolving set of needs and behaviours, preferences which will have an impact on where intervention is needed, what can be improved immediately, and how future investment should be targeted. 
Case study 
Optimisation in action
At Riverbank House in the City of London, we worked closely with the building’s head lease tenant, to successfully implement targeted optimisation measures resulting in: 
  * Energy reductions of up to 50% on major HVAC systems with a return on investment of just 1.3 years. 
  * A close working relationship with the buildings facility management contractor, working collaboratively to improve both the efficiency, operation, and “maintainability” of systems. 
  * An ongoing holistic approach to building operational performance, with work to maximise optimisations while maintaining (or improving) tenant comfort and a desire for continuous improvement. 


## Protecting long term asset value
Building optimisation is about protecting an investment and vital given the focus developers and owners have on future lettings and resale. It’s about using available information in a more integrated and meaningful way – letting the building’s data speak clearly about performance and galvanising action. 
Given the complexity of buildings’ operations and the commercial relevance they need to maintain, targeted operational review and continuous commissioning can deliver significant reductions in energy use and operating costs, often before major retrofit investment is required. There is a bigger strategic opportunity, however, and being proactive is incredibly important. These early wins help fund further improvements, build organisational confidence and create stronger assets that continue to perform, adapt and deliver value throughout their operational life.
