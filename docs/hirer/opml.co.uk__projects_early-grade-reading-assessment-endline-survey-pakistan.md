The Sindh Early Learning Enhancement through Classroom Transformation (SELECT) project, funded by the World Bank and implemented by the Government of Sindh’s School Education & Literacy Department (SE&LD), was designed to address these gaps by investing in Continuous Professional Development (CPD) for teachers across 12 districts.
We are delivering the SELECT Early Grade Reading Assessment (EGRA) Endline Survey to assess whether and to what extent these investments have improved student reading outcomes. The study covers approximately 7,252 Grade 2 students across 604 government primary schools. It uses standardised EGRA tools to measure skills in letter sound recognition, decoding, oral reading fluency, and reading comprehension in both Sindhi and Urdu.
The SELECT project builds on a 2024 baseline assessment and delivers both accountability and learning value – establishing what reading gains have been achieved, which groups have benefited most, and how classroom practices have changed as a result of teacher professional development.
The findings are used to revise Sindhi and Urdu reading performance standards for Early Childhood Education (ECE) to Grade 5, ensuring that provincial benchmarks reflect what students can realistically achieve and what good teaching looks like in Sindh’s classrooms.
### Challenges
Measuring whether a professional development programme has genuinely improved how children read is difficult. Teachers across Sindh’s 12 SELECT districts vary in their training exposure, classroom conditions, and language of instruction, some teach in Sindhi, others in Urdu. Getting a reliable, comparable picture of reading outcomes across this diversity requires careful design.
The Survey must reach the same schools assessed at the baseline in 2024, administer standardised oral assessments to over 7,000 Grade 2 students, and do so with a level of consistency that makes the results meaningful for policy. Field implementation across 12 districts brings its own challenges: variable school access, security considerations in some areas, low Grade 2 enrolment in certain districts, and the risk that external programmes or policy changes influence reading outcomes independently of SELECT.
We have planned carefully for these risks. Our sampling approach allows for school substitution where needed, our field teams are trained to inter-rater reliability standards of at least 90% agreement before deployment, and our analysis framework explicitly accounts for confounding factors. The endline also introduces a new task – letter sound recognition – not included in the 2024 baseline, which requires additional piloting and validation before full-scale data collection.
### Expertise
We are using a sequential explanatory mixed methods design that combines standardised student assessments with classroom observations, structured questionnaires, Key Informant Interviews (KIIs), and Focus Group Discussions (FGDs). This design allows us to measure what has changed in reading outcomes and then explain why, using evidence from teachers, headteachers, and community members.
EGRA is administered to Grade 2 students using Tangerine™ – a digital data collection platform that enables real-time encrypted uploads and live quality monitoring. Classroom observations use the SELECT-TEACH tool, applying standardised rubrics to document instructional practices consistently across schools. Quantitative analysis is conducted in Stata, covering baseline-to-endline comparisons, gender- and district-disaggregated results, zero-scorer rates, and multivariate regression linking SELECT exposure to reading outcomes. Qualitative data from KIIs and FGDs are analysed in Dedoose through a deductive thematic framework.
All field activities follow strict ethical and safeguarding standards, including voluntary participation, written parental consent, child assent, and a “do no harm” approach throughout. Assessors are trained to stop immediately if a child shows signs of discomfort. All data are anonymised before analysis and stored on secure, password-protected servers. 
### Impact
We are generating the first rigorous evidence on what two years of teacher professional development under the SELECT programme have achieved for early reading in Sindh. For the Government of Sindh, Pakistan, and the World Bank, this means a credible, data-driven picture of student reading gains across 12 districts, disaggregated by gender, language of instruction, district, and school location, that can directly inform decisions about scaling, adapting, or redesigning the programme.
Beyond accountability, the endline is designed to generate practical learning. Our findings show which instructional practices are most strongly associated with reading gains, and how school leadership and home learning environments shape outcomes. This evidence helps government and its partners make smarter investments in literacy, understanding not just whether outcomes improved, but why, and what to do next.
Through this work, we are living our mission to improve lives through sustainable change, putting evidence at the heart of decisions that shape how thousands of children in Sindh, Pakistan, can learn to read and write.
