This project supports integration of climate resilience into HIV, Tuberculosis and Malaria (HTM) programming and development of climate-resilient health systems across eight African countries: Chad, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Ethiopia, Ghana, Mali, Mozambique, Nigeria, and Somalia. Working in partnership with national governments and the Global Fund, the initiative responds to growing concerns about the impact of climate change on public health and the sustainability of programmes addressing HTM.
Key stakeholders include Ministries of Health, Environment and Finance, national public health institutions, civil society organisations, the World Health Organization and other technical partners. The project operates within a broader policy debate on how to strengthen health systems’ resilience to climate-related shocks, improve cross-sector coordination, and ensure that adaptation investments are evidence-based and financially viable.
The project’s primary objective is to help countries identify climate-related risks to health systems and vulnerable populations, and to translate this evidence into practical adaptation and disaster risk reduction plans. Through climate and health assessments, stakeholder engagement and strategic planning, the initiative aims to support countries in developing prioritised, costed and investment-ready actions that strengthen resilience and safeguard health outcomes in an increasingly challenging climate context.
### Challenges
This project addresses the growing threat that climate change poses to public health systems and disease control programmes. Across many African countries, rising temperatures, extreme weather events, droughts, floods and changing disease patterns are increasing pressure on already stretched health systems. These challenges can disrupt the delivery of essential services, damage infrastructure, increase the vulnerability of at-risk populations and undermine progress in the fight against HTM. At the same time, many countries lack the evidence, coordination mechanisms and planning tools needed to effectively assess and manage climate-related health risks.Addressing these issues is complex. One of the main challenges is the need to bring together stakeholders from different sectors, including health, environment and finance, which often operate under separate priorities and systems. Data limitations may also make it difficult to accurately assess future climate risks and their health impacts. In addition, resource constraints and competing development priorities can hinder the implementation of adaptation measures. Ensuring that plans are both technically robust and financially feasible requires strong stakeholder engagement and sustained political commitment. The project therefore seeks not only to identify risks, but also to build consensus and develop practical, evidence-based solutions that can be implemented and sustained over the long term.
### Expertise
The project combines climate risk analysis, health systems assessment and participatory planning to support the development of climate-resilient health systems. A central methodology is the Climate-Health Vulnerability Assessment, which examines how current and future climate hazards may affect health systems, health outcomes and programmes addressing HTM. These assessments draw on climate, epidemiological and health systems data to identify vulnerabilities, priority risks and gaps in existing capacities. There are several models for developing and designing climate-health vulnerability and adaptation assessments and our project is using most commonly used and accepted WHO guidance: [Climate change and health: vulnerability and adaptation assessment](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240036383).The project also uses a multi-stakeholder engagement approach, bringing together representatives from ministries responsible for health, environment and finance, alongside public health institutions, civil society organisations and technical partners. Workshops, consultations and validation exercises are used to ensure that findings are grounded in national priorities and supported by key decision-makers.Building on the assessment findings, the project supports the development of Climate-Health Adaptation Plans (CHAPs). These plans identify and prioritise adaptation and disaster risk reduction measures, including community-led and gender-responsive interventions, and assess their costs and feasibility. Policy and institutional reviews are also undertaken to evaluate existing governance arrangements, financing mechanisms and coordination structures. These initiatives are followed by costing the CHAPs using a robust methodology and tools that can be adapted to national and sub-national contexts. By integrating evidence generation, stakeholder collaboration and strategic planning, the project produces practical, investment-ready recommendations that help countries strengthen resilience and prepare for future climate-related health risks.
### Impact
This project will enable participating countries to better understand, anticipate and respond to the health impacts of climate change. By identifying climate-related vulnerabilities and risks within health systems and programmes addressing HTM, the project will support the development of practical and costed adaptation measures that strengthen resilience at national and local levels.The expected impact is improved preparedness for climate-related health threats, stronger coordination between health, climate and finance stakeholders, and more informed resource allocation and decision-making based on robust evidence and risk analysis. It is envisaged, that the health systems will be better equipped to protect vulnerable populations, maintain essential services during climate shocks, and safeguard progress towards public health goals. The project will also help countries to mobilise investment for climate adaptation by developing clear, prioritised and implementation-ready plans, informed by the costing exercise. This work directly supports our organisational vision of helping governments and partners achieve sustainable and inclusive development outcomes. It reflects our commitment to evidence-based policymaking, institutional strengthening and long-term resilience. By bringing together technical expertise, stakeholder engagement and practical planning, the project helps countries to be aware and better prepared for interconnected challenges of climate change and public health, ensuring that development gains are protected and that health systems can continue to serve communities effectively in an increasingly uncertain climate future.
