The West of England needs to attract private investment to deliver a just energy transition. Achieving this requires coordinated action across the public sector, a credible pipeline of projects and a clear approach to engaging delivery partners.
Working with regional partners and stakeholders, Arup identified £3bn of near-term investment needed across the local energy system and developed an action plan to support a just transition for heat, power, transport, industry and buildings. In collaboration with Global City Futures, we also created a process that enables projects to move quickly from conception into development, funding and delivery. 
Alongside cutting carbon emissions, a just energy transition will improve health and wellbeing, strengthen communities, drive economic growth and create high-quality green jobs. Investment in the opportunities outlined through the plan could generate an estimated 6,500 jobs across the region’s green economy. 
Our work informed the West of England’s investment proposition and identified clean energy as a priority sector within the region’s Growth Strategy. 
## Identifying the investment opportunity
We established a shared vision and strategic roadmap for accelerating investment in clean energy, supporting economic growth and delivering a just transition across Bath, Bristol and North East Somerset, North Somerset and South Gloucestershire. 
Working with Global City Futures and Advanced Infrastructure Technologies Limited, we set out the tools needed to drive faster, fairer and more effective change across the region. We demonstrated how clean energy investment can help meet regional and national climate goals, reduce fuel poverty, empower communities through participation in project design, and support clean economic growth and job creation. 
This was a critical input to the West of England Mayoral Combined Authority’s 10‑year Growth Strategy, helping to guide the strategic use of devolved powers and funding to attract private investment and maximise impact. 
Our analysis identified capital expenditure requirements across five sectors: buildings, heat, electricity, industry and transport. We found opportunities to retrofit 300,000 homes, unlock up to 2.5GW of renewable energy generation, and expand low-carbon heat supply across the region. 
## Five investment portfolios 
Using a whole‑systems approach, we used this analysis to inform the development of five cross‑cutting investment portfolios designed to accelerate clean energy delivery at scale. These portfolios represent the region’s primary investment areas. 
### 1. Virtual power market 
A programme of solar PV and battery storage for large roof spaces across the region. We identified more than 1,200 suitable buildings with rooftop solar potential capable of generating over 250GWh of electricity annually. 
### 2. Community energy accelerator
A network of neighbourhood energy systems combining technologies such as battery storage, renewable power generation, heat pumps, shared ground loops, and electric vehicle charging infrastructure.
### 3. Low carbon homes programme 
Building on the work of Retrofit West, this retrofit programme targets owner-occupier and social housing. It supports the deployment of heat pumps, energy efficiency measures, solar PV, battery storage and EV charging. The programme has the potential to save 12,000 TCO₂ and £4.5 million in energy bills each year. 
### 4. Clean growth hubs 
Focused on industrial and commercial areas, these hubs bring together and facilitate multiple technologies or approaches to decarbonisation, helping businesses accelerate their transition to lower-carbon operations. 
### 5. Low carbon businesses programme 
An energy efficiency, renewable generation and heat decarbonisation programme targeting the region’s 40,000+ small and medium-sized enterprises, as well as their landlords.

## Pipeline and operating model design
We developed a structured approach to mobilising investment and building a robust pipeline of clean energy projects across the West of England. The model supports projects that decarbonise the region’s energy system, providing a practical route from strategic ambition to project delivery.
Recognising constraints on public finances, the approach places a strong emphasis on attracting private investment alongside public funding. It provides a clear framework for developing, prioritising and delivering projects at scale. The outputs of this work formed the foundation of a programme business case, setting out preferred funding mechanisms, delivery models and long‑term resourcing requirements to implement and operate at scale. 
Collectively, the work positions the West of England as a leading region for net zero investment, supported by a strategically aligned and investment‑ready project pipeline.
