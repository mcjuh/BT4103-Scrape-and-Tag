# Integrated due diligence: A new lens for sustainable infrastructure
When people hear the term “sustainable infrastructure,” many still picture the usual suspects—wind farms, solar panels, EV charging, and fleet electrification. Recent global upheaval, however, has meant that the world, and therefore what we call ‘sustainable’, has changed.
Today, sustainability is not just about reducing impact, it is about managing volatility. It is also about climate resilience, resource efficiency, social responsibility, and economic sustainability. Proving, with numbers and tangible, deliverable solutions rather than narratives, that sustainability both creates and protects value.
It is this need for evidence where integrated technical and sustainability due diligence steps in by validating whether an asset can truly deliver continuity and performance under stress and whether its design, technology, and operational assumptions hold up against real-world volatility and sustainability challenges. 
**Resilience: The new due diligence premium**
Climate reality, whereby extreme weather events are becoming more frequent and severe, has highlighted economic risk, with failures causing significant losses and underlining physical vulnerabilities. Combined with geopolitical stressors, macroeconomic pressures, and policy evolutions, resilience is a topic on the rise within infrastructure investment circles.
Why? Resilience delivers key support, helping infrastructure investors ensure the long-term success of their projects and investments whether by:
  * Providing cash flows able to survive shocks;
  * Keeping supply chains operating when external pressures increase;
  * Enabling flexibility to adapt to policy and regulatory shifts; or
  * Maintaining community trust and a social license to operate.


**How integrated due diligence****unlocks and protects lasting value**
For infrastructure investors, every deal carries new complexity. Investors are not just underwriting an asset; they are underwriting supply chains, political risk, regulatory regimes, and potential climate events. In this new environment, investment decisions rely more than ever on the strength of due diligence.
That is why integrated technical and sustainability due diligence is key. The approach links technical feasibility and performance (e.g., asset conditions, engineering, operations, etc.) with sustainability risks and opportunities (e.g., decarbonization, physical climate risks, social performance, etc.) to inform investment and transaction decisions. By integrating these perspectives, investors can unlock clarity, confidence, and faster deal execution to drive growth, protect margins, and create lasting value across investment portfolios.
A leading sustainability strategy will fail if an asset or project cannot operate as intended, with failure risks including:
  * **Cost overruns —** inaccurate cost estimates for physical risk mitigation or compliance retrofits
  * **Stranded assets —** high emission assets risk being stranded without technical solutions to meet future sustainability standards**​**
  * **Reputational harm —** misalignment of contractual, technical, and sustainability obligations can trigger penalties and reputational harm**​**
  * **Climate vulnerability —** exposure to flooding, heat stress, or extreme weather events can impair asset performance and valuation


_What these risks look like in reality_
These risks are not theoretical, and some high-profile cases show how environmental dependencies can quickly translate into financial and operational consequences. For example, [Tesla’s](https://tnfd.global/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/BNEF_When-the-Bee-Stings_Tesla.pdf) Berlin Gigafactory faced months-long delays in 2022 after concerns over local groundwater depletion triggered investigations and stalled construction, leading to $5.7 billion in additional costs.
**Integrated due diligence is a resilience test**
Integrated due diligence validates design redundancies, stress-tests business models, and assesses scenarios against key sustainability challenges and opportunities. Pricing these resilience measures into valuations helps:
  * **Unlock growth —** by delivering technical solutions that boost the bottom line, reduce GHG emissions, decarbonize infrastructure, and improve exit multiples at the end of an investment period
  * **Protect margins —** by enabling robust risk mitigation strategies with technical solutions incorporated into today’s biggest sustainability challenges**​**
  * **Ensure a social license to operate —** by integrating community considerations into technical planning and operational procedures
  * **Deliver lasting value —** by bolstering a stronger reputation and strengthening exit valuations


_What these benefits look like in reality_
Recent ERM projects highlight how integrated due diligence directly strengthens commercial outcomes. For instance, ERM [helped](https://www.erm.com/insights/fleet-decarbonization-strengthening-business-value-creation-through-targeted-emissions-reductions/) a U.S.-based company reduce its fleet’s emissions intensity by 34 percent and save $2.2 million in operational and fuel costs after due diligence revealed that a staged approach to fleet electrification, rather than a full transition, would best balance emissions reductions, operational savings, and capital efficiency.
In short, resilience is a value driver, a differentiator that protects exits and stabilizes returns. Integrated technical and sustainability due diligence is thus key to unlocking a commercially focused view and moving from narrative to numbers—sharpening understanding and pricing of risks and opportunities to accelerate execution and future proof investments.
**What’s Next?**
The next phase of infrastructure investing will not be won by assets that are simply “green”, but by those that can perform through disruption. As volatility becomes structural, resilience is no longer a narrative—it is a test of value, cash flow durability, and exit certainty.
Integrated due diligence turns ambition into assurance, translating climate, operational, and social risks into quantified, actionable insights. It is how investors move from stories to stress tested value, protect downside, and build infrastructure portfolios designed not just to transition, but to endure.
