# The mainframe advantage
As AI scales into production, enterprises are re‑evaluating where their most critical workloads should run—and realizing that the mainframe is central to an AI-powered future.
[Download the insights (will open in a new tab)](https://www.ibm.com/downloads/documents/us-en/16951f0d5c615e97)[Subscribe for more insights from IBM IBV (will open in a new window)](https://www.ibm.com/thought-leadership/institute-business-value/en-us/newsletters)
For more than a decade, cloud‑first was the default choice for enterprise technology. It promised speed, flexibility, and lower upfront costs—and for many workloads, it delivered. But the AI era is changing the equation. As AI moves out of pilots and into production, and as intelligence becomes part of live transactions, architecture decisions are no longer just technical choices. They are business decisions, with real consequences for cost, resilience, and trust.
New research from the IBM Institute for Business Value (IBV) shows a clear shift underway. Nearly three‑quarters of executives are moving away from cloud‑first defaults toward placing workloads based on outcomes. That shift reflects experience, not ideology. 72% of organizations report higher‑than‑expected cloud costs once applications reach production. As AI becomes part of live transactions—approving payments, detecting fraud, enforcing compliance—the tolerance for disruption, cost volatility, or gaps in control drops sharply.
For leaders, the question is no longer “Where can we run AI?” but “How do we bring AI to the applications and data where mission‑critical execution occurs?” For many organizations, that question is leading them back to a platform that has always sat at the heart of execution: the mainframe.
> For leaders, the question is no longer “Where can we run AI?”, but “How do we bring AI to the applications and data where mission‑critical execution occurs?”
###  From systems of record to systems of intelligent execution
For years, the mainframe was described as a system of record—reliable, essential, but static. That framing no longer holds. As AI moves into live operations, the systems that execute transactions are becoming the systems that think. The question is no longer where insight is generated, but where decisions are made.
That shift is exposing a gap between experimentation and execution, clearly reflected by 72% of organizations seeing cloud production costs exceeding expectations. What works in pilots often fails in production—especially when AI is embedded directly into the flow of work rather than layered on top of it.
Mainframes behave differently because they were built for constant execution, not bursts of activity. They are designed to run the most important processes of the business without interruption, with controls built in rather than added later. That matters when AI is approving transactions, flagging risk, or enforcing policy in real-time—not hours or days after the fact.
Executives see this clearly. 75% expect mainframe‑based applications to remain central to digital transformation, and 60% say mainframe platforms are essential to enabling AI innovation. The mainframe is no longer just keeping score. It is becoming the place where intelligence meets execution—at scale, under pressure, and with accountability built in. 
### Why hybrid wins—and why execution needs an anchor
Hybrid architecture has emerged as the winning platform strategy for running mission‑critical work. Nearly half of executives say hybrid delivers the best overall outcomes for the transactional applications that run the business. What has changed is not the need for flexibility, but the discipline behind platform decisions. As AI moves into production, leaders are no longer relying on a single default. They are choosing platforms based on what workloads must deliver—predictable execution, clear economics, and built‑in control.
That discipline is already showing up in how leaders place workloads. Nearly three in four executives are moving away from cloud‑first defaults and toward outcome‑based workload placement. The shift reflects experience, not theory. As AI becomes part of live operations, leaders are learning that execution, cost, and control cannot be treated as separate concerns. They rise—or fall—together.
In this environment, hybrid only works if execution is anchored. Systems that handle live transactions must behave predictably every time, under pressure. This is where the mainframe stands apart. Among single‑platform options, executives prefer the mainframe nearly five‑to‑one over public cloud alone for mission‑critical transactional workloads. The preference is not about history. It is about certainty—knowing where the business cannot afford surprises.
Other platforms continue to support experimentation and change at the edges. But the pattern is clear: intelligence is extending outward, while execution is being pulled back to the core. Hybrid is no longer about spreading work across platforms. It is about putting execution where it is strongest—and protecting the systems that carry the most risk.
> The pattern is clear: intelligence is extending outward, while execution is being pulled back to the core.
###  The economics reality check: production reveals the truth
Economic optimism thrives in pilots—and fades in production. Especially with AI. 
Once AI systems move into steady use, assumptions break down quickly. 72% of organizations report higher‑than‑expected cloud costs once applications reach production, with public‑cloud costs averaging a mean of 1.5 times initial expectations. These are not edge cases. They are what happens when AI moves from experimentation into execution.
What changes in production is not demand, but behavior. AI workloads run continuously. They move more data. They demand higher reliability. Costs stop following forecasts and start reflecting reality. In this environment, executives consistently rate the mainframe higher than public cloud on cost per transaction, cost predictability, and total cost of ownership. The difference shows up at scale, under pressure, day after day.
The response has been decisive. 73% of organizations have planned, attempted, or completed the return of workloads from public cloud to on-prem or private cloud environments. This is not a retreat from modernization. It is a course correction—placing core execution where economics are stable, visible, and governed. As AI increases compute demands, platforms that obscure cost behaviour lose credibility. Platforms that make economics predictable regain trust.
Yet the research also exposes a leadership gap. Only 14% of organizations consistently compare costs across platforms. As AI magnifies inefficiencies, this lack of economic discipline becomes more dangerous. Leaders who treat cost as an after‑the‑fact report will struggle to scale AI responsibly. In the AI era, predictable economics are not a finance concern. They are an execution advantage. 
**Action guide**
### Anchoring AI where execution matters most
As AI moves from pilots into production, architecture decisions become leadership decisions. Organizations that scale AI successfully are not chasing platforms. They are applying discipline—deciding where intelligence belongs based on how work must execute, how costs behave at scale, and how trust is maintained under pressure.
#### For CIOs and CTOs: reset the rules
  * **Move beyond cloud‑first mandates.** Require clear justification for where every mission‑critical, AI‑enabled workload runs—based on execution, economics, and governance.
  * **Design for production economics.** Make cost per transaction and cost behavior under sustained load standard measures before scaling AI.
  * **Keep AI close to core systems when decisions matter.** When AI affects money, customers, or regulatory exposure, prioritize platforms built for predictable execution and control.
  * **Elevate execution decisions.** Changes that affect core transaction systems should receive executive‑level review. AI raises the cost of failure—and governance must reflect that. 


#### For enterprise architects: design hybrid intentionally
  * **Anchor execution deliberately.** Separate workloads that must run continuously and predictably from those designed for experimentation or analysis.
  * **Test for real conditions.** Validate AI‑enabled workflows under full volume, peak demand, and failure scenarios—not just pilot environments.
  * **Standardize integration.** Use consistent interfaces and monitoring to prevent hidden data paths and governance gaps.
  * **Modernize without destabilizing the core.** Focus on integration, testing, data design, and release discipline—not just code conversion. 


#### For all leaders: connect execution, economics, and trust
AI removes the buffer in architecture decisions. When intelligence runs inside transactions, execution, cost, and control can no longer be traded off against one another. Organizations that anchor mission‑critical AI on platforms designed for sustained execution—while extending flexibility elsewhere—build advantage faster and with fewer surprises. In the AI era, the mainframe is not a legacy choice. It is a strategic one.

**FAQs**
Why are leaders rethinking cloud‑first strategies for AI‑driven workloads?
As AI moves into live transaction paths, the cost of failure rises sharply. 72% of organizations experienced higher‑than‑expected cloud production costs, and nearly three‑quarters of executives are moving away from cloud‑first defaults toward outcome‑based workload placement. When execution, economics, and trust must hold at the same time, cloud‑centric architectures often struggle to deliver predictable performance and governance at scale. 
What role does the mainframe play in the AI era?
The mainframe is evolving from a system of record into a system of intelligent execution. 75% of executives expect mainframe‑based applications to remain central to digital transformation, and 60% say they are essential to enabling AI innovation. Its advantage lies in embedding AI directly into transactions while sustaining reliability, auditability, and performance under continuous load. 
How do mainframes compare with public cloud on economics at scale?
Cloud economics often look attractive in pilots but become volatile in production. Executives report public‑cloud costs averaging a mean of 1.5 times initial expectations, with 72% exceeding forecasts once workloads reach production. In contrast, organizations rate the mainframe higher on cost per transaction, cost predictability, and total cost of ownership, driving 73% to plan, attempt, or complete workload repatriation from public cloud to on-prem or private cloud environments. 
Why is hybrid architecture becoming the dominant model—and why anchor it on the mainframe?

The research shows organizations increasingly anchor execution on the mainframe for predictability and control, while using cloud environments closer to the edge for flexibility and innovation. Among single‑platform options, the mainframe is preferred nearly five‑to‑one over public cloud alone for critical transactions. 
How does the mainframe strengthen trust, governance, and data sovereignty for AI?
When AI operates inside regulated, customer‑facing transactions, governance cannot be added later. Executives increasingly prefer to keep AI close to core systems, rising from 21% today to 38% preferred. Mainframes are valued because they keep control, auditability, and accountability built into execution, helping organizations meet regulatory and sovereignty requirements as AI scales.
