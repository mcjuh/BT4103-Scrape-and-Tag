# A Strategic Guide to Choosing a Manufacturing ERP System

In the dynamic world of discrete, process, and hybrid manufacturing, staying competitive means having effective tools that support decision making. A modern Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) system is the central tool connecting production, inventory, purchasing, finance and sales so that they can share real-time data, streamline operations and support informed decisions. But how does an organization choose an ERP that suits their needs?
## Phase 1: Conducting a Strategic Assessment
Before exploring the array of ERP options, it is essential to clearly define strategic goals, such as reducing production delays, improving inventory accuracy, automating purchasing and planning, consolidating data into one system, and enhancing traceability or compliance. Consider:
  * How might a new ERP enhance operational visibility and profitability in real time?
  * What features could increase business agility?
  * Are there specific business requirements or industry regulations that an out-of-the-box ERP might not address?
  * What are potential impacts on the organization and the strategic direction of the company? 
  * What are the guiding principles that will define successful business outcomes?


Involve key stakeholders early in the process to gather diverse perspectives and address pain points.
## Phase 2: Selecting the Right Manufacturing ERP with Scalability in Mind
An ERP system should address current needs and have the capabilities to adapt as the organization grows. Cloud-based solutions offer flexibility, but it's important to choose a system that aligns with future growth plans.
With business goals in mind, explore ERP options that align with them. Consider:
  * Size and Scale of Operations: Can the ERP accommodate current operations and scale as the business grows.
  * Specific Type of Manufacturing: Look for ERP solutions tailored to specific manufacturing needs, whether discrete, process, or mixed-mode.
  * Complexity of Revenue Streams: Select an ERP capable of managing the intricacies of the organization's revenue models.


Remember, not all ERPs are the same. Focus on solutions designed for manufacturing and assess whether a monolithic or composable architecture best fits organizational requirements. Scalability and alignment with long-term growth should remain top priorities.
## Phase 3: Configuration vs. Customization
The purpose of application configuration is to meet business requirements and outcomes. While configuration is expected, extensive customization - writing of code or changing data tables - can complicate future updates and create reliance on a few key employees. Choose options that maintain agility and ease of updates.
## Phase 4: Implementation and Updates for Continuous Improvement
Successful ERP implementation relies on several key factors that enable a smooth transition and long-term efficiency:
  * Identify Risks and Showstoppers: Understand potential challenges and have plans ready to address them.
  * Align with Executive Principles: Ensure alignment with guiding principles and track metrics to measure success.
  * Communicate Goals and Leverage Technology: Clearly communicate objectives and use technology to drive organizational change.
  * Engage Users and Test Thoroughly: Prepare for deployment by engaging users, conducting thorough testing, and seeking feedback.


Implementation is just the beginning of an ERP transformation. Continuous engagement and proactive problem-solving are essential to keep the ERP system running efficiently and foster ongoing improvement.
