# Integrated Transfer Pricing and Customs Value Solutions
The need to coordinate transfer pricing and trade and customs
Pricing Decisions Now Drive Both Tax and Tariffs 
Transfer pricing and trade and customs have traditionally operated in parallel, with one focused on income tax and profitability and the other focused on import values and duty compliance. That separation no longer works. 
While transfer pricing policies and adjustments may influence the values declared for related-party imports. Pricing decisions now carry simultaneous tax and duty consequences, making this a shared financial issue. 
The Misalignment
Both functions evaluate related-party pricing, though they do so through different frameworks. Without coordination, this creates a structural disconnect. Prices set to achieve arm’s-length tax outcomes may also form the basis of customs values, but the two frameworks apply different rules and documentation requirements. Transfer pricing operates on periodic testing while customs authorities apply values transaction by transaction. Year-end adjustments may improve tax results but may increase duties and trigger additional obligations. As a result of these instances of misalignment, transfer pricing policy can be optimized for tax while still creating cost or risk for customs. 
Tariffs Have Raised the Stakes 
Tariffs have turned pricing into a margin-sensitive decision. A single adjustment can shift both sides of the equation, either improving tax outcomes while increasing duty costs or reducing tariffs while introducing transfer pricing risk. In many cases, tariff costs may offset or erode expected tax benefits. Decisions made in isolation can now create real financial leakage. 
The Gap
Most companies have a transfer pricing framework in place but lack a coordinated approach across transfer pricing and customs. Intercompany prices are often set first, with customs implications assessed later. This reactive model leads to unexpected duty costs, inconsistent positions and increased audit exposure. 
## A Better Approach: Shared Framework 
Leading organizations are moving to a coordinated model where transfer pricing and trade and customs are evaluated together before pricing decisions are implemented.
### A Shared Framework Brings Clarity Across
  * Exposure: Where tariffs and margin are most impacted 
  * Alignment: Whether transfer pricing policies support declared customs values 
  * Adjustment impact: How true-ups affect duties 
  * Total cost: Tax, tariffs and earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA) are viewed together. 
  * Defensibility: Consistent, audit-ready support
