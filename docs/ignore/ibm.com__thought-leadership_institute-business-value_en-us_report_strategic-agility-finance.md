# Dynamic finance at work
Turning decision speed into performance gains
[Download the insights (will open in a new tab)](https://www.ibm.com/downloads/documents/us-en/15db1d60a641f6e4)[Subscribe for more insights from IBM IBV (will open in a new window)](https://www.ibm.com/thought-leadership/institute-business-value/en-us/newsletters)
Finance is no longer defined by how well it tracks performance—but by how decisively it shapes outcomes.
As AI transforms everything from business and operating models to customer experience and supply chains, leaders need to stay on top of what’s working, what’s not, and what’s next. Not all AI capabilities are created equal. Some offer quick wins; others offer long-term potential. Many ideas sound great in principle but stumble in practice.
That’s where finance comes in. Not just as a scorekeeper, but an embedded execution partner. Finance teams that stay close to AI and digital transformation initiatives enable business agility. They dynamically allocate capital, model evolving disruptions, risks and opportunities, and help business and technology teams understand whether strategic initiatives and investments are delivering the intended value at every stage.
> Finance teams that lean into speed and innovation drive business impact.
In an AI-powered business landscape, a more dynamic finance function is essential to achieve the necessary speed, trust, and control at scale. When finance is embedded early, transformation accelerates and strategic agility increases. When finance lags, progress stalls and value erodes.
New research from the IBM Institute for Business Value (IBV), conducted in collaboration with Oracle, reveals a stark performance divide. Our survey of 600 CFOs and other senior finance leaders across 15 geographies and 18 industries shows that only 12% of finance organizations demonstrate advanced maturity in both strategic influence and digital agility. (Download the report for our full research methodology.)
  * **Strategic influence** is defined as finance’s contribution to enterprise value, including innovation and growth initiatives, influence on strategy and investment decisions, and adoption of value-based KPIs.
  * **Digital agility** is defined as finance’s use of technology to operate at speed and scale, including scenario planning, integration of finance technology, and AI maturity in forecasting and planning.


These organizations consistently deliver a competitive advantage by outperforming peers: 63% report effective strategy execution compared to 46% of less advanced organizations. They also make innovation funding decisions 19% faster and have seen higher ROI from enterprise resource planning (ERP) and enterprise performance management (EPM) modernization.

###  Finance accelerates value creation
The most advanced finance organizations don’t wait to respond to strategic guidance, they provide it. These leaders operate finance as a connected, decision-centric function; integrated into transformation offices, investment prioritization forums, and technology governance. By institutionalizing finance’s role in transformation, they help ensure that capital, capability, and strategic intent move in sync.
This embedded model gives finance direct influence over what gets funded, how initiatives are sequenced, and which efforts scale—rather than limiting its role to tracking performance after decisions are made.
> While nearly half of finance functions collaborate on innovation planning, only 13% play a true strategic role in guiding innovation and transformation.
The most advanced finance organizations also invest in decision infrastructure: enterprise-wide financial and operational data platforms, scenario-based planning and reallocation processes, and automated investment and performance reviews. Near-real-time insights, scenario-based modeling, and automated variance analysis reduce the lag between signal and action, powering faster, more informed decisions.
Driving value at scale requires a new operating model for finance. The very mechanisms designed to ensure control—fixed budgets, annual planning cycles, and narrow financial KPIs—can slow decision-making and dilute impact. Yet to optimize impact, finance must continuously evolve how it plans, funds, and measures success. 
### Value-based finance replaces rigid control
As our new research shows, finance must take a more integrated, more agile approach to provide the real-time data and decision-making support their organization needs. Its role has expanded beyond explaining what has already happened to defining what will happen next. Accuracy, control, and stewardship are still essential—but they’re no longer enough.
In a dynamic business environment, market conditions shifts, risks and supply chain disruptions emerge, and opportunities surface every day. Organizations that can reallocate capital quickly—to where it creates the most value—win.
> Only 8% of finance teams operate with fully dynamic planning.
The logic is simple. Still, many finance functions remain locked in rigid structures: fixed budgets, annual planning cycles, and efficiency-only KPIs. In fact, only 8% operate with fully dynamic financial planning and budgeting processes, according to our research.
How is such dynamism brought to life? Our study indicates that modernized EPM platforms are essential. These platforms integrate data, automate workflows, and surface insights for decision-makers. Some 82% of advanced finance organizations report improvements in decision-making timeliness following EPM modernization. And seven in 10 report faster and more accurate forecasting and stronger cross-functional planning alignment.
**FAQs**
How can building a more dynamic finance function improve innovation?
It fuels faster, data-driven decisions. The most advanced finance organizations make innovation funding decisions 19% faster than peers—and 63% report effective strategy execution that drives faster, iterative innovation cycles and more dependable outcomes. 
How dynamic are current financial planning and budgeting processes at most organizations?
41% of financial planning and budgeting processes are somewhat dynamic and 34% are regularly updated for new scenarios. Only 8% are fully dynamic and responsive. 
Does scaling AI in finance make it easier to scale enterprise AI?
It can. 86% of the most advanced finance organizations have embedded AI with planning systems in forecasting and planning processes, which lets them make strategic moves faster.
