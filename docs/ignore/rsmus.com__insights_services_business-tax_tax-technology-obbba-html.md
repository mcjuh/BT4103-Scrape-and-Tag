# How tax technology helps businesses adapt to the One Big Beautiful Bill Act
### Best practices for navigating tax policy with clarity, speed and confidence
November 18, 2025
##  Key takeaways 
Map your financial and tax data to enable smarter modeling and faster decisions.
Use tax technology to visualize how OBBBA provisions interact and impact strategy.
Align tax technology with business planning to unlock the full value of your tech investments.

For C-suite executives, understanding the tax implications of new legislation is critical to making effective business decisions. Timeliness and accuracy are key to minimizing risk and seizing new opportunities.
The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) contains tax changes that touch nearly every corner of a business—from capital investment and research and development to global structuring and state compliance. How quickly can you quantify the effects on your business? How do you adapt practically, such as adjusting investment plans, restructuring entities and updating tax reporting?
In that effort, view tax technology not as a stand-alone solution, but as a powerful, strategic enabler when embedded within a well-orchestrated ecosystem of quality data and streamlined processes.
Its true value emerges when organizations understand system functionality, harness its integration capabilities, and have clear visibility into tax-sensitive data and its location. This holistic configuration supports smarter decision making, enhances reporting accuracy and fosters cross-functional collaboration.
Tax technology enables businesses to be agile in modeling, forecasting and responding to tax changes. However, unlocking its full potential requires leaders to adopt best practices that align technology with business priorities, ensure data integrity and promote collaboration across teams.
### **1. Clarify your view of your data environment**
Tax modeling is only as effective as the data on which it relies. Yet many organizations still operate with fragmented systems, inconsistent data ownership and limited visibility into tax-sensitive information.
When tax teams receive processed data from upstream functions, such as accounting, it becomes difficult to trace inputs, validate assumptions or run scenario analyses.
To benefit strategically from tax technology, businesses must first map their current state—identifying where tax-relevant data resides, evaluating its accessibility and assessing its quality. This supports automation, reduces manual intervention and creates a scalable framework for proactive modeling.
Organizations without a clear and integrated view of their data landscape risk delays, errors and missed opportunities. Modeling becomes reactive rather than strategic, and compliance risks grow as legislation evolves. Seamless alignment of technology, quality data and effective processes is essential to pivot confidently amid regulatory change.
### **2. Model how tax provisions interact**
Tax provisions under the OBBBA are deeply interconnected, and understanding their interplay is essential for strategic tax planning. Tax technology, when aligned with quality data and effective processes, becomes a powerful modeling tool to quantify and visualize how provisions interact and affect a company’s broader tax profile.
Businesses that grasp the direct and ripple effects of new provisions can proactively manage unintended consequences, optimize their overall tax position and avoid costly surprises as rules take effect.
For example, immediate expensing of domestic R&D under new rules may reduce the allowable deduction for business interest. Electing 100% bonus depreciation could influence exposure to certain global taxes and affect various global tax calculations.
Treating provisions in isolation can lead to suboptimal tax positions and missed strategic opportunities. Digital modeling tools, however, support scenario planning by quantifying trade-offs and informing decisions.
When integrated with reliable data and cross-functional collaboration, these tools help leaders align tax strategy with broader business objectives and realize a valuable return on their tax technology investment.
### **3. Integrate tax technology with business planning**
OBBBA tax implications affect enterprise-level business decisions, such as investment timing, entity structuring and global operations. When tax technology supports business planning, strategic insights enable tax professionals to help shape decisions that affect cash flow and operational efficiency.
Tax technology streamlines processes, aligns data, and connects systems and people to support fast, accurate reporting across business functions. Aligning tax modeling with capital forecasts, R&D initiatives and global structuring plans helps ensure that tax is not siloed but actively informs strategic direction.
Without this integration, organizations risk marginalizing the tax function, leading to missed savings opportunities, increased compliance risks, inefficient capital deployment and suboptimal entity design.
### **4. Empower teams to use tax technology effectively**
Investing in tax technology is only part of the equation. Real transformation happens when companies also invest in the people and processes that support adoption.
Many organizations implement sophisticated tax software but fall short of realizing their full ROI due to limited training, lack of integration support or insufficient cross-functional alignment.
Tax and finance teams must be equipped with the skills, understanding and collaborative mindset to use these tools effectively. Building internal champions who can bridge tax, finance and IT functions helps ensure that platforms are not only implemented but fully leveraged.
Without investing in enablement, tax technology often goes underutilized. Teams revert to manual work-arounds—sacrificing the efficiency, accuracy and agility needed to respond to legislative changes and evolving business needs.
### **5. Plan for state and global tax complexity**
As tax reporting grows more complex, state and international requirements must be embedded into the tax technology ecosystem instead of treated as afterthoughts.
Jurisdictional inconsistencies, such as state-level decoupling from federal tax changes, and global reforms—like the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development’s Pillar Two as well as evolving intellectual property location rules— introduce layers of complexity that demand proactive planning.
Tax technology, when paired with quality data and effective processes, empowers teams to track these differences, model impacts across geographies and make informed decisions. The resulting foresight helps businesses avoid costly compliance errors, identify savings opportunities, forecast cash flows and effective tax rates more precisely, and support cross-border transactions and expansions confidently.
Without integrating these requirements into the ecosystem, companies risk noncompliance, overpayment or underpayment across jurisdictions. Those pitfalls undermine strategic goals and expose businesses to avoidable risk.
### **Tax policy and tax technology: From reactive to strategic**
The OBBBA represents a landmark opportunity for businesses to rethink how they manage tax complexity and risk. When they integrate tax technology with quality data and effective processes—and support that combination with empowered teams—the technology becomes a strategic asset instead of just a compliance tool.
Organizations that model how provisions interact, invest in adoption, and align technology with business priorities gain the ability to anticipate legislative impacts, optimize cash flow, and make faster, better-informed decisions.
For C-suite leaders, this means moving from reactive compliance to proactive strategy. By embedding tax technology into planning and decision making, and by supporting teams with the skills and structure to use it effectively, businesses can uncover new opportunities, reduce risk and support business objectives no matter how tax policy shifts.
