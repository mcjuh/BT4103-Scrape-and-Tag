# What Sirius means for partnership tax positions today
April 27, 2026 
In a rapidly evolving tax environment, few issues have created as much uncertainty in partnership tax law as the scope of the limited partner exception for self‑employment tax.
In an article for _Tax Notes Today_ , Grant Thornton’s Partners Grace Kim and Joe Pacello, along with Senior Manager Whit Cocanower, offer [**a clear, insightful look**](https://www.grantthornton.com/content/dam/grantthornton/website/assets/content-page-files/tax/pdfs/2026/practical-implications-limited-partner-exception-after-sirius-1.pdf) (PDF - 1007.23KB) at a debate that continues to shape partnership tax strategy. The authors examine the Fifth Circuit’s recent decision in _Sirius Solutions_ and why it matters now.
Their article, _Practical Implications of the Limited Partner Exception After Sirius_ centers on a pivotal question: Should eligibility for the limited partner exception depend on a partner’s legal status or their level of involvement in the business? The authors walk through the evolution of this issue, contrasting earlier Tax Court decisions that emphasized a functional, activity-based approach with the Fifth Circuit’s interpretation grounded in state law definitions of limited liability.
Beyond the legal analysis, Kim, Pacello and Cocanower focus on what this means in practice, how the _Sirius_ ruling may influence tax reporting positions, planning strategies and structural decisions across partnerships. With ongoing IRS scrutiny and conflicting judicial views, the landscape remains unsettled. For organizations navigating partnership structures and self-employment tax exposure, it provides both context and practical insight into what comes next.
