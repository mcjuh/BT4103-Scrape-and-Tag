# Chemical inventory management: Achieving the right product mix
### Better visibility, inventory management and S&OP processes can support growth
August 21, 2026
##  Key takeaways 
Poor inventory visibility increases the risk of excess stock and customer service issues.
Improved warehouse systems and data provide more accurate inventory management.
S&OP processes can help align demand forecasting, supply planning and inventory health.
Supply chain Manufacturing
Chemical companies have always had to be disciplined about striking the right inventory balance, but recent years of volatile demand and supply chain disruption have exacerbated the challenge, making agility increasingly important for businesses. Some still carry excess or obsolete inventory in some areas while also experiencing stockouts of critical items, and others are trying to determine whether they have the right products in the right locations to meet customer demand. Achieving effective chemical inventory management requires companies to strike the right balance between customer demand, inventory costs and operational flexibility.
Among chemical companies and chemical manufacturing companies in particular, product expiration dates, recertification requirements and complex storage considerations can quickly turn excess stock into a costly liability. Overstocked products may become obsolete because of expiry, shifting demand or other factors, creating operational and financial pressure for companies that lack clear visibility into inventory health.
U.S. chemical manufacturers’ inventories-to-shipments ratio stood at 1.49 in May 2026, according to the [Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/A25SIS), meaning inventories equaled roughly one and a half months of shipments on a seasonally adjusted basis.
Although some companies have right-sized their inventories, recent disruptions exposed persistent visibility gaps, particularly in order management and sales and operations planning (S&OP). Reviewing S&OP processes, supporting technologies and governance structures can help chemical companies address inventory challenges, improve customer service and support profitable growth.
## Inventory visibility challenges in the chemical industry
Antiquated IT systems and imprecise warehouse inventory systems are common contributors to supply chain visibility problems. Without real-time, accurate information about chemicals’ batch dates, quantities, locations and other shipment details, it’s hard to provide customers with reliable updates.
But that’s not the only issue; without good supply chain visibility, it’s also challenging to understand the ripple effects of shifting products around to fill various orders. This can mean decisions about which customers to prioritize might have unexpected ramifications.
These issues are compounded for businesses that track products across multiple warehouses or for teams that try to split orders up manually if their technology can’t accurately reflect such a change.
Demand-side visibility is also critical. Inaccurate forecasts, changing customer orders and shifting lead times can make it harder to determine whether inventory levels are too high, too low or misaligned with expected demand. For chemical companies, the result can be excess stock in some products and shortages in others.
Ultimately, synchronizing supply-side and demand-side signals is critical for balancing inventory requirements with business needs.

## Strategies to improve chemical inventory management
Updated systems that harness advanced technologies can provide real-time, accurate data about chemicals’ batch dates, quantities, locations and other shipment information. This can dramatically reduce guesswork for companies fulfilling orders amid various supply-demand disruptions.
Here are some specific ways companies can take steps toward greater visibility:
  * **Updating the warehouse management system:** Clear supply chain visibility hinges on accurate data. Updating the warehouse management system can give a more complete picture of inventory types, availability, batch dates, locations and amounts. Companies might implement track-and-trace technologies such as RFID and barcode scanning to monitor chemical containers throughout the supply chain and improve the accuracy of inventory data across systems. Integrating inventory management systems with hazardous materials handling protocols can also provide real-time updates on stock levels, expiration dates and safe storage conditions unique to chemical products. Centralized digital platforms for warehouse management can improve connections between suppliers, manufacturers and distributors, allowing for instantaneous visibility into shipment status, transit conditions and any disruptions affecting sensitive or regulated chemicals. 
  * **Establishing a clear S &OP process:** Investing in developing or improving an S&OP process is central to identifying the right inventory mix while maintaining the desired inventory health. Too often a company’s sales forecast does not align with its financial forecast, and neither forecast aligns with the company’s supply planning. S&OP is the cross-functional process that begins with developing a consensus-based demand plan that the entire organization aligns around. Teams can then develop a supply plan that will meet customer service expectations while minimizing inventory carrying costs.


There are four key components of the monthly S&OP process:
  1. **Historical reconciliation:** Understand forecast accuracy trends and insights while updating forward-looking statistical forecasts.
  2. **Demand review:** Align product lifecycle management (development, launch) and customer forecasts and market insights as an overlay in creating an unconstrained demand plan.
  3. **Supply review:** Manage against supply chain capacity constraints, manufacturing capabilities and supplier risks to develop a proposed consensus plan and mitigation strategy.
  4. **Consensus plan:** Align the organization around a consensus plan with trade-off consideration and financial plan reconciliation.


  * **Focusing on performance metrics that matter:** Chemical companies aiming to enhance their inventory visibility should zero in on key metrics such as service level performance, forecast accuracy and inventory health. Driving these metrics into the daily performance management system can improve the overall supply planning function while driving SKU-specific inventory parameter setting. This can help achieve more optimal stock levels, minimize waste due to obsolescence, and support timely fulfillment of customer orders. Tracking days of supply and order cycle times can also provide deeper insights into areas for improvement. 
  * **Developing an inventory planning strategy:** An effective inventory planning strategy for chemical companies should incorporate demand forecasting, safety stock calculations and supplier lead time management. It is important to evaluate storage requirements based on chemical properties and regulatory constraints, while also establishing clear reorder points to maintain continuity of supply. Chemical companies should also regularly audit and reconcile physical inventory against digital records to catch discrepancies caused by spills, contamination or regulatory changes, which are particularly critical in chemical supply chains.


## The takeaway
These strategies can equip businesses to respond swiftly to market changes, optimize inventory levels, enhance supply chain visibility and reduce excess and obsolete stock. Ultimately, this can enable chemical companies to improve customer service and increase efficiency, paving the way for improved operations.

## Frequently asked questions 

What is chemical inventory management?  
    
Chemical inventory management involves tracking, planning and optimizing inventory while accounting for shelf life, hazardous materials requirements, storage conditions and customer demand. 

Why is inventory visibility important for chemical companies?  
    
Inventory visibility can help organizations monitor inventory locations, expiration dates, stock levels and supply chain disruptions, reducing waste and improving customer service. 

What is S&OP in chemical manufacturing?  
    
Sales and operations planning is a cross-functional process that aligns demand forecasts, supply plans and financial goals to improve service levels and inventory performance. 

How can chemical manufacturers reduce excess inventory?  
    
Modern warehouse management systems can help manufacturers improve forecasting, increase supply chain visibility and establish inventory health metrics.
