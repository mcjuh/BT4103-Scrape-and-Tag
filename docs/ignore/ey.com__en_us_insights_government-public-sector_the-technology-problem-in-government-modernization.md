#  The technology problem in government modernization isn’t what you think 
[ Lori Feller ](https://www.ey.com/en_us/people/lori-feller)
Managing Director, Technology Consulting, Ernst & Young LLP
4 minute read  03 Aug 2026

#### Why the next era of transformation starts with business outcomes, not tools.
Read more  Read less 
#####  ****In brief****
  * The core challenge isn’t access to technology — it’s aligning it with how government actually operates.
  * Modernization stalls when tools outpace governance, workforce capacity and operating models.
  * Progress depends on shifting from technology adoption to solving mission-critical business problems.


Read more  Read less 
How EY can help 
  * [ EY State and Local Center for Government Modernization ](https://www.ey.com/en_us/government-public-sector/state-and-local-government-modernization)
The EY State and Local Center for Government Modernization is a place for public leaders to explore what’s working, what’s changing and what comes next.

State and local governments are not struggling because they lack access to modern technology. Cloud platforms, AI tools, digital portals and advanced analytics are already in place across the public sector.
The deeper challenge is this: Technology is moving faster than the way government is designed to operate.
Budgets are tightening. Expectations are rising. Risks — cyber, climate, workforce — are no longer episodic. And leaders are being asked to deliver tangible results with less room for error than ever before.
In this environment, modernization is no longer about adopting the next tool.
It’s about solving real business problems — and using technology deliberately to do it.
## The real modernization gap: When technology outpaces operating reality
Across the US SLED landscape, we see a consistent pattern:
  * Platforms modernize, but governance stays siloed
  * AI pilots scale, but trust frameworks lag
  * Automation expands, yet workforce capacity remains strained
  * Resilience investments grow, while operating models stay reactive


The risk isn’t lack of ambition. It’s misalignment.
When technology decisions are made in isolation — without rethinking governance, accountability, funding models and workforce design — modernization becomes fragmented. Benefits stay local. Risks multiply. And transformation stalls.
The next phase of government modernization demands a different approach.
## A business-forward view of transformation
Leading organizations are flipping the script.
Instead of asking “What technology should we deploy?” They start with “What problem are we solving — and what outcome matters most?”
Across state and local government, the most forward-looking leaders are organizing transformation around a small set of mission-critical business challenges:
Read more  Read less 
  * ##  Protecting service continuity with a constrained workforce
Workforce shortages aren’t temporary. Automation and AI are being used not to replace people but to preserve capacity, reduce administrative burdens and allow staff to focus on complex, high-value work.
Read more  Read less 
  * ##  Making faster, better decisions under pressure
Data and AI are increasingly essential to budgeting, risk management, care delivery and emergency response. But success depends on strong data foundations, governance and responsible AI practices that build trust at scale.
Read more  Read less 
  * ##  Breaking down silos that slow delivery
Legacy systems didn’t just create technical debt — they reinforced organizational fragmentation. Modern platforms expose these seams. Real progress comes when governance, funding and ownership models evolve alongside the technology.
Read more  Read less 
  * ##  Treating resilience as an operating requirement, not a response plan
Climate events and cyber threats are now constant. Resilience is being embedded into systems, processes and decision-making — designed in from the start, not activated after disruption.
Read more  Read less 


These are business problems first.
Read more  Read less 
Technology is the accelerator — not the answer.

##  What this moment demands from public sector leaders
The governments moving fastest aren’t deploying more tools. They’re making clearer choices.
Four leadership imperatives consistently separate progress from stall:
Read more  Read less 
  * ##  Stop treating modernization as an IT program
Technology investments must be paired with changes to governance, accountability and operating models to unlock enterprise value.
Read more  Read less 
  * ##  Govern AI before you scale it
Responsible AI isn’t a compliance exercise — it’s a prerequisite for sustainable adoption, public trust and long-term impact.
Read more  Read less 
  * ##  Automate to protect scarce capacity
Automation delivers the most value where workforce constraints threaten service continuity and mission outcomes.
Read more  Read less 
  * ##  Fund resilience like infrastructure
Preparedness must be built into core systems, not bolted on after a crisis.
Read more  Read less 


## What leading governments are doing differently
The governments making the most progress aren’t defined by the technologies they deploy — they’re defined by how they choose to modernize.
Across jurisdictions, a common set of shifts is emerging.
Read more  Read less 
  * ##  From technology programs to business transformation
Modernization efforts that succeed are no longer framed as IT initiatives. Leaders are aligning technology investments with governance, funding and accountability structures so that change scales across the enterprise — not just within individual programs.
Read more  Read less 
  * ##  From experimentation to trust led AI
AI adoption is accelerating, but the most effective organizations are putting trust mechanisms in place before scaling. Strong data foundations, clear governance and ethical guardrails are becoming prerequisites — not afterthoughts — for meaningful impact.
Read more  Read less 
  * ##  From automating tasks to protecting capacity
With persistent workforce shortages, automation is increasingly used to preserve service continuity rather than simply reduce costs. The focus is on relieving administrative burden so limited staff can concentrate on complex, mission critical work.
Read more  Read less 
  * ##  From reactive preparedness to built in resilience
Resilience is no longer treated as a response capability. It is being embedded into operating models, systems and decision making processes — designed to function under constant risk, not occasional disruption.
Read more  Read less 


## The defining challenge ahead
Technology will continue to evolve rapidly. That is not the constraint.
The defining challenge for state and local governments is whether institutions evolve just as quickly — in how decisions are made, how work is structured, and how risk and accountability are managed in a permanently digital environment.
The next era of modernization will belong to organizations that align strategy, operations and technology around clear business outcomes and public value rather than viewing transformation as a sequence of tools to deploy.
## The bottom line
The future of government modernization will not be decided by who adopts the most advanced technology.
It will be defined by who best aligns strategy, operations and technology around real business outcomes — and who has the discipline to modernize the institution at the same pace as the tools.
Read more  Read less 
#### Summary 
Government modernization is not constrained by access to technology, but by misalignment between tools and how government operates. As expectations rise and resources tighten, fragmented approaches — where technology outpaces governance, workforce capacity and operating models — limit impact. Leading organizations are shifting focus: starting with mission-critical business problems; embedding resilience and trust; and aligning strategy, operations and technology to deliver measurable public outcomes at scale.
