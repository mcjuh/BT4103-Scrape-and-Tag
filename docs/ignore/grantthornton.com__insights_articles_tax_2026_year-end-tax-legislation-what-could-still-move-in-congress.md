# Year-end tax legislation: What could still move in Congress
September 01, 2026 
Listen to the podcast
With Washington heading into the final months of 2026 and midterm elections looming, business leaders may be asking if Congress done with tax legislation for the year ꟷ or whether there are still meaningful developments ahead.
In our new episode of Grant Thornton's _D.C. Dispatch_ podcast, host David Sites is joined by fellow Grant Thornton Washington National Tax Office tax legislative affairs specialists Storme Sixeas and Colin Wilhelm for a practical discussion about what, if anything, businesses should realistically expect from Congress before year-end.
The episode begins with a reality check. After last year's sweeping One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OB3), lawmakers have largely shifted their focus from passing major tax legislation to monitoring the implementation of what was enacted. But that doesn't mean nothing is happening.
Sites, Sixeas and Wilhelm discuss several issues that could still attract attention in the coming months, including bipartisan tax administration legislation, digital asset tax proposals, tax extenders, and targeted fixes to existing law. The discussion explores why legislation aimed at modernizing IRS operations and improving taxpayer services may have a better chance of advancing than more ambitious tax measures.
The episode also looks at how election-year politics are shaping the legislative outlook, while also highlighting developments outside Congress, including the Treasury Department's ongoing work to implement OB3 through regulations and guidance.
For tax executives, finance leaders or anyone trying to understand Washington's year-end agenda, the discussion offers valuable context on where policymakers may still find common ground and where expectations should remain tempered. To hear the latest on what is in store in federal taxation for the rest of 2026, tune in to the full _D.C. Dispatch_ podcast episode.
