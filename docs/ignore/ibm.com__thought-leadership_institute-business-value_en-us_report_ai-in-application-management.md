# Application management as the enterprise performance engine
Decoupling effort from value is the key to scaling AI
[Download the insights (will open in a new tab)](https://www.ibm.com/downloads/documents/us-en/177ce39dff447872)[Subscribe for more insights from IBM IBV (will open in a new window)](https://www.ibm.com/thought-leadership/institute-business-value/en-us/newsletters)
AI is already changing application management services (AMS). Organizations are using AI to predict incidents, automate routine work, improve resilience, and reduce the effort required to keep critical applications running. Yet despite widespread adoption, most organizations have not translated those gains into lasting business value.
The problem is not technology. AI is delivering results. In fact, 91% of executives report that AI has already reduced AMS effort. Yet only 3% operate AI-enabled AMS at scale today, even though 63% expect it to become their primary AMS model by 2028.
New research from the IBM Institute for Business Value, based on a survey of 1,000 global technology, operations, and finance leaders, reveals why: the **value mismatch**. Operating models, incentives, governance structures, and contracts built for a labor-based world are preventing organizations from capturing the full value of AI-enabled AMS.
Organizations making the most progress are taking a different approach. They redesign AMS around outcomes rather than activity, shift work from response to prevention, and reinvest automation gains into modernization and continuous improvement.
> Operating models, incentives, governance structures, and contracts built for a labor-based world are preventing organizations from capturing the full value of AI-enabled AMS.
### The value mismatch is holding AMS back
AI changes how value is created. As AI eliminates repetitive work and prevents incidents, value increasingly comes from outcomes rather than activity.
Yet AMS remains anchored to older operating models. 71% of respondents say contract structures are now a greater barrier to AI-enabled AMS than technology itself. While 83% say AMS contracts still reward labor-based delivery, only 11% report incentives fully aligned to AI-enabled AMS outcomes.
Organizations that scale AI-enabled AMS successfully redesign the system around outcomes. Seven in ten executives say traditional ticket- and SLA-based metrics no longer reflect the value AMS creates. Leading organizations focus on reliability, resilience, incident prevention, and business performance. Nearly three-quarters report reshaping workflows around problem solving, shifting an average of 25% of AMS capacity from reactive operations toward proactive engineering.
### From support function to performance engine
Overcoming the value mismatch delivers more than efficiency. 71% of respondents report faster recovery from high-severity incidents than two years ago, while mean time to detect major incidents has fallen by 26% and mean time to recover by 19%.
As reliability improves, organizations gain capacity that can be redirected toward modernization. Two-thirds say AI has enabled faster or more cost-effective modernization initiatives. Among organizations operating AI-enabled AMS, 30% report significant modernization acceleration, compared with only 9% of others.
This is where value begins to compound. Reliability reduces disruption. Less disruption frees capacity. Capacity accelerates modernization and creates new opportunities for automation and performance improvement. Over time, AMS evolves from a support function into an enterprise performance engine. 
**Action guide**
Three plays to scale AI-enabled AMS
**1. Overcome the value mismatch** Review contracts, incentives, governance, and performance metrics for assumptions built around manual effort. Replace labor-based measures with outcomes tied to reliability, recovery, incident reduction, resilience, and automation performance. Reward providers for eliminating repeat work, not preserving it.
**2. Redesign work before scaling AI** Start where AI can deliver the greatest operational impact: recurring incidents, repetitive service requests, and manual bottlenecks. Shift resources from reactive support toward proactive engineering and prevention, allowing AI to address root causes rather than symptoms.
**3. Capture the efficiency dividend** Treat released capacity as a strategic asset. Reinvest it into modernization, observability, prevention, and continuous improvement. Track how AI-generated capacity is used and establish clear reinvestment targets so efficiency gains translate into sustained business value.
AI value in AMS is no longer constrained by technology. It is constrained by the value mismatch.
Organizations that redesign incentives, workflows, and governance to align with how AI creates value can convert efficiency gains into sustained business outcomes and transform AMS into an engine for reliability, modernization, and enterprise performance.
