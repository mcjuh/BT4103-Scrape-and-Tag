# Redesign for enterprise AI
Focus on critical value streams to drive growth.
[Download the insights (will open in a new tab)](https://www.ibm.com/downloads/documents/us-en/16ddab5aa1547458)[Subscribe for more insights from IBM IBV (will open in a new window)](https://www.ibm.com/thought-leadership/institute-business-value/en-us/newsletters)
Despite increased spending, most artificial intelligence initiatives are still failing. Only 37% had delivered the business value expected by senior leaders by the end of 2025, according to a new survey conducted by the IBM Institute for Business Value (IBV) in partnership with Oxford Economics. The typical enterprise AI portfolio generated USD115 million in net value in 2025—an ROI of 51%.
Looking to 2028, executives have set their sights much higher. They’re aiming to deliver USD462 million in net value and boost ROI to 105%. However, the 1,000 technology CxOs, AI transformation officers, and line of business sponsors we surveyed expect to deliver on these financial targets while taking just 29% of AI initiatives into production at scale. That’s where the math goes sideways.
The fact is, agentic AI can’t deliver better outcomes unless agents are deployed in production environments. And the tangle of complexity and technical debt in business and IT environments is creating friction that keeps agentic AI from operating effectively.
> At the end of 2025, only 37% of AI initiatives had delivered the expected business outcomes.
Not all friction is technical. Some of it is human. Even when AI systems are available, employees may not understand how they create value, where they fit into existing workflows, or why specific tools were adopted. Adoption friction can prevent organizations from capturing returns even when the technology itself performs as intended.
AI agents also break traditional span-of-control models. Leaders will need to build hybrid team structures that clearly outline how people and agents should work together to spur effective adoption at scale. 
“If we apply AI inside each silo only to improve individual work efficiency, the benefit disappears at the enterprise level,” says Yasuo Kawai, Vice President, Head of Digital Unit, Honda Japan. “To operate AI enterprise‑wide, enterprise‑wide reform is necessary.” 
### Breaking through bottlenecks
The biggest barrier to AI impact isn’t model quality or data volume—it’s friction across systems that weren’t designed to talk to each other. In 2025, 21% of AI ROI was lost to friction in the IT and business environment, according to the executives we surveyed.
That’s because agents don’t sit on top of IT infrastructure. They operate within it. When their environment is fragmented, inconsistent, or weighed down by decades of technical debt, even the smartest AI hits a wall.
Executives say the top obstacles to high-scale, high-ROI AI are processes, data, and decision rights that haven’t been optimized for AI. Large-scale AI-driven process redesign is needed to build resilience. In addition to technical upgrades, organizations must create new risk, governance, and security protocols, and redesign workflows and process flows from start to finish.
> “Treating IT spend as investment rather than a cost can create uncomfortable conversations. But it’s the only way to demand and achieve real returns.” 
> **Gavin Wood** CIO, APA Group
More than three in four executives agree: applying AI to isolated parts of a workflow delivers limited value unless the entire process is redesigned. Most enterprises are starting with an IT landscape built from thousands of rational-but-isolated decisions—each one sensible in the moment but not orchestrated as a whole.
The result is an IT environment that spans multiple clouds, platforms, and jurisdictions, with thousands of integration points that no single individual fully understands. Only 10% of executives say their AI development and operations infrastructure is fully standardized across environments on a unified platform.
The goal is to redesign processes to remove the friction that limits the value agentic AI can unlock. Processes that work the same way every time, that thrive on structured data and repeatable logic, have already undergone process optimization. What remains are the processes and value streams that have resisted standardization. The ones that don’t follow a script. These are the places where humans still make the call—because the problem was never simple enough to solve with traditional automation.
These complex, variable, context-dependent value streams are where agentic AI finds its sweet spot. They’re the decisions that shape customer outcomes, drive competitive advantage, and unlock new revenue. Reducing friction in these value streams is where enterprises should focus their AI investments. 
### The real size of the prize
AI spend is rising, but not in a vacuum. Between 2025 and 2028, executives in our survey expect IT spend to balloon from USD1.4 billion to an estimated USD2.2 billion, on average. At the same time, 58% of executives are under pressure to cut non-AI tech costs.
That creates a direct conflict: the same legacy environments that slow AI progress are now competing for funding with the initiatives meant to replace them. Without clear prioritization, organizations risk spreading investment too thin—funding incremental improvements across the enterprise without delivering meaningful value anywhere.
Defining a clear success threshold for all AI investments—and stopping projects that don't reach it—is necessary to make AI transformation financially sustainable. In fact, [recent IBM IBV research](http://ibm.biz/enterprise-2030) found that 70% of executives say they’re looking to use the value gained from AI to fund investment and growth across the organization. Early gains matter. But they are only the starting point.
This is where reinvestment becomes critical. Operational efficiency gains in overhead functions should not simply reduce costs; they should create capacity to tackle the harder problem—transforming the work that drives revenue, margin, and competitive performance. By focusing efforts on revenue-generating value streams, organizations have the potential to deliver 7x more value than if they cut administrative costs alone. 
**Unlock AI's potential value** Redesigning customer-facing value streams has a potential revenue impact that is 7x greater than the cost impact of reducing SG&A spend
###  Renovate the IT estate for AI
Most of the work required to drive growth with AI isn’t related to building new systems. It is making existing ones operable for AI. That begins with clearing away layers of accumulated complexity and removing organizational friction.
The opportunity is significant: revenue-generating value streams offer the greatest potential return. But they also carry the greatest risk. Leaders need to balance both—ensuring that what moves into production is ready, resilient, and governed.
Successfully redesigning a value stream for agentic AI requires changes in four closely related practices: architecture, data, decision rights, and business and IT operations. How an organization renovates each area—and how it redesigns the teams, roles, and controls around them—determines not just the value AI can deliver, but how safely and sustainably it can scale.**Architecture** Most organizations today operate hybrid environments in practice, but not by design. Workloads reside in multiple clouds and on-premises systems for valid reasons—cost, performance, regulation. But without a consistent execution layer across these environments, every boundary becomes a barrier.**Data** High-value process data is distributed, with 74% living outside public cloud environments. Plus, it’s maintained in formats optimized for storage rather than action. Agentic systems require something different: continuous, contextual, real-time access to the state of a process. Without that, they reconstruct context at every step—introducing latency, cost, and error.**Decision rights** Agentic AI requires organizations to think carefully about decision-making. When an AI system acts autonomously, who owns the outcome of an agent’s decision? What thresholds define acceptable autonomy? How is performance measured and corrected?**Business and IT operations** Like any complex transformation, renovating an IT estate carries significant overhead. Every improvement comes with setup costs: aligning stakeholders, mapping dependencies, establishing baselines. A lot of work has to happen before there’s any chance of success. With agentic AI, continuity of effort and continuous improvement are key. You want today’s work to build on yesterday’s work. You want teams that maintain context, deepen expertise, and chip away at the problem over time rather than ramp up, deliver a milestone, and move on.
