# Resilience in action
How can government leaders mobilize teamwork and technology to improve crisis response and resilience?
[Download the insights (will open in a new tab)](https://www.ibm.com/downloads/documents/us-en/107a02f5d0c8f34c)[Subscribe for more insights from IBM IBV (will open in a new window)](https://www.ibm.com/thought-leadership/institute-business-value/en-us/newsletters)
As a scan of news headlines will confirm, this is an age when pandemics, natural disasters, climate change, cyberattacks, geopolitical tensions, and other events disrupt nations, communities, and people all over the world. The scope, scale, and cadence of these shock events are testing the foresight, response capacity, and resilience of governmental organizations like never before.
In 2022, the IBM Institute for Business Value (IBM IBV), the IBM Center for The Business of Government (the IBM Center), and the National Academy of Public Administration (the Academy) launched an initiative to help governments identify and develop core capabilities critical to building resilience. Since then, this “Future Shocks” initiative has included expert roundtable discussions, government executive survey research, and a series of reports on emergency preparedness, cybersecurity, supply chains, organizational resilience, and other domains.
> Future crises will not respect borders or bureaucratic boundaries and will test the agility of government partnerships and technology. 
###  What real-world crisis response and resilience looks like
These reports provide government leaders with a strategic understanding of processes related to planning, preparation, and crisis response. However, leaders are also looking for tactical perspectives and a ground-level view of what resilience looks like in the middle of a crisis, when situational awareness is difficult to obtain, and uncertainty is ubiquitous.
To provide real-world examples of how governments collaborated with academic and private-sector experts to respond to crises, the newest report in the Future Shocks initiative includes a series of case studies from around the world. These studies focus on how a community hospital system coordinated response to the COVID-19 pandemic, how countries worked across borders to help pandemic patients get critical care regardless of nationality, how a small country managed an enormous war refugee crisis, and how a US state capital proactively addressed power grid vulnerabilities related to extreme weather events. 
> Infrastructure may survive a disaster, but how will constituents fare? Resilience plans that don’t accommodate human needs will fail.
###  Where teamwork, technology, and timing make a difference
Each of these case studies demonstrate how innovative strategies and strong partnerships can enhance preparedness. These experiences also offer critical insights for government leaders and other stakeholders in preparation for an increasingly volatile future. They show how public and private entities can join forces to address complex challenges, from emergency health coordination to leveraging digital tools for crisis management. And they go beyond technology to explore human factors, including social, organizational, and political dynamics, that should be included in any holistic approach for resilience.
Key learnings from the challenges explored in the case studies point to a fundamental observation: that collaborative, forward-looking planning is essential to safeguard constituents and provide operational continuity. Download the report to help your government clients learn how collaboration, innovation, and people-first strategies are essential when it comes to preparing for and responding to future shocks.
