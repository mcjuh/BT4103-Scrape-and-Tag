# The ERP leapfrog
How Australian enterprises can turn decades of tech debt into AI leadership
[Download the insights (will open in a new tab)](https://www.ibm.com/downloads/documents/us-en/16951f13ae617629)[Subscribe for more insights from IBM IBV (will open in a new window)](https://www.ibm.com/thought-leadership/institute-business-value/en-us/newsletters)
### The state of ERP in Australia and its impact on AI
As artificial intelligence advances through Australian enterprises, leaders across industries are discovering that the biggest barrier to enterprise‑wide AI isn’t the model, the imagination, or the appetite for experimentation—it’s the decades‑old ERP foundations still sitting at the center of many operations.
A new study from the IBM Institute for Business Value, _The ERP Leapfrog: How Australian enterprises can turn decades of tech debt into AI leadership_ , explains why Australia may now be uniquely positioned to build AI‑ready enterprises faster than global peers. 
### Australia’s ERP gap: A challenge—and an opening
The research highlights a striking maturity gap across 17 industries. Only 11% of Australian organizations run on modern cloud ERP platforms. In the US and UK, it’s nearly 23%.
The US and the UK are not outliers—many global peers completed their ERP modernization years ago, long before agentic AI redefined what modern ERP must support. Their systems were built for efficiency, standardization, and incremental transformation. They were not designed for what agentic AI requires: machine‑readable workflows, real‑time autonomous decisioning, or cross‑functional automation.
Now they must retrofit. 
> Australia, by contrast, has not yet locked itself into pre‑AI design choices. That delay comes with obvious pain points, but it also creates a rare opportunity: the country can modernize ERP once, directly into an AI‑native posture. 
### Why early AI wins aren’t expanding across the enterprise
Across Australia, AI pilots are multiplying—finance automations, marketing assistants, forecasting models, HR copilots, customer‑service intelligence. In most cases, these pilots perform well. The issue emerges when an organization tries to expand them across the entire business.
The trail leads back to the same place: legacy ERP environments that rely on slow, inconsistent data, customized workflows, and manual stitching between systems. AI agents can observe and assist inside a department, but once they’re asked to cross into others, the foundation can’t give them the visibility or authority they need.
Enterprises cannot realize AI’s full value if the workflows that determine things like margin, cost‑to‑serve, and operational speed are out of reach.
96% of Australian leaders say ERP data is essential for agentic AI. Yet half of AI initiatives remain stuck in pilot mode. The insights are clear: capability limitations at the core—not lack of vision at the edges—are holding back scale. 
### The growing cost of delaying ERP modernization
The study quantifies the economic drag created by aging ERP systems. When AI runs in fragmented environments, returns plateau around 8% ROI. Once ERP is modernized and designed around AI‑ready workflows, projected ROI rises to roughly 40%. 
**Modernizing ERP can turn 8% AI ROI into 40%**
This difference compounds quarter after quarter. Slower processes, manual reconciliation, duplicated workflows, working‑capital lock‑up, and inconsistent data definitions all create friction that AI can’t overcome. This compounding penalty is, in essence, a hesitation tax—the real cost enterprises incur the longer they remain on legacy foundations. 
### What the 2028 deadline means
ERP vendors are phasing out support for legacy platforms in 2027. By 2028, AI‑embedded ERP is expected to be the norm among global competitors. Australian leaders see the timeline clearly: 97% say they plan to reach full ERP modernization maturity by 2028.
But there’s a difference between meeting a deadline and seizing a strategic window.
> Enterprises that approach the 2028 deadline as a technical migration exercise risk repeating what’s already happening overseas: modernizing once for infrastructure, then modernizing again for intelligence. 
Australia can avoid the costly second program by designing now for the world AI is creating—not the world ERP was historically built for. 
### What “AI‑native ERP” actually means
Today, AI‑native ERP is defined not by cloud hosting or system version, but by machine‑readability. That means designing processes, data flows, and governance so both humans and intelligent agents can operate consistently and confidently.
Four design considerations sit at the center of this shift:
  * **Decision rights:** Which decisions can an AI agent make independently, and where are human reviews required?
  * **Data velocity:** Can the ERP deliver reliable, real‑time data streams, or does batch processing introduce too much lag?
  * **Process standardization:** Are workflows simplified enough for agents to navigate without confusion or exception overload?
  * **Governance integration:** Do agents have clear rules for escalation, information sharing, and cross‑functional cooperation?


These design choices, made early, determine whether an enterprise builds a platform that will last a decade—or require expensive retrofits within two years. 
### A more practical modernization model
Modern ERP transitions are fast, 90-day sprints designed to pay for themselves. These cycles target the highest‑friction operational areas—such as financial close delays, excessive cost‑to‑serve, or working‑capital inefficiency—and deliver measurable improvements quickly.
Those wins free up capital, build confidence, and create a self‑funding modernization engine. Instead of one massive transformation, enterprises move in precise, repeatable increments. 
### A window that won’t remain open
Legacy ERP systems are expiring. AI expectations are rising. Global competitors are modernizing their cores. The convergence of these forces creates a narrow, high‑leverage window for Australian enterprises. Those that act now can modernize once—with AI‑native design at the center. Those that delay may find themselves modernizing twice.
_The ERP Leapfrog_ offers a deeper analysis of this moment: the hesitation tax, the ROI acceleration after modernization, the urgency of the 2028 window, and the design principles that distinguish enterprises that scale AI from those that stay stuck in pilot cycles.
