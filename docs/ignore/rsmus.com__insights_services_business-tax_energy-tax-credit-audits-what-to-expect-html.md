# Energy tax credit audits: What taxpayers should expect
### IRS is scrutinizing transferable credits, clean fuel credits and IRA incentives
July 24, 2026
# 
Federal tax Excise tax consulting
Business tax Energy Tax policy Credits & incentives
## Executive summary: Emerging tax controversy issues in energy credit space
As taxpayers continue to invest heavily in clean energy projects and monetization strategies under the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA), federal tax controversy risk is rapidly becoming a central consideration for energy credit stakeholders.
While much of the focus since enactment of the IRA has centered on qualification requirements, registration procedures and monetization opportunities, the next phase of the market is expected to involve increased IRS examination activity, substantiation disputes and enforcement initiatives targeting energy credit claims.
The IRS has continued to expand its compliance infrastructure related to clean energy incentives, particularly surrounding transferable credits, prevailing wage and apprenticeship requirements, lifecycle emissions determinations and registration procedures. As billions of dollars in credits continue to flow through the market, taxpayers should anticipate heightened scrutiny regarding both technical qualification and procedural compliance.
Taxpayers participating in clean fuel, advanced manufacturing, carbon capture, hydrogen and renewable energy credit programs may benefit from evaluating controversy readiness now. Early attention to documentation, governance and substantiation practices can help reduce audit risk and support credit claims during examination.
## IRS scrutiny of energy tax credits is increasing
The IRS has publicly emphasized that administration and compliance oversight remain key components of IRA implementation. Recent guidance, pre-filing registration requirements and IRS compliance campaign activity indicate an increased focus on substantiation and eligibility requirements for energy-related tax incentives.
Several factors are contributing to increased controversy risk, including significant dollar amounts associated with transferable and direct pay credits, rapid market growth and participation by new entrants, reliance on evolving administrative guidance, complex technical qualification standards, increased use of third-party studies, certifications and lifecycle modeling, and heightened political and regulatory scrutiny surrounding IRA incentives.
Unlike traditional tax credits, many IRA-related incentives involve operational, engineering, environmental and labor compliance considerations that extend beyond standard tax reporting. As a result, examinations are likely to be extremely fact-intensive and may require taxpayers to maintain documentation and support for factual and technical determinations spanning tax, engineering, environmental and labor compliance matters.
## Transferable energy tax credits face growing audit risk
The transferability rules under section 6418 have fundamentally changed the energy credit marketplace by allowing taxpayers to monetize certain credits through credit transfers.
Section 6418 provides that an eligible taxpayer may elect to transfer all or a specified portion of eligible credits to an unrelated transferee. The rules establish detailed procedural and compliance requirements, including pre-filing registration, election reporting requirements and substantiation obligations.
The [One Big Beautiful Bill Act also added restrictions](https://rsmus.com/insights/services/business-tax/obbba-tax-clean-energy.html) prohibiting transfers of certain energy credits to specified foreign entities for tax years beginning after July 4, 2025.
While transferability has increased liquidity and investment activity, it has also introduced additional IRS controversy risks.
Potential IRS focus areas may include:
  * Validity of transferred credits
  * Adequacy of due diligence performed by credit purchasers
  * Registration compliance
  * Timing of credit generation and transfer
  * Excessive credit claims
  * Basis calculations
  * Prevailing wage and apprenticeship substantiation
  * Documentation supporting domestic content or energy community qualification


Although transferred credits generally remain subject to recapture and adjustment rules, market participants continue navigating unresolved questions regarding audit risk allocation between transferors and transferees.
As transfer markets mature, well-defined indemnification provisions and diligence procedures may provide greater clarity around audit exposure and support more efficient transaction execution.
## IRS examinations may focus on section 45Z clean fuel credits
[Section 45Z clean fuel production credits](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2026/key-takeaways-from-new-section.html) present particularly significant controversy potential due to the technical and evolving nature of the regime. The IRS is likely to closely scrutinize several aspects of these claims, including issues related to the emissions rate, such as lifecycle greenhouse gas emissions calculations, emissions rate determinations and provisional emissions rate applications.
The IRS is also likely to scrutinize feedstock qualification, book-and-claim frameworks, related-party sales issues, producer registration requirements and substantiation supporting fuel production volumes. In many cases, taxpayers must rely on evolving administrative guidance, technical modeling and external data sources to support credit calculations, creating uncertainty regarding the level of documentation the IRS may ultimately require upon examination.
Additionally, taxpayers operating across multiple regulatory regimes, including Renewable Fuel Standard (RFS) requirements, state low-carbon fuel programs and federal tax credit frameworks, may face increased complexity in reconciling operational data and compliance positions.
## How Loper Bright could affect energy tax credit disputes
Recent developments in administrative law may also impact future energy credit controversy. Following the Supreme Court’s decision in Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo, taxpayers may increasingly challenge Treasury regulations and regulatory guidance governing IRA incentives.
Certain energy credit programs rely heavily on notices, FAQs, registration procedures and other forms of administrative guidance. As taxpayers and advisors continue evaluating the legal authority supporting various guidance positions, disputes regarding administrative procedure and regulatory interpretation may become more common.
This dynamic may be particularly relevant in areas involving lifecycle emissions methodologies, registration requirements, domestic content calculations, prevailing wage compliance standards, credit transfer procedures and recapture mechanics.
As a result, future controversies may involve not only technical qualification disputes but also broader challenges regarding the scope of the Treasury and IRS authority in drafting regulations.
## Energy tax credit audit readiness
Examination readiness may help taxpayers respond more efficiently to IRS inquiries and support credit positions if a claim is reviewed. Readiness often involves attention to several documentation and process-related areas, including:
  * Maintaining contemporaneous substantiation supporting all eligibility requirements
  * Implementing formal governance and compliance procedures
  * Coordinating tax, engineering, environmental and operational documentation
  * Reviewing transfer agreements and indemnification provisions
  * Evaluating audit readiness for technical studies and third-party reports
  * Monitoring evolving IRS guidance and enforcement activity
  * Conducting periodic internal compliance reviews


Given the complexity and financial significance of IRA incentives, integrating controversy preparedness into energy credit planning may help organizations address documentation and substantiation issues before an examination begins.
## Future IRS enforcement trends for energy tax credits
The energy credit market is entering a new phase in which controversy risk, audit preparedness and administrative scrutiny are becoming increasingly important. As IRS enforcement strategies related to IRA incentives continue to evolve, examination activity is likely to focus increasingly on substantiation, procedural compliance and technical qualification requirements.
Evaluating documentation and governance processes before an examination occurs may help organizations identify gaps, support credit positions and reduce disruption if IRS scrutiny increases. Early preparation may also help mitigate financial exposure and operational disruption as enforcement activity evolves.
Given the technical and operational complexity of many energy credit programs, organizations often benefit from a multidisciplinary approach to examination readiness. Tax advisors with experience in energy incentives, credit monetization transactions and controversy matters can help assess documentation, review governance and compliance processes, identify potential areas of examination risk and evaluate substantiation supporting credit claims.
