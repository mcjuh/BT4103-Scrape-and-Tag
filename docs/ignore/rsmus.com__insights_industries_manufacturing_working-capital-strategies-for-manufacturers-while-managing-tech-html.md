# Working capital strategies for manufacturers while managing technical debt
### Zero in on cash flow prioritization, strategic use of debt and cost management
December 10, 2024
##  Key takeaways 
Higher capital costs have underscored the importance of enterprise value.
One area where strategic debt investment might have the biggest payoff is automation.
Inventory management is central to managing costs and can help free up cash.
Manufacturing
An effective capital allocation strategy is imperative for manufacturers, especially as high interest rates and increasing capital costs have underscored the importance of enterprise value.
Companies should develop or revisit their working capital strategies to ensure they are positioned to manage investments and technical debt, and ultimately drive growth.
Cash flow prioritization, strategic use of debt and cost management are three key components of such strategies. Taken together, they create a comprehensive framework for operational efficiency and financial stability.
## Cash flow prioritization
In the current economic environment, manufacturers should seek out ways to enhance free cash flow. Cash generation is vital to their operations, investments and overall growth and can reduce unnecessary expenditures. It can also allow manufacturers to pay down more debt. Some approaches include:
  * **More careful management of receivables and payables** : By analyzing customer payment behaviors, for instance, manufacturers can streamline collections and encourage timely payments through incentives. 
  * **Balancing capital expenditures with operational needs** : Investments in machinery, technology or facilities should align with long-term strategic goals while ensuring that sufficient cash remains available for operational needs. By evaluating the return on investment (ROI) of capital expenditures, manufacturers can prioritize projects that will generate the most significant positive impact on free cash flow.
  * **Using technology to measure capital expenditure ROI** : Using Internet of Things devices, sensors and even digital twin simulations to gather data can help manufacturers assess how useful various capital expenditures may be. As part of the effort to measure ROI, organizations should also assess technical debt and suboptimal technology infrastructure across operations.


Well-planned capital expenses are another key factor in generating free cash flow. Manufacturers should ensure they have a rigorous budgeting process that assesses the necessity and potential return of each investment (balance sheet optimization, for instance, may help). By prioritizing projects that lead to increased productivity or cost savings, manufacturers can maximize the impact of their capital expenditures.
Short-term investments are just as important for organizations to assess as long-term investments. For instance, those with a short payback period, as well as opportunities with future paybacks—such as investing in energy-efficient machinery—may require a substantial up-front cost but can significantly reduce operational expenses over time.
## Strategic use of debt
Even amid higher interest rates, the strategic use of debt can be a critical component of a working capital strategy, providing manufacturers with the financial flexibility to pursue growth initiatives and maintain operational stability.
One area where strategic debt investment might have the biggest payoff is automation. While borrowing costs are higher, strategically automating processes can lead to enhanced efficiency, reduced labor costs and improved product quality. By securing financing to invest in automation technologies—such as robotics or advanced manufacturing systems—companies can streamline operations, significantly boost productivity and often get a relatively quick return on investment. As production processes become more efficient, manufacturers can better respond to market demands, leading to increased profitability.
Companies can also strategically use debt to finance expansion into new markets, particularly in areas with lower interest rates. Entering new geographical areas or diversifying product lines can be capital-intensive but often results in increased revenue streams. By securing loans to support market entry strategies, manufacturers can tap into new distribution networks and localized production capabilities, enhancing their competitive position and cash flow potential.
Finally, ongoing management and renegotiation, refinancing or extending terms of existing loans is another key element in the strategic use of debt. Manufacturers should regularly review their debt structures to identify opportunities for better terms or lower interest rates. This may reduce monthly obligations and free up cash for operational needs or strategic investments.
## Cost management
Cost management is integral to a successful working capital strategy, particularly for manufacturers dealing with margin compression.
Inventory management is central to managing costs and often a significant missed opportunity to free up cash. Manufacturers must strike a delicate balance between maintaining sufficient inventory levels to meet production needs and minimizing excess stock that ties up valuable working capital. Optimizing inventory levels can also minimize waste and improve overall operational efficiency.
Productivity is another essential component of effective cost management. Manufacturers can enhance productivity through employee training, process optimization and the implementation of best practices. Encouraging a culture of continuous improvement enables employees to identify inefficiencies and contribute ideas to make processes better.
Investing in employee training can also lead to higher quality outputs, which can reduce costs associated with defects and rework. By prioritizing and empowering employees in this way, manufacturers can significantly enhance their overall cost management strategy.
## Resilience for the future
A sound, well-crafted working capital strategy is essential for manufacturers to thrive in an increasingly competitive landscape. Prioritizing cash flow, using debt strategically and implementing rigorous cost management practices can drive optimization that is so essential amid higher interest rates.
By integrating these components, companies can build a resilient framework that supports both immediate operational needs and long-term growth objectives.
