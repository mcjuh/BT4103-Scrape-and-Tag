#  What are Scope 1, Scope 2, and Scope 3 emissions? 
Last updated: January 2023

Scope 1, Scope 2, and Scope 3 emissions are a categorisation framework used to classify greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions associated with an organisation's activities, products, or services.
These categories help in understanding the sources of emissions and devising strategies to mitigate them effectively.
  1. Scope 1 Emissions: Scope 1 emissions encompass direct GHG emissions that result from sources that are owned or controlled by the organization. These emissions are produced from activities such as burning fossil fuels on-site (e.g., company-owned vehicles, boilers, furnaces) and other processes like chemical reactions that occur within the organization's boundaries.
  2. Scope 2 Emissions: Scope 2 emissions include indirect GHG emissions resulting from the generation of purchased electricity, heat, or steam consumed by the organisation. These emissions occur outside of the organisation's direct operational control but are associated with the energy it purchases and uses.
  3. Scope 3 Emissions: Scope 3 emissions encompass all other indirect GHG emissions that occur in the value chain of the organisation. These emissions are associated with activities and sources both upstream and downstream from the organisation, including but not limited to: 
    1. Upstream: Purchased goods and services, transportation of goods, extraction and production of purchased materials, and employee commuting.
    2. Downstream: Use and disposal of products sold by the organisation, investments, and other activities related to the organisation's products during their lifecycle.
