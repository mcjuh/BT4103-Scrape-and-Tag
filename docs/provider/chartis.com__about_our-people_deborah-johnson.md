# Deborah Johnson
Partner and Vice President of Learning 
Deb Johnson is Partner and Vice President of Learning with Chartis, where she is responsible for driving measurable business impact by designing, aligning, and ensuring execution of skill development for all Chartis colleagues. She has more than 30 years of consulting, talent, and learning experience and has worked to drive strategic change, cultivate hot skills, and enhance recruitment, retention, and advancement among large and diverse workforces.
Prior to joining Chartis, Deb was Chief Learning Officer for the Deloitte Technology Academy, where she drove tech skill development for the organization’s 170,000+ employees across the US, India and Mexico including AI, cloud, cyber, blockchain, and quantum computing as well as software engineering and major ERP packages. She has also led learning for Deloitte’s Consulting practice including leadership and professional skills development, new hire on-boarding, and industry/sector skill development. Deb began her career as a strategy & operations consultant, conducting strategic analysis and driving process improvements for clients in various industries including high-tech, telecommunications, media, healthcare (payer), and financial services.Deb received her Master of Business Administration from the University of California, Los Angeles Anderson Graduate School of Management. She also holds dual Bachelor of Arts degrees in Economics and Dramatic Literature from New York University.

###  What have you found to be the most rewarding part of your career? 
Everything I do is about making people into the best versions of themselves so that they can make the biggest impact in the world, and I find that hugely rewarding. Doing this work in the context of healthcare and knowing the potential downstream impact on people’s health and wellbeing makes that reward especially sweet. 
###  Best piece of advice you’ve ever been given? 
My father once told me ‘Something is only a problem if it has a solution. Otherwise, it’s just a fact of life.’ Over the years, I’ve found that little nugget of wisdom to be incredibly useful in breaking through the inertia that comes with feeling overwhelmed and spurring me to focus on what I can influence to drive positive change. 
###  What inspires you? 
Nelson Mandela said ‘Education is the most powerful weapon you can use to change the world’ and that resonates deeply with me. I come from a big family of professional educators-- both my parents and my husband’s parents were all teachers, and they inspire me as well.
