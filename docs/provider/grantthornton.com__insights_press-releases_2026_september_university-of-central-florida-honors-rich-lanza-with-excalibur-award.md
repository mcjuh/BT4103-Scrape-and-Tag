# University of Central Florida honors Grant Thornton’s Rich Lanza with Excalibur award for accounting innovation
September 03, 2026 
**ORLANDO** —The University of Central Florida (UCF) today announced that [**Rich Lanza**](https://www.grantthornton.com/people/new-jersey/edison/lanza-rich), managing director within Audit & Assurance Services at Grant Thornton LLP, has been named the inaugural recipient of its Excalibur Innovator in Accounting Award — citing his efforts to exponentially grow the use of a complete set of general ledger data as evidence to be fully analyzed when completing the financial statement audit.
Established by UCF’s Dixon School of Accounting, the award recognizes individuals advancing the profession through groundbreaking contributions, including the application of data, technology and forward-looking ideas that are reshaping modern accounting and auditing.
## A leader in audit innovation
Lanza brings more than 35 years of experience to his role at Grant Thornton LLP, where he helps to enhance the firm’s risk analysis, audit approaches and client insights.
Lanza’s work has redefined how auditors use data — moving beyond traditional, retrospective methods to more dynamic approaches that identify risk, detect anomalies and potentially uncover fraud.
Widely recognized for advancing general ledger analytics, Lanza has played a key role in developing journal and text entry analysis techniques that now enable auditors to efficiently analyze large volumes of financial and text data, respectively. His contributions have also influenced how the profession applies the professional auditing standards related to fraud risk and testing methodologies.
The inflection point in computer assisted audit technologies likely occurred when Lanza provided the AICPA’s fraud task force his published works to define and bridge the technology gap in journal entry testing, as promulgated in SAS 99 (2001). Journal entry testing morphed due to SAS 99, from a top-level account balance analysis to an atomic level inspection of journal entry debits and credits. Grant Thornton capitalized on Rich’s general ledger innovations in being [**awarded a patent**](https://www.grantthornton.com/insights/press-releases/2021/october/gt-awarded-patent-for-innovative-technology-in-whole-ledger-analytics) for their journal entry technology.
“Audit analytics over the past 25 years would have not been the same without the transition from balance to ledger that Rich has been so instrumental in advancing,” said Jay Thibodeau, director of the Dixon School of Accounting at UCF. “He has been a driving force in modernizing audit through the practical application of data and analytics. His work has significantly improved how auditors identify risk and deliver insight, and his influence on both practice and thought leadership makes him the perfect initial recipient of our newly created award.”
Thibodeau stressed that Lanza’s work has culminated in the creation of a data ‘lake’: “Rich’s contributions to the profession provide clarity and direction previously unrevealed within accounting information, allowing practitioners to effectively guide and better serve their clients.”
Before joining Grant Thornton, Lanza spent more than 12 years leading his own firm, where he integrated data systems into internal audit and advisory functions. He began his career at KPMG Peat Marwick in 1991 and subsequently held a range of important technical roles in the industry, including assisting Toys R Us with cost recovery while also managing the Year 2000 project for the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants.
He holds a degree in public accounting from Pace University, is a frequent conference speaker and has authored more than 20 books, as well as numerous articles and research papers.
## An award reflecting UCF’s vision for the future
The Excalibur Innovator in Accounting Award reflects UCF’s commitment to leading through innovation. Faculty at the Dixon School of Accounting created the award to emphasize forward-looking research and real-world application in accounting and auditing.
This philosophy extends to the classroom, where UCF equips students with critical thinking skills and data fluency to address emerging challenges in the profession.
“Advancing the profession requires us to think differently about how we teach, how we research and how we engage with practitioners,” Thibodeau said. “Creating this award is a natural extension of our mission to bridge the gap between academic insight and professional impact.”
UCF presented Lanza with the award in May 2026 at its annual accounting conference, which convenes academics, practitioners and industry leaders to explore the trends shaping the future of accounting.

**About University of Central Florida and the Dixon School of Accounting**
The mission of the Dixon School of Accounting at the University of Central Florida is to pursue excellence in accounting education. As a professional school in a major public research university, we are committed to teaching, research and service to the profession that advance the accounting discipline and prepare future leaders in business, government and academia.
Our state-of-the-art curriculum provides students with technical expertise in the field and develops critical thinking, team building, communication and information technology skills. We offer a four-year undergraduate Bachelors of Science in Business Administration (BSBA), a Master of Science in Accounting (MSA) and a Doctor of Philosophy degree in Accounting (Ph.D.). Both the Dixon School of Accounting and the Barry S. Miller College of Business are among an elite group of programs that are independently accredited by the AACSB.
