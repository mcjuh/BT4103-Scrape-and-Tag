## IT architecture now defines business strategy
As AI agents move from pilots to production, systems make autonomous decisions at volumes no human governance can realistically supervise. In this environment, technology architecture now determines what the business can pursue—and how fast it can move.For CIOs, CTOs, and other C-suite technology leaders, the challenge lies not in deploying new tools but in redesigning an enterprise technology foundation built for a slower, more predictable environment. Many tech leaders are still operating with IT architectures optimized for stability, governance models dependent on manual review, and investment frameworks built for multi-year asset lifecycles—approaches that can't keep pace with AI's speed.Those making progress aren't attempting complete IT overhauls. They're making targeted investments in infrastructure adaptability, governance by design, and portfolio discipline—three pillars that work together to create structural readiness.Research from the 2026 Tech Leader Study, conducted in partnership with Oxford Economics, reveals how CIOs and CTOs must engineer these three pillars into their organizations to scale AI with speed and control.

## Infrastructure adaptability enables AI at scale.
For years, preparedness meant stability—aligning to a known operating model optimized for continuity and cost. That logic worked when capabilities evolved slowly. But when models, platforms, and tools change faster than planning cycles, stability becomes a barrier.For CIOs and CTOs, preparedness today means optionality—the ability to shift workloads, rotate models, and absorb new capabilities without triggering large-scale disruption. By that measure, many organizations are less prepared than they assume.As enterprises scale from hundreds to thousands of AI agents, immobility morphs from technical inconvenience into strategic constraint. Our research finds cloud costs exceeded original projections by 48% on average, and 80% of tech leaders report data transfer costs higher than expected. The root cause is architectural: while 88% of organizations are attempting to move workloads to a different cloud provider, only 25% of those workloads are easily portable today.
> “The most critical architectural capability is integration. We don't know what's coming next, so the foundation must support constant change.”
Part 2:
## Governance by design makes AI control possible.
Optionality creates speed, and speed creates exposure—unless control is built in. For years, control in enterprise technology was exercised through policy, approvals, and review cycles. That approach worked when systems moved at human pace. When AI agents operate autonomously at scale, it breaks down.By 2027, enterprises expect to deploy an average of 1,661 AI agents—a 38% increase from today. At that rate, tech leaders are tasked with managing hundreds of thousands of autonomous decisions daily. And manual governance can't keep up with that math.For CIOs and CTOs, this means governance must shift from reviewing what happened to engineering what's possible—before systems go live. Organizations that engineer control into their AI systems deploy 16x more agents than those relying on manual governance, while spending 4x less of their AI budget and delivering 18% higher operating margins.

## Portfolio discipline redefines AI investment efficiency.
Efficiency has long meant cost discipline. That meaning made sense when technology investments were built to last and depreciated over years. But AI introduces assets with an average 14-month lifecycle and uneven returns, where success is unpredictable and timing is compressed.This changes things for today’s tech leaders. Traditional capital planning—where investments are approved once, deployed, and depreciated over years—no longer applies. CIOs and CTOs must now build mechanisms to refresh models, redirect capital from underperforming bets, and scale what works—in real time, as learning accelerates.At AI scale, efficiency is about allocating better, not just spending less. Yet 84% of technology leaders haven’t fully operationalized AI financial management, and 85% lack full visibility into real-time AI spend. Organizations that manage AI as a portfolio—rather than optimizing investments individually—deploy 2.4x more agents with no higher AI/IT budget.
> “We’re building a control plane for AI, so we can identify what's productive, elevate what's valuable, and retire what isn't. Some experimentation waste is unavoidable—but that's the price of learning fast.”
## Prepare your IT foundation for AI at scale
CIOs and CTOs aren’t just enabling business strategy—they're now defining what strategies are even possible. In an AI‑driven enterprise, and as quantum capabilities emerge, the differentiator isn't boldness. It's whether the organization is built to place big bets with speed, discipline, and confidence.
