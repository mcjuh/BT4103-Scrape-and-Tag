# AI in motion
In collaboration with Dubai Future Foundation, we investigate how orchestrating AI governance drives enterprise value.

#### What is an AI orchestration layer?
Adding an AI orchestration layer is helping enterprises move past stalled pilots and fragmented deployments—and toward AI that scales, integrates, and delivers measurable value. Our research shows sizeable benefits. 
#### The economics of coordinated AI
The financial implications are substantial. A $20‑billion enterprise loses roughly $140 million a year to AI irregularities—half due to governance gaps. Without system-level visibility, organizations are building portfolios they can’t control. 
**Benefits for organizations that lean into orchestration-led governance**
#### Why AI orchestration and governance matter now: AI is advancing faster than the structures built to manage it
Across industries, enterprises are running dozens of AI pilots and early deployments. Yet despite significant investment and widespread experimentation, many leaders report that progress slows the moment AI needs to scale. Systems built for individual use cases rarely connect. Governance varies by team, region, and vendor. And as AI becomes more autonomous, its interactions across workflows grow harder to track. Only 18% of organizations maintain a complete AI inventory, which puts them at a disadvantage.
The research highlights a central tension: AI has become powerful enough to influence critical decisions, but most organizations still govern it with frameworks designed for earlier, slower technology. Visibility is partial. Ownership is diffused. And as AI proliferates, fragmentation becomes increasingly expensive.
> AI sprawl is already eroding as much value as companies are creating.
Executives describe an environment full of activity but short on cohesion. They want AI that acts as a unified capability—but they are operating with a landscape of siloed models and agents that rarely “see” each other.
The result is inconsistent outcomes, limited reuse, and growing operational risk. Orchestration can address all three issues. 
#### AI governance is being redefined
For years, AI governance meant compliance, security checks, data policies, and periodic model reviews. But AI no longer fits into static oversight cycles. Agentic systems now make decisions continuously, collaborate across functions, and adjust behavior based on real‑time conditions.
The scope of governance has expanded accordingly. Today it includes transparency, resource efficiency, ethical design, environmental impact, workforce considerations, and risk management. And it must reconcile all of this across a patchwork of global regulations.
Enterprises increasingly need governance to be operational—embedded directly into the systems where AI runs—rather than a set of guidelines that lag behind the technology.
> Most enterprises have built AI portfolios they can’t fully see, let alone govern.
This evolution is where orchestration comes in. It transforms governance from a set of digital documents into an active capability. 
#### Orchestration—the new backbone of enterprise AI
An AI orchestration layer coordinates the full landscape of AI activity across an organization. It connects models, agents, workflows, and data flows into a coherent system. It makes governance programmable. It ensures policies follow the AI wherever it operates. And it prevents the drift, duplication, and operational inconsistencies that stall scale.
Orchestration gives leaders a unified view of their AI estate:
  * What systems exist
  * Where they operate
  * What data they access
  * How they behave across regions
  * How they interact with one another


The results are clear across the research. Organizations using orchestration-led governance report stronger productivity gains--a holistic orchestration layer is associated with six times greater productivity impact than compliance‑only approaches. AI also becomes more predictable, more accountable, and more strategically useful. 
#### Sovereignty’s rising role in the future of enterprise AI
Ninety‑three percent of executives say sovereignty now shapes their AI strategy.
As AI systems operate across markets, enterprises must manage diverse data residency rules, security requirements, and jurisdiction‑specific regulations. AI agents can move faster than legal review cycles. Data may need to remain in certain countries even as insights move globally.
Orchestration enables this. It encodes sovereignty directly into the AI stack. Policies become dynamic. Systems adapt automatically to local rules. Enterprises maintain global reach with local control—something nearly impossible to do manually.
The result is a foundation for scaling AI safely, responsibly, and internationally. 
#### Ecosystem collaboration requires governance that can move with the AI
Partners, suppliers, and customers interact with enterprise AI systems directly. Yet only of organizations extend governance across their ecosystem. 
This is an emerging risk—and an emerging opportunity.
Orchestration allows identity, permissions, and policy enforcement to travel with the AI as it moves across organizations. It creates a consistent governance environment that supports multi‑agent collaboration while preserving enterprise control.
[IBM’s own transformation](https://www.ibm.com/case-studies/ibm-office-of-privacy-and-responsible-technology) illustrates the potential. By centralizing governance upstream, the company shortened data clearance cycles dramatically and made more than 2,000 datasets and models ready for reuse. Governance became a source of speed. 
#### The new model for AI leadership
By 2030, AI will be embedded across most large organizations. The leaders emerging in the data treat orchestration not as a technical upgrade, but as strategic architecture. They invest in systems that can adapt across borders, respond to real‑time conditions, and keep autonomous AI aligned with enterprise intent.
They prioritize workforce readiness, operational transparency, and control of core AI components. Most importantly, they treat governance as infrastructure—an enabler of speed, resilience, and long‑term value creation. 
**FAQ**
What is an AI orchestration layer?
An AI orchestration layer is the operating structure that coordinates models, agents, workflows, data flows, and governance policies across the enterprise. It provides continuous visibility, consistent guardrails, and a unified execution environment so AI operates as a connected system. 
Why is orchestration essential now?
AI estates have expanded faster than organizations can govern them. Most enterprises run pilots with limited coordination, and only 18% maintain a current AI inventory. Agentic AI raises the stakes: systems act autonomously, interact with other agents, and make decisions at machine speed. Orchestration embeds governance directly into operations—enforcing policies, aligning behavior, and delivering the real‑time visibility required to scale AI safely and consistently. 
What are the biggest benefits of using an AI orchestration layer to govern AI?
The IBM + DFF study shows orchestration-led governance results in:
  * 13x higher likelihood of scaling AI
  * ~30% fewer irregularities, which cost a $20B company ~$140M annually
  * 20% higher ROI
  * 169% greater transparency
  * 132% stronger data‑privacy protection


These gains turn AI from isolated experimentation into a coordinated enterprise capability. 
Does AI orchestration and AI governance improve enterprise value?
Yes--roughly $140 million annually in a $20‑billion enterprise. Half of that impact comes from preventable irregularities—duplication, drift, inconsistent performance, and lack of reuse. With orchestration, organizations see about $48 million in annual value preserved or generated. 
How does AI orchestration help organizations meet sovereignty requirements?
Ninety‑three percent of executives factor sovereignty into their AI strategy. Orchestration embeds sovereignty directly into the AI architecture—automatically applying data‑residency rules, national security requirements, and jurisdiction‑specific policies as AI systems operate across regions. This ensures compliance while maintaining global scale.
