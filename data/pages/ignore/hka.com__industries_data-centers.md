# Data Centers
Clients face speed‑to‑market pressure, complex MEP integration, and tightening energy and sustainability constraints, increasing exposure to delay, defects, outages, and high‑value disputes that directly impact uptime, service availability, and operational resilience.

## Understanding data center risk
From site selection and grid interconnection through construction, commissioning, and operation, data centers face a unique combination of technical, commercial, and legal challenges. 
Common issues include:
  * Schedule pressure driven by speed‑to‑market and revenue imperatives
  * Delay and disruption arising from long‑lead equipment, procurement strategies, labor availability, trade stacking, and other impacts
  * Design development challenges, particularly where tenant requirements change after construction has commenced
  * MEP system failures affecting power, cooling, redundancy, and resilience
  * Commissioning, handover, and integration issues between construction and specialist installations
  * Energy availability, grid interconnection, and regulatory constraints
  * Sustainability, carbon, and water performance disputes, including greenwashing allegations
  * Outages, cyber incidents, and physical security failures that can rapidly escalate into business interruption, insurance, and contractual claims
  * Increased reliance on behind‑the‑meter generation creates standard‑of‑care and due‑diligence risk when systems traditionally built by power producers are executed under construction‐driven delivery models.
  * Commissioning risk is rising as generation, UPS, controls, and protection systems are integrated without consistent access to specialized testing equipment and experienced power‑sector manpower.
  * Microgrid configurations, especially islanding and resynchronization with the grid, can introduce instability or equipment damage if protection and controls are not rigorously designed and validated.
  * Long lead times for major equipment like transformers and generators create schedule and financial exposure that can determine whether a project proceeds or stalls.
  * Performance shortfalls after commissioning often lead to disputes over whether issues stem from design, construction, testing scope, or operational guidelines.
  * Regulatory requirements for grid interconnection are changing rapidly across jurisdictions, creating uncertainty and delay risk that can materially impact project timelines.
  * Mandates requiring capacity matching with renewables are pushing developers toward complex front‑of‑the‑meter strategies with added execution and market risk.


In many cases, a technical issue does not remain isolated. It quickly becomes a commercial dispute requiring independent expert analysis across schedule, engineering, and damages.
