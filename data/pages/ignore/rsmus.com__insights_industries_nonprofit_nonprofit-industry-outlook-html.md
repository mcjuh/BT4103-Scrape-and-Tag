#### Nonprofit industry outlook
# The great wealth transfer: Opportunities for philanthropy
July 23, 2026
##  Key takeaways 
A massive intergenerational wealth transfer will create major opportunities for philanthropy.
Nonprofits should engage heirs, spouses and advisors before assets transfer.
Women donors will play a major role as transferred wealth shifts to spouses.
## What is the nonprofit outlook?
RSM’s nonprofit outlook examines key trends shaping the industry. The United States is entering the largest wealth transfer in its history, creating a significant opportunity for philanthropy. Nonprofits must refine their donor engagement now, build sustainable funding streams and advance long-term mission goals as philanthropic priorities evolve.
##  Nonprofit trend #1: The great wealth transfer will create opportunities for philanthropy 
[ The great wealth transfer: Opportunities for philanthropy The Real Economy industry update—Nonprofit: The great wealth transfer will create a significant opportunity for philanthropy. Read more # Nonprofit Nonprofit ](https://rsmus.com/insights/industries/nonprofit/great-wealth-transfer-philanthropy-opportunities.html)
The great wealth transfer is already underway, but it is more likely to unfold as a long-term, multistate transition than a sudden windfall. Nonprofit leaders who modernize fundraising, strengthen donor relationships, engage women and next-generation donors, and build the right capabilities now will successfully convert this historic shift into lasting, mission-driven support. Those who delay will risk missing one of the most significant philanthropic opportunities in a generation.
[ Learn more about the great wealth transfer ](https://rsmus.com/insights/industries/nonprofit/great-wealth-transfer-philanthropy-opportunities.html "Learn more about the great wealth transfer")
##  Nonprofit trend #2: Changing donor communication in a new tax environment 
[ Nonprofits rethink donor communication in a new tax environment The Real Economy industry update—Non-profit: Organizations rethink donor communication in a new tax environment. Learn more # Nonprofit Nonprofit ](https://rsmus.com/insights/industries/nonprofit/nonprofits-rethink-donor-communication-new-tax-environment.html)
Some tax changes to the charitable contribution deduction rules could affect nonprofits and their donors. These changes in giving will evolve in ways that could disrupt established fundraising models. For nonprofit leaders, the implication is clear: maintaining traditional communication strategies may not be enough. Growth in the new tax environment will require sharper messaging, smarter segmentation and stronger technology infrastructure.
[ Learn more about donor communication in a new tax environment ](https://rsmus.com/insights/industries/nonprofit/nonprofits-rethink-donor-communication-new-tax-environment.html "Learn more about donor communication in a new tax environment")
##  Nonprofit trend #3: The growing popularity of impact investing 
[ Nonprofits may benefit from the growing popularity of impact investing The Real Economy industry update—Nonprofit: Impact investing can benefit organizations in various ways. Read more # Nonprofit Education Nonprofit Education ](https://rsmus.com/insights/industries/nonprofit/nonprofit-industry-outlook-summer-2025.html)
Nonprofit organizations are working to adapt to the evolving philanthropy landscape of changing federal priorities, international trade modifications and ongoing policy shifts. The dual pressures of rising operational expenses and decreased federal funding are reshaping how nonprofits plan, secure resources and deliver programs. While challenging, this creates opportunities for the adoption of innovative strategies, and chief among them is impact investing.
Impact investing is an investment strategy that aims to generate measurable social or environmental benefits alongside financial returns. This approach aligns with the objectives of many nonprofit organizations, enabling them to seek investments from individuals or groups interested in their cause.
[ Learn more about impact investing ](https://rsmus.com/insights/industries/nonprofit/nonprofit-industry-outlook-summer-2025.html "Learn more about impact investing")
##  Nonprofit trend #4: Charitable giving will remain resilient 
[ Charitable giving will remain resilient despite economic headwinds The Real Economy industry update—Nonprofit: A downturn in giving will likely be small and short-lived, with a solid recovery in 2026 and beyond. Read more # Nonprofit Economics  Nonprofit Economics  ](https://rsmus.com/insights/industries/nonprofit/nonprofit-industry-outlook-spring-2025.html)
Among the many stressors mounting for nonprofit leaders in 2025, one rises to the top: a potential economic downturn roiling their donor base. Many economists now project an [economic downturn](https://rsmus.com/middle-market/the-real-economy.html "economic downturn")—if not a full recession—later this year. So what will be the likely impact on nonprofits?
Historical data and key positive trends in philanthropy show that nonprofits can offset risks related to an economic downturn. Understanding this data and trends is vital for nonprofit leaders in planning and executing their organizational budgets and fundraising strategies.
[ Learn more about how charitable giving will remain resilient ](https://rsmus.com/insights/industries/nonprofit/nonprofit-industry-outlook-spring-2025.html "Learn more about how charitable giving will remain resilient")
##  Nonprofit trend #5: Nonprofits are building for the future with DAFs 
[ Nonprofits building for the future with DAFs The Real Economy industry update—nonprofit: The growth of DAFS is helping nonprofits build for the future. Read more # Nonprofit Nonprofit ](https://rsmus.com/insights/industries/nonprofit/nonprofit-industry-outlook-winter-2025.html)
Total grants from donor advised funds (DAFs) have grown significantly over the past decade, nearly doubling in the last five years alone. DAFs permit donors to irrevocably contribute assets, including appreciated property, to public charities that own, sponsor and control the funds. Donors obtain an immediate income tax deduction and retain advisory privileges over the investment and distribution of such assets for charitable purposes.
DAFs are popular philanthropic vehicles because they alleviate administrative burdens commonly associated with stand-alone charitable organizations and typically accept gifts of complex assets, among other benefits.
[ Learn more about nonprofits building for the future with DAFs ](https://rsmus.com/insights/industries/nonprofit/nonprofit-industry-outlook-winter-2025.html "Learn more about nonprofits building for the future with DAFs")
