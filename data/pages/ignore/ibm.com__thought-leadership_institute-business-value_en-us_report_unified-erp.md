# Unified ERP for the agentic enterprise
In partnership with SAP, we examine how agentic AI exposes ERP fragmentation and demands semantic alignment at scale.
[Download the insights (will open in a new tab)](https://www.ibm.com/downloads/documents/us-en/16951f2649b789d8)[Subscribe for more insights from IBM IBV (will open in a new window)](https://www.ibm.com/thought-leadership/institute-business-value/en-us/newsletters)
Most enterprises don’t have an AI problem. They have a meaning problem.
As organizations experiment with agentic AI, they’re realizing a hard truth: autonomy collapses when the enterprise can’t agree on what things mean. When “customer” and “product” have various definitions across systems, agents don’t accelerate work. They pause, escalate, or make decisions that can’t be trusted. This is why so much AI ambition is stalling not in the lab, but in operations.
It’s also why ERP has become strategic again—not just as a system of record, but as the system that defines reality. ERP is where the enterprise decides what is true, how work connects across functions, and which decisions machines are allowed to execute. AI doesn’t smooth over ERP fragmentation. It exposes it. Every customization, inconsistency, and workaround becomes a risk multiplier as autonomy increases.
New research from the IBM Institute for Business Value (IBM IBV) shows a clear inflection point. According to our survey of 1,000 senior executives spanning 20 industries and 12 countries, the organizations that treat ERP as semantic infrastructure—not just transactional plumbing—are the ones able to move from AI pilots to trusted, scaled autonomy. They aren’t modernizing ERP to keep systems current. They’re doing it to make the enterprise legible to machines.
More than two-thirds (68%) of enterprises say accelerating ERP modernization is critical to achieving competitive advantage and operational efficiency. The question now facing leaders isn’t whether agentic AI is coming. It’s whether their ERP can be trusted with it.
> AI doesn’t smooth over ERP fragmentation—it exposes it.
###  Why do semantics determine whether agentic AI can scale?
Despite 99% of organizations expecting cloud‑modernized ERP by 2028, only 38% have embedded semantic definitions into their AI models today. Without shared meaning for core entities like “customer” or “product,” agents hesitate or escalate decisions. As a result, organizations estimate their AI initiatives are moving 30% slower than they could, turning fragmentation into a brake on autonomy.
In Part 1, we examine how consistent enterprise semantics turn ERP into a foundation agents can actually reason on.
> 68% of organizations say accelerating ERP modernization is critical to achieving competitive advantage and operational efficiency.
### How does governance multiply return on agentic AI investments?
Organizations with strong governance and moderate AI maturity outperform high‑tech, low‑governance peers—reporting 7% higher ROI and 163% greater confidence in AI reliability. Yet fewer than half have governance structures in place to oversee autonomous agents. Governance operationalizes semantics, ensuring shared definitions translate into accountable, auditable decisions at scale.
Part 2 explores how governance turns autonomy into an asset—making AI decisions trusted, traceable, and repeatable at scale.

### How do cross‑silo agents create structural advantage?
Only 46% of organizations say their ERP enables effective cross‑functional coordination. Those that do report higher AI ROI and broader workflow transformation. With unified semantics, cross‑silo agents can orchestrate end‑to‑end workflows—eliminating handoffs, surfacing exceptions early, and preventing small inconsistencies from cascading into operational risk.
In Part 3, we investigate how agentic coordination across functions unlocks efficiencies no single silo can achieve.
