##  Understanding the energy industry and how RSM supports energy companies 
The energy industry encompasses the exploration, production, generation, transportation and distribution of resources that power economies. Key segments include oil and gas—spanning upstream exploration and production, midstream transportation and storage, and downstream refining and distribution—as well as electric power and utilities and a rapidly growing renewables sector, such as wind, solar and energy storage. Together, these segments form an interconnected value chain, with companies balancing legacy operations and new energy investments to meet evolving demand, regulatory requirements and sustainability goals.
RSM works with organizations across the energy industry to help them respond to change with clarity and confidence. Our teams combine industry knowledge with practical insight to help energy companies manage risk, improve performance and position their businesses for long-term resilience.
## Energy industry trends
Companies in the energy sector will need to adapt their operations proactively to thrive in an evolving landscape. This imperative applies across the energy ecosystem, whether a company’s segment is oil and gas, power and utilities, or renewable/clean energy.
The evolving energy mix, shifting energy policies and supply chain dynamics are just some of the factors that businesses in this sector face. A continued focus on resilience will be essential for companies to remain competitive.
Here are the top six trends RSM has identified for the energy industry:
**Growth across energy sources**
The North American energy landscape is experiencing a major transformation. Demand for energy is growing rapidly, fueled by a combination of factors: increasing data center needs, the reshoring of manufacturing, widespread electrification and electric vehicle adoption.
[ Related insight ](https://rsmus.com/insights/industries/energy/the-evolving-energy-mix.html "Related insight")
**Shifting energy policy and market dynamics**
In the United States, a higher-for-longer cost of capital has been driving energy companies toward consolidation and streamlining; it is also prompting them to preserve cash and to focus more strategically on improving enterprise value through margin expansion and additional revenue opportunities.
[ Related insight ](https://rsmus.com/insights/services/business-tax/obbba-energy-sector.html "Related insight")
Multicolor icon
**Digital transformation and the rise of AI**
To remain competitive and resilient, energy companies are increasing their adoption of cloud solutions, automation and/or artificial intelligence across business and operational processes, providing improved efficiency, reliability and safety. 
[ Related insight ](https://rsmus.com/insights/industries/energy/digital-solutions-can-help-with-energy-grid-modernization.html "Related insight")
**Energy cybersecurity and resilience**
As companies across the energy value chain increasingly become digital-first companies—with interconnected systems and automated business and industrial processes—more opportunities arise for cybercriminals to compromise businesses and disrupt critical operations.
[ Related insight ](https://rsmus.com/insights/industries/energy/2026-cybersecurity-mmbi-energy-snapshot.html "Related insight")
**Supply chain shifts**
Tariffs and an evolving trade environment will continue to affect organizations' supply chains and investment decisions, with direct impacts ranging from higher prices for key metals like steel and copper as well as finished goods, to extended lead times for some items.
[ Related insight ](https://rsmus.com/insights/economics/the-us-is-in-a-better-position-to-endure-an-oil-and-gas-shock.html "Related insight")
Multicolor icon
**Workforce transformation**
Companies must adopt more intentional and innovative approaches to attract and retain talent, especially as employee expectations and demographics continue to evolve. The rapid advancement of AI and the digitization of operations demand that the current workforce adapt through upskilling and reskilling.
[ Related insight ](https://rsmus.com/insights/industries/energy/the-top-6-trends-shaping-the-energy-sector.html "Related insight")

## Frequently asked questions 

How is the energy industry using AI to improve operations?  
    
Companies are using AI to optimize asset performance, predict equipment failures and improve maintenance planning, helping reduce downtime and extend asset life. Advanced analytics and machine learning also enable better demand forecasting and grid management. RSM can help energy companies define a practical AI strategy and implement scalable solutions that align with business objectives. 

How are energy supply chains shifting amid global volatility?  
    
Energy supply chains face pressure from geopolitics, localization trends and equipment constraints. Leaders are diversifying suppliers, improving visibility and stress-testing scenarios to balance cost and reliability. RSM can help assess supply chain risk and build more resilient sourcing and planning models. 

How should energy companies manage the evolving energy mix and growing demand?  
    
The inclusion of renewables and low-carbon options adds complexity to portfolios, capital planning and compliance. Companies must balance returns, reliability and stakeholder expectations. RSM can help evaluate portfolio strategies and align operating models in the context of this changing energy mix. 

How should the energy industry respond to evolving cybersecurity risk?  
    
Cyberthreats increasingly target operational technology, remote access and third parties. Energy leaders should focus on asset visibility, segmentation and tested response plans. RSM can help assess cyber risk and design security programs that support safe, reliable operations.
