# Navigating autonomous networks
In partnership with TM Forum, we explore how telecoms embrace network automation for operations and growth.

#### FOREWORD
Autonomous networks can deliver transformative change for the global telecom industry, driving efficiency and unlocking growth through zero-touch, zero-wait, zero-trouble operations. What started as an aspiration is now a commercial and operational reality—and the next frontier of competitive advantage for communications service providers (CSPs).
The findings from this report reveal both the challenges that CSPs face and the significant opportunities that lie ahead, mirroring findings from TM Forum’s own research. That only 6% currently operate highly autonomous networks underscores the nascent stage of this transformation, yet the projected leap to 22% reaching Level 4 autonomy within three years signals the pace of change that will reshape our industry.
At TM Forum, we see this momentum reflected across our global membership. The shift from concept to execution is tangible—members are applying shared frameworks, open architectures, and best practices to create real operational impact. At our global summit DTW Ignite 2025, held in June, Level 4 capabilities were on display in multiple domains, again demonstrating that autonomous networks are no longer future concepts—they are being operationalized now.
The foundation for this change is TM Forum’s Open Digital Architecture (ODA), now firmly embedded across the industry. Eighteen CSPs, representing over two billion subscribers globally, are already accredited by TM Forum as “Running on ODA”—a testament to the architecture’s modularity and maturity. Our newly launched ODA Component Certification is further reducing integration complexity, empowering the industry to adopt plug-and-play approaches at scale.
Crucially, ODA’s role in enabling agentic AI—systems capable of perceiving, reasoning, and acting—means that AI-native, intent-driven operations are not only possible but are becoming the norm. This fusion of AI and automation is the path to self-healing, self-optimizing networks that deliver operational efficiency and create new monetization opportunities.
Yet this transition requires more than technology; it demands industry-wide collaboration, shared frameworks, and trusted methodologies. This is the rationale driving TM Forum’s Autonomous Networks industry mission: a collaborative, standards-based effort to transform telecom networks into self-managing, intelligent, and future-proof platforms.
This report is a timely and valuable contribution to this journey. Its insights—grounded in executive perspectives and unique industry data—offer a valuable roadmap for those navigating the autonomous networks journey. Through open collaboration and shared commitment, we can revolutionize networks to transform our industry and fuel growth.
**Nik Willetts** CEO, TM Forum
> “Achieving Level 4 autonomy requires a coordinated transformation across data, systems, processes, and culture.”
> **Kim Krogh Andersen** Product and Technology Group Executive, Telstra
### The race to network autonomy is on
Telecom networks are the nervous system of today’s digital world—and they are under pressure like never before. Manual processes and legacy infrastructure are struggling to keep pace with surging data traffic and high customer expectations. As revenue growth stagnates and fierce competition rises from cloud-native operators and hyperscalers, the status quo can’t last. 
Breakthroughs are imminent that will enable CSPs to become true digital players. Autonomous networks that think, learn, and adapt without constant human oversight will deliver efficiencies, reliability, and opportunities. Built on declarative architectures, they can self-configure for automated provisioning, self-heal, and self-optimize in real time via closed-loop controls. These intelligent networks reduce operating costs, improve reliability, and enable on-demand digital services through self-serve, automated, and self-correcting operations. 
Communications service providers are no strangers to evolution, but the transition to autonomous networks is more than an incremental step. It’s a giant leap forward. New research from the IBM Institute for Business Value (IBM IBV) and TM Forum reveals that the race for network autonomy is undeniably on—and those who lead it will redefine the competitive landscape. 
Most CSPs are just off the starting line. Our research shows the average level of autonomy across network domains sits at just 1.9 on TM Forum’s autonomous network maturity scale. Only 6% of CSPs report Level 4, a highly autonomous network (see the figure below). Early adopters are investing strategically to pull away from the pack. But in three years, almost one in four executives (22%) say they expect their organization to reach Level 4. Another 39% anticipate reaching Level 3, up 17 percentage points from today. 
**The first mile** Most CSPs are in the early days of network automation but anticipate reaching higher levels of autonomy quickly.

Defining what makes a network autonomous
An autonomous network is a telecommunications network that self-configures, self-optimizes, self-heals, and self-secures with minimal human intervention and using real-time analytics and machine learning (ML) models to drive closed-loop feedback and intent-based policy enforcement.
#### How it works:
**Declarative/intent-based architecture** Operators define the desired outcome (“intent” or end state), and the automation system determines and executes the “how.”
**Closed-loop controls** A feedback-driven control system that continuously monitors network telemetry compares output against defined setpoints or policies, and automatically adjusts configuration or resources to maintain the desired condition—without human intervention.
**Enabling technologies** These include cloud-native microservices, AI/ML inference engines, intent-based APIs, and an end-to-end observability fabric that ingests streaming telemetry as governed data products.
> “For each domain, we decide the right automation level. It doesn’t make sense to force Level 4 everywhere … our focus is business-driven, not level-driven.”
> **Roberto Kung** Senior Vice President of Technology and Operations, Orange/Sofrecom
**Part one**
### Autonomy takes root
#### Where CSPs are gaining ground 
Tomorrow’s telecom leaders won’t beat complexity with higher headcount. CSP networks have generated massive volumes of alarms and events for decades, but things are moving far beyond human capacity to manage manually. This operational reality, combined with [customer demands for “Zero-X” experiences](https://inform.tmforum.org/features-and-opinion/autonomous-networks-once-a-hype-gradually-becoming-a-reality-for-telcos)—zero wait time, zero human intervention, zero problems—elevates proactive fault detection and rapid self-healing from luxury to imperative. 
In fact, 73% of network executives in our study say their organizations have crafted clear, phased roadmaps that transition from basic automation to truly autonomous operations. Six in 10 say senior leadership prioritizes investments in autonomous network technologies. By 2028, executives expect 35% growth in network budgets as a percentage of annual revenue, with 19% of that directly fueling transformation.
Industry leaders aren’t investing to simply increase their overall level of autonomy—they’re prioritizing domains where automation drives the most value. In fact, many domains plateau at Level 3 because the incremental ROI of reaching Level 4 is outweighed by data gaps or regulatory guardrails. “There are three key drivers: agility, quality of service, and efficiency. For each business entity, fixed, mobile, etc., we align these drivers with its marketing and business strategy,” says Roberto Kung, Senior Vice President of Technology and Operations, Orange/Sofrecom. CSPs start small, choosing where to automate based on ROI, risks, and data readiness, with plans to scale quickly as use cases prove value. 
> “We prioritize [automation focus areas] based on urgent business needs, technological readiness, and industry insights. … Troubleshooting and self-healing are among the most urgent and relatively achievable.”
> **Senior research executive** Asia-Pacific CSP
Overall, CSPs report the most progress with implementing autonomous service ticket resolution and incident self-healing (30%) and autonomous performance monitoring, proactive fault prediction, and predictive maintenance (29%). These domains offer high ROI, fast time-to-value, and alignment with operating expense priorities. 
“[The key] is ensuring each incremental step, from basic automation to intelligent automation pays back meaningfully,” says Jason Keane, Head of Portfolio Business and Operations Support Systems, Ericsson. 
**Setting the pace** Level 3 and 4 CSPs translate concepts into capabilities while Level 0-2 CSPs remain in trial mode.
At the same time, security automation is not negotiable. In our [2024 survey of telecom executives](https://ibm.co/telecom-networks-cloud-ai), 55% said their organization had suffered a network security breach within the past year, and 42% cited network security as a top challenge over the next three years. With threat landscapes expanding faster than human teams can monitor, autonomous security and risk management represents an existential priority regardless of current autonomy level. Indeed, it’s the most implemented use case for Level 3 and 4 CSPs in our new study, with 69% reporting partial or full implementation, and it’s the second-most piloted use case for Level 0-2 CSPs (40%). 
This emphasis suggests both urgent necessity and practical advantage. While telecom providers have established security tooling, [IBM IBV research](https://ibm.co/telecoms-unified-cybersecurity-platform) shows that they juggle an average of 107 different security solutions from 39 vendors. These numbers underscore the need to consolidate security telemetry in a unified observability layer. Moving to integrated security platforms where tools share telemetry, policies, events, and threat intelligence can strengthen defenses and support broader autonomous network objectives through consistent observability, automated detection, and policy-driven remediation. 
> “We have a dedicated committee and growing teams focused on identifying, prioritizing, and executing use cases—both for cost savings and revenue generation.”
> **Ali Tizghadam** Chief Automation Architect, TELUS
**Part two**
### The business case
#### How autonomous networks impact the bottom line
Network autonomy is both an operational concern and a strategic business imperative, driving efficiency and opportunity. Autonomous networks can fundamentally revolutionize service delivery, producing operational expense savings today while laying the foundation for future growth.
In our study, 63% of telecom executives agree that autonomous networks can enable new service models and drive revenue growth; 69% say autonomous network solutions are essential for enhancing customer experiences. 
As network automation extends from a back-office efficiency play to a customer-facing competitive differentiator, KPIs reflect a triple bottom-line impact. High-performing network autonomy boosts operational dependability to support better customer service, which, in turn, drives commercial success. 
“All these drivers are connected—saving costs, improving efficiency, ensuring quality, and increasing customer satisfaction—and together, they help increase revenue," says a senior research executive from an Asia-Pacific CSP.
Incident resolution and customer satisfaction top the list of KPIs, cited by almost half (49%) of executives. Revenue-generating measures also rank highly, including new service deployment speed (44%) and revenue growth from digital innovation (41%). Operational efficiency is prominent as well: energy efficiency and cost savings are each cited by more than 40% of executives. 
> “What are customers monetizing with autonomous networks today? The honest answer is: they aren’t at the end state, but they are capturing value along the journey.”
> **Jason Keane** Head of Portfolio Business and Operations Support Systems Ericsson
**Triple bottom line** Top KPIs used to measure impact of autonomous networks reflect multifaceted business value.
In pursuing network autonomy, CSPs are looking to transcend their traditional role as connectivity providers. Their leading area for pilots: autonomous digital service innovations. These capabilities will help CSPs to personalize offerings at unprecedented scale, dramatically accelerate time-to-market, and monetize advanced network capabilities such as network slicing-as-a-service or guaranteed low-latency gaming experiences. Telstra models an example: “Our adaptive networks enable customers to design, quote, and activate services in real time through a composable, canvas-style interface,” explains Kim Krogh Andersen, Product & Technology Group Executive at Telstra. “This inversion of control empowers customers to tailor their network experience dynamically, rather than relying on static, predefined products.”
Yet, our interviewed executives view near‑term revenue uplift as secondary. Crucially, capabilities such as network platforming, open APIs, and cloud‑native functions depend on high levels of automation to run economically. “We shouldn’t overpromise on revenue,” cautions a senior research executive at an Asia-Pacific CSP. “Autonomy is about agility and quality—and the revenue comes later.”
Early movers on the network automation journey provide a compelling business case for how results play out. Level 3 and 4 CSPs demonstrate:
  * **ROI advantage.** They estimate 19% ROI from their autonomous network investments today versus 13% for those at Level 0-2, with projected 2028 returns of 27% versus 20%.
  * **Investment intensity.** Given those gains, it’s understandable that they are spending 17% more than peers on autonomous networks as a proportion of their network transformation budget—setting the stage for further advantage.
  * **Revenue enablement.** They self-attribute 21% more of their revenue to autonomous network initiatives than Level 0-2 CSPs—likely from improved customer service, faster launch cycles, and compounding new capabilities. 


CSPs pursuing autonomy more aggressively are seeing measurable results. Consider these real-world examples—certified by TM Forum:
  * [Telefónica Vivo](https://www.tmforum.org/press-and-news/validated-progress-proven-value-dtw-ignite-accelerates-the-pathway-to-autonomous-network-maturity-for-the-connectivity-ecosystem/) has reached Level 4 autonomy in network creation and planning for its transmission network, with efficiency gains of up to 90% in route convergence and a 60% reduction in budget approval time.
  * [Ericsson and TDC NET’s Level 4 RAN energy efficiency solution](https://www.ericsson.com/en/news/2025/6/tdc-net-and-ericsson-achieves-industry-first-certification-from-tm-forum-of-level-4-autonomy) reduced the energy consumed to transmit one gigabyte of data by approximately 5%.


Interviewed executives also offered examples:
  * Orange’s SD-WAN domain operates at Level 4, serving B2B clients that demand full self-service and real-time control.
  * TELUS is reclaiming unused IPv4 addresses using an internal closed-loop app, turning them into valuable resources.
  * Verizon’s capital planning and optimization initiative ensures internal and external data is used to identify the best locations for future network investments.


**Perspective**
CSPs take different paths to autonomy
Autonomy has begun to unlock premium-priced offerings at Telstra, with two flagship initiatives—adaptive networks and wireless slicing—demonstrating the commercial potential of autonomous capabilities, notes Telstra’s Andersen. 
The adaptive networks offering represents a fundamental shift in how connectivity is delivered and experienced. It enables customers to design, quote, and activate services in real time through a composable, canvas-style interface. This inversion of control empowers customers to tailor their network experience dynamically, rather than relying on static, predefined products. 
The wireless slicing initiative, built on TM Forum’s Open Digital Architecture (ODA), showcases how autonomy can support SLA-backed, differentiated services. The slicing product experience leverages advanced analytics and observability fabrics to ensure performance in near real time.
For a large Asia-Pacific CSP, the pursuit of autonomous networks began five years ago, says a senior research executive. They initiated several pilot projects including one focused on troubleshooting wireless networks across 5G, 4G, and 3G—a task complicated by the need to manage diverse technologies and vast customer bases. Traditionally, fault diagnosis was slow and manual, leading to delays and customer dissatisfaction. To address this, the CSP developed a platform integrating multiple service capabilities and orchestrating them using AI models. This platform automates intelligent fault diagnosis, identifying root causes and network locations efficiently. A “one-button diagnosis” feature simplifies troubleshooting by integrating BSS and OSS data, standardizing interfaces, and leveraging AI trained on extensive network data. Results include boosting first-call resolution from 80% to 91%, reducing fault processing time by 20 minutes, and increasing customer satisfaction from 93% to 97%, showcasing significant impact in network troubleshooting and self-healing.
> “Together, cloud and AI form the foundation of our scalable, intelligent, end-to-end autonomous network operations and innovation.”
> **Senior research executive** Asia-Pacific CSP
**Part three**
### Technology backbone
#### How to enable the autonomous network advantage
At the heart of the autonomous network transformation: elastic cloud infrastructure and AI-driven intelligence. Cloud supplies the ability to scale on demand. AI enables real-time insight and action. Together, they are the backbone that enables autonomy.
Our research shows that 57% of telecom executives see cloud and AI as playing a decisive role in accelerating their transition from basic automation to advanced autonomous networks. Yet looking at cloud and AI individually, there’s a striking perception divide. While 55% cite cloud infrastructure as critical, only one in three say the same about advanced generative AI (AI that creates new content) and agentic AI (AI systems that pursue goals with minimal human intervention).
This disconnect translates into an adoption gap in operational reality. CSPs have migrated nearly half (49%) of network operations to cloud environments, but only 19% have embedded AI in three or more network/OSS functions, where most closed loops sit today. These findings are consistent with our [2024 survey of global telecom executives](https://ibm.co/telecom-networks-cloud-ai) where we saw solid adoption of cloud but less use of AI in network operations. 
> “Although AI shows benefits, its full potential is still untapped. We are researching how to maximize AI’s value across broader scenarios, focusing on talent cultivation, computing resources, and tool development.”
> **Senior research executive** Asia-Pacific CSP
**The cloud-AI paradox** Cloud lays the groundwork, but AI remains a vast frontier for unleashing true autonomy in network operations.
An underlying reason for AI delays: infrastructure challenges. “CSP networks pour out metrics, but most aren’t filtered, aggregated, or correlated into insights that actually restore service or boost performance,” says Ericsson’s Keane. “Once you strip out noise and surface the telemetry that matters, the business case improves instantly….”
Executives acknowledge this data paradox—networks generate abundant telemetry, yet it remains trapped in silos with quality issues that block autonomous capabilities. “Not all teams currently possess high-quality, fit-for-purpose data for their domains, so some engineering and ETL/ELT is needed before real progress can be made,” notes Michael Hanson, Senior Director of Business Transformation, AI & Data, Verizon. Further complicating matters, the lack of standard agent-to-agent (A2A) interfaces—mechanisms that allow software agents to communicate and collaborate—prevents closed loop systems from sharing intents and telemetry across diverse networks and legacy components. 
What does an effective technology backbone look like? Once again, CSPs at Level 3 and 4 point the way:
  * **Deeper AI deployment.** 38% report embedding AI in three or more network functions versus just 11% of Level 0-2 CSPs.
  * **Commitment to cloud.** The share of Level 3 and 4 CSPs rating cloud infrastructure as critical to transitioning to autonomous networks was 60% greater than among Level 0-2 CSPs.
  * **Future-forward financing.** Leaders also invest 23% more on generative AI and 21% more on agentic AI, and they allocate 7% more of their cloud investments to autonomous network initiatives.


The next step for front-runners is to separate network software from proprietary hardware and publish intent-based open APIs across the radio, transport, and core layers, enabling closed-loop controllers to translate business intent directly into automated device configurations.
Achieving true network autonomy requires synchronized focus on AI maturity, cloud modernization, and data management. CSPs must convert streaming telemetry into governed data products and deploy an end-to-end observability fabric that ingests those assets into real-time, closed-loop assurance workflows. Telstra’s Knowledge Plane offers a live blueprint, notes Andersen. This intelligent reasoning layer dynamically derives actions at runtime.
But technology alone does not enable autonomy. Culture and trust are just as critical. “Mindset shift has been a critical part of this journey,” explains TELUS’ Chief Automation Architect, Ali Tizghadam. “In the end, explainability and transparency have been essential to shifting the way we think about designing and operating the network in a scalable, automated way.”
“Today we keep humans in the loop for decisions that aren’t yet deterministic,” notes Verizon’s Hanson. “Once telemetry proves an action consistently drives the right outcome, we move it to closed loop with monitoring.”
“Business and IT must work as a single, iterative team,” adds Ari Banerjee, Chief Strategy Officer at Netcracker Technology. “Many projects failed when IT moved first and business users weighed in too late, rejecting solutions that didn’t match their use cases.”
Also important: collaboration among CSPs, vendors, system integrators, hyperscalers, and industry forums when co-developing reusable closed-loop frameworks to avoid vendor lock-in and foster interoperability. “Until these bodies align on a common ‘iceberg’ of foundational, below-the-waterline standards and leave only the tip for differentiation, operators will keep reinventing integrations and delaying launches,” says Banerjee. 
**Action guide**
How to win in the autonomous network race
Network autonomy isn't just inevitable—the journey is already underway. Start with these three high-impact actions that can deliver progress quickly.
**01. Identify and mobilize around high-value scenarios.** Pinpoint three to five network domains with immediate autonomy potential by analyzing three key indicators: operational pain points (ticket volumes, MTTR statistics, outage frequencies), customer impact metrics (NPS detractors, churn triggers, service complaints), and operational costs by domain. Map current workflows with a cross-functional team that includes network specialists, data scientists, product owners, and business stakeholders across these domains. For each identified scenario, document the business case with both cost reduction and customer experience metrics, and establish clear success criteria that can be measured within 90 days. Create a multiyear OPEX rebalancing plan to reallocate spend gradually from legacy upkeep to these pilots, so each scenario has a measurable budget and executive support.
**02. Create your data nerve center.** Build a unified observability fabric that streams network telemetry from OSS/BSS, core, transport, performance counters, KPIs, and event traces into a centralized open (ODA-aligned) data lake. Expose those feeds through an end-to-end observability platform that drives machine learning-powered assurance workflows and AI-driven resolution assistants, enabling real-time anomaly detection and closed-loop remediation. Create a simple catalog of reusable data sets with clear owners and quality targets, so teams can safely reuse the same feeds across use cases. Modernize OSS/BSS into modular building blocks with open interfaces so automation can scale across the network.
**03. Cultivate an autonomous-first culture and partner model.** Align people, governance, and partnerships to support network autonomy. Develop in-house expertise through telecom-specific AI literacy programs that focus on practical applications such as using machine learning for spectrum optimization, predictive maintenance of cell towers, and automated configuration of virtual network functions. Establish governance leadership by creating a cross-functional council responsible for the roadmap, funding cadence, KPIs, and a trust index that tracks model drift and explainability. Team with leading systems integrators and cloud providers under shared SLAs and run joint sandbox environments to co-develop unified, intent-based automation across RAN, transport, core, and OSS/BSS. 
> “Early successes in autonomy are promising, but they risk diverting focus from building the foundational layers—consistent data models, governance, and architectural discipline—required for true Level 4 scale.”
> **Kim Krogh Andersen** Product and Technology Group Executive, Telstra
