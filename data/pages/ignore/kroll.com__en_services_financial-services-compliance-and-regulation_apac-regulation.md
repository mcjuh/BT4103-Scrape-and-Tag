What should a fund manager consider when establishing business operations in Singapore?
A fund manager seeking to establish a fund management business in Singapore will generally need to obtain a Capital Markets Services (CMS) licence for fund management from the MAS, unless an exemption applies.
Key considerations include:
  * Substantive fund management activities MAS expects licensed fund managers to conduct substantive fund management activities in Singapore to qualify for grant of licence.
  * Personnel requirements Fund managers are generally required to have at least two Singapore-based individuals who collectively satisfy MAS's minimum competency and experience requirements set out in Appendix 1 of the Guidelines on Licensing and Conduct of Fund Management Companies. Where any criteria cannot be collectively fulfilled, additional personnel may be required.
  * Capital requirements The fund management company must meet the applicable regulatory capital requirements set out in the First Schedule of the Securities and Futures (Financial and Margin Requirements for Holders of Capital Markets Services Licences) Regulations. For fund managers serving only accredited and institutional investors, the minimum base capital requirement is S$250,000.
  * Local incorporation and office premises The company should be incorporated in Singapore and maintain a dedicated physical office in Singapore that is secure, appropriately resourced, and used exclusively for the company's operations.
  * Application fees The estimated upfront fees would include a one-time fee of S$1,000 for the application submission, along with S$200 for each representative appointed. In addition to these requirements, fund managers should consider their governance framework, compliance and risk management arrangements, operational infrastructure, and ongoing regulatory reporting obligations before commencing operations in Singapore.


Note: Licensing requirements may vary depending on the nature and scope of the fund management activities.
