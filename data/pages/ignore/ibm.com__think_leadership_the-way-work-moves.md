##  Your people are the platform 
AI can transform how a company works—if the company is actually ready for it. And that readiness depends as much on people as it does on platforms. It’s tempting to assume culture will snap into place after a gen AI rollout. It won’t. People are what make technology initiatives thrive or stall, and they have to be prepared for change. Can your enterprise absorb new ways of working and scale them meaningfully? Do your talent models and incentive structures reflect the pace of change? These aren’t soft questions. They’re the ones that determine whether your AI strategy holds.

##  When the path forward isn’t clear 
The patterns here are familiar, but hard to see from the top—precisely where vision needs to originate. A team might be rewarded for a local win that increases friction across the system. A short-term fix can quietly become the new baseline if no one’s looking out for systemic solutions. Most leaders agree that bold, aligned culture is critical to AI success. But few believe their organization operates that way. There’s a reason for the gap. The holistic redesign AI demands can feel like breaking systems that still work when you’ve spent years shoring up against market volatility. Staying the course seems practical in the near-term but the change ahead is seismic.
### Transformation needs an operating model
There’s meaningful distance between a successful pilot and a scalable solution. Without a framework to connect disciplines, transformation runs on individual willpower and heroics have a shelf life. As pressure builds, the impact compounds. Progress increasingly depends on a shrinking set of people carrying more load. Workarounds proliferate. Effort disconnects from results, morale slips and costs surface everywhere—in rework, attrition and a growing gap between investment and return. This is how pilot failure becomes systemic—through cultural and structural friction that builds long before anyone names it.

### Designing institutional rhythm
Scaling AI means redesigning how work moves, and backing that redesign with a clear mandate. Talent often wants to innovate and is frequently ahead of leadership on tool adoption, so this isn’t just about top-down directive. Incentives should reward experimentation and outcomes, not just baseline delivery. Governance must balance central coherence with localized flexibility. Research supports the performance gains these shifts produce. Through our work with clients, we’ve learned what it takes to reimagine roles, workflows and accountability together. Sometimes the most useful thing we can offer is the perspective that comes with having gone through it first.
How to get ahead 
##  How to get ahead 
Incentivize the behavior your enterprise actually needs 
Narrow KPIs and expansive strategies don’t coexist. If you want cross-functional thinking, measure and reward cross-functional outcomes. 
Invest in your people as seriously as you do in your platforms 
77% of entry-level workers will see their roles shift because of AI, and so will more than 1 in 4 senior executives.1 Adoption accelerates when people feel supported. When they don’t, unofficial AI use increases alongside compliance risk. 
Visibility at the top makes change credible 
When leaders model change through action—using AI tools, challenging assumptions and making hard calls transparently—it makes transformation is real. This matters most for the middle managers and cross-functional leads who carry the weight of change every day.
