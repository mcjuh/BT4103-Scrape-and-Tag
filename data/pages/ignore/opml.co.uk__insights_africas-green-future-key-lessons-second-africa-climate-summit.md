On 8-10 September 2025, the [Second Africa Climate Summit (ACS2)](https://africaclimatesummit2.et/) brought together thought leaders, policymakers, and climate experts to tackle some of the most pressing climate issues facing the continent.
At the summit, [Michael Adenew](https://www.opml.co.uk/people/michael-adenew), Country Director for our Ethiopia office, participated in a panel discussion at the CIFF (Children's Investment Fund Foundation) Pavilion. The panel tackled a defining challenge: How do we unlock climate finance that works for Africa?
_Photo: Panellists at the CIFF Pavilion_
One clear message emerged from the panel discussion: Africa is not short on innovation; it is the systems and structures required to scale these innovations that are lacking.
To move from potential to tangible impact, experts highlighted the need for four key elements:
  1. **National ownership and strategic plans** : Governments must take charge with clear, bankable strategies that inspire confidence among investors, Multilateral Development Banks (MDBs), and Development Finance Institutions (DFIs).
  2. **Blended, de-risked financial instruments** : Tailored finance solutions that reduce risk and are adapted to the unique contexts of African countries are essential to encourage investment.
  3. **Local capacity and institutions** : To ensure funding translates into long-term resilience, local capacity, technology, and institutions must be strengthened.
  4. **Shift from pledges to outcomes** : Climate finance must focus on delivering tangible results, not just tracking inputs.


_Photo: Michael Adenew, Country Director – OPM Ethiopia, speaking at the panel_ A defining moment of the summit was the release of the [**Addis Ababa Declaration**](https://africaclimatesummit2.et/news-detail/Addis-Ababa-Declaration-officially-and-Successfully-Adopted), which outlined a bold commitment to mobilise $50 billion annually for climate finance. While the ambition is significant, the success of this plan hinges on developing the right tools to transform capital into actionable, implementable projects on the ground.The Declaration stressed the importance of climate finance being:
  * **Just** : Ensuring that investments avoid debt traps and promote sustainable, long-term growth.
  * **Accessible** : Reaching local governments, innovators, and communities on the frontlines of climate change.
  * **Transparent** : Built on solid data, institutional readiness, and real-time accountability to maintain trust and efficiency.


The summit also witnessed the inauguration of the **Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam (GERD)** , which served as a powerful symbol of Africa's potential to deliver transformative, sovereign-led green infrastructure. The project underscores the potential for regional cooperation and the long-term development benefits that such initiatives can bring.
The key takeaway from the ACS2 summit was clear: Climate finance must be rooted in the priorities of individual African nations, delivered through strong, capable institutions, and measured by its impact. Only then can the finance be effective, just, and scalable, driving meaningful change across the continent.
