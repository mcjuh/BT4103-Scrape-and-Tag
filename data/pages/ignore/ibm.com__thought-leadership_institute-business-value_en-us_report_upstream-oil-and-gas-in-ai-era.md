# Upstream oil and gas in the AI era
Upstream AI works within workflows, yet system-level impact stays limited. Redesigning operations, not adding use cases, unlocks scale.

### Why AI isn't scaling—and how to fix it
Artificial intelligence is delivering real, measurable value across upstream oil and gas operations—yet most organizations are struggling to scale that value beyond isolated workflows. This study reveals a critical disconnect: AI works, but the operating models that upstream companies rely on were never designed to support the coordinated, system-level execution that AI requires. 
### Strong conviction, limited system-level impact
Executive confidence in AI is high. 93% of upstream oil and gas leaders expect AI to deliver measurable competitive advantage within three years, and AI investment is climbing—from 9% of IT budgets today toward 18% by 2029. Yet most organizations have not translated that conviction into enterprise-wide performance. Deployments remain concentrated in individual workflows and proof-of-concepts rather than end-to-end operational processes, limiting the ability to capture coordinated benefits across the value chain. 
**Strong executive conviction in AI-driven competitive advantage**
The value AI creates today is real but bounded. Organizations report meaningful operational gains, including a 12% reduction in drilling non-productive time, 9% shorter well delivery cycle times, and 16% fewer safety incidents disrupting operations. These wins are concentrated in areas with repeatable workflows, clear decision boundaries, and high data availability—precisely where AI can be embedded with limited organizational change. As a result, most value aligns with the traditional upstream model of discipline-level optimization. 
### The AI coordination gap
There is a clear "AI coordination gap." When AI is deployed within existing workflows, it improves individual parts of the system but doesn't fundamentally change how those parts operate together. Gains in drilling or production don't reliably translate into system-level performance improvements.
> “The leaders that succeed will not simply adopt AI. They will redesign how their operations work.” 
> **Zahid Habib** Global Energy and Resources Industry Leader, IBM Consulting
The root cause is structural. Upstream operating models evolved to optimize individual disciplines—subsurface, drilling, and production—each with its own data, workflows, and decision processes. That approach delivered strong results within functions but wasn't built for coordinated execution across the enterprise.
Three reinforcing constraints define the challenge:
  * 57% of organizations cite data fragmentation as a major barrier to scaling AI. 
  * Only 9% report real-time integration between operational technology (OT) and enterprise IT systems. 
  * Just 12% have achieved enterprise-level data integration across upstream operations.


These limitations become most acute in advanced applications—closed-loop drilling, autonomous production, and integrity risk prevention—where most organizations remain stuck in pilot or evaluation stages with agentic AI. AI today remains largely advisory, with only 5% of organizations allowing it to execute high-risk actions under defined guardrails. 
### Redesigning how upstream operations work
Scaling AI requires structural, not incremental, change. Organizations must shift from asset-level optimization to cross-asset coordination, from siloed data to a unified operational data layer, from pilot-based AI to embedded operational AI, and from advisory analytics to execution-linked AI.
The report outlines six fundamental changes leaders must make:
  1. **Design** end-to-end workflows around decisions that span reservoir, drilling, and production—rather than launching disconnected AI initiatives.
  2. **Connect** subsurface, drilling, and production data at enterprise scale through a unified operational data layer across OT, engineering, and enterprise systems.
  3. **Define** decision rights across field, engineering, and operations teams, with clear guardrails and accountability.
  4. **Embed** AI into live field and remote operations, moving from project-based pilots to continuous operational capability supported by industrial digital workers.
  5. **Standardize** operating practices across fields and assets to enable reuse of models, workflows, and playbooks.
  6. **Build** a workforce aligned to AI-enabled operations, focused on workforce orchestration rather than replacement.


### The path forward
AI is not failing in upstream oil and gas—the ability to scale it is. The constraint isn't a lack of use cases, investment, or technical capability. It's a mismatch between how operations are organized and what's needed to operate intelligently at scale. The leaders who succeed won't simply adopt AI. They'll redesign how their operations work, extending AI beyond localized gains to enterprise-scale impact across production, reliability, safety, and capital efficiency.
