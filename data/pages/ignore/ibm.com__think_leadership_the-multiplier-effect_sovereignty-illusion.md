#  The sovereignty illusion: Why most enterprises don’t control what they think they do 
Nearly every executive recognizes the value of controlling their AI. But far fewer have the ability to use technology on their own terms
#### The executive brief
  * Many C-suite leaders believe they control their AI. But risks lurk beneath the surface.
  * The issue isn’t just technical; rising costs, geopolitical pressures and vendor lock-in create urgency.
  * The solution is redesigning platforms and prioritizing critical AI systems.


##  The illusion 
[Digital sovereignty](https://www.ibm.com/solutions/digital-sovereignty) is the ability to control your own digital destiny. Many executives believe they’ve effectively handled it.
But across today’s AI-driven enterprise, control is fragmenting.
Data may sit in one jurisdiction, models may be trained in another, and orchestration layers may operate somewhere else entirely. What looks controlled on paper often behaves very differently in practice. Geopolitical tensions, an expanding regulatory climate and the race for AI only add to the pressure.
### _"The CXOs I work with are worried. They want sovereignty—but they don’t have visibility into what’s really happening underneath.”_
**Sandeep Chanda,** Partner, CTO, and Global Data Leader, IBM Consulting
It’s no longer just a technical issue. It’s showing up in rising costs, reduced flexibility, regulatory exposure and diminished leverage over vendors. Organizations that assume control may be making high-stakes decisions without fully understanding their dependencies.
[AI in motion](https://www-api.ibm.com/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:16fbf42e-18b3-4c3e-89b5-6096f8e41fd4/original/as/ai-in-motion.pdf) research from the IBM Institute for Business Value found that 93% of global executives say sovereignty now shapes their AI strategy. And yet less than a third know what AI their teams are using or where that technology operates. Only 18% keep an up-to-date AI inventory.
The real question for the C-suite is simple: Do you actually control your AI stack … or just believe you do?
##  Why it's happening 
AI hasn’t only changed the pace of disruption. It has shifted the nature of organizations’ dependencies on external suppliers or service providers. The enterprise technology playbook that worked for decades no longer applies.
In AI, dependencies go beyond predictable infrastructure and applications. The AI model and the services around it can be updated with little warning, creating fresh types of volatility. Organizations that overlook how dependencies have evolved are laboring under the sovereignty illusion.
“AI has introduced new forms of dependency that evolve faster than traditional governance, procurement or technology cycles were designed to handle,” said Ana Paula Assis, IBM Senior Vice President and Chair, EMEA and APAC, in the foreword for the IBM Institute for Business Value’s new [The Calculus of AI Sovereignty study](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-06-17-ibm-study-limited-control-and-rising-dependencies-leave-enterprises-exposed-in-the-age-of-ai). “That is why AI sovereignty has become one of the most defining leadership issues of this moment.”
Vendor lock-in—when a company becomes reliant on a particular provider—is one sovereignty issue affecting organizations as they adopt the next generation of AI technologies.
[IBM research](https://www.ibm.com/thought-leadership/institute-business-value/report/ai-sovereignty) shows that only 9% of global executives report an excellent understanding of their dependencies on AI vendors, models and infrastructures. 71% say switching their primary AI vendor or model would be difficult if they had to do it today.
Once locked in, organizations lose important leverage. “The most critical element of sovereignty is portability,” Chanda said. “And often organizations ignore it.”
Another contributor to the sovereignty illusion: organizations too often take an ad hoc approach to AI. This heightens the risk that information at the model level will leak.
“Many people are using AI as a bolt-on,” Chanda explained. “But then there is data behind these AI systems, which needs to be sovereign as well. Putting boundaries on where AI-relevant data travels is a big gap today.”
Closing that gap is what turns sovereignty from an illusion into reality.
##  Why it matters now 
Regulatory pressures and geopolitical fragmentation have caused the sovereignty illusion to escalate into a boardroom concern.
Geopolitical activity around control of data shows no sign of slowing down. [Gartner research](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-09-09-gartner-reveals-top-technologies-shaping-government-ai-adoption) predicts that 65% of governments worldwide will introduce some technological sovereignty requirements by 2028. The anticipated rules will aim at bolstering countries’ sovereignty while protecting them from interference by outside regulators, Gartner reports.
An enterprise operating a customer-facing model in Europe faces one set of requirements. An enterprise in Saudi Arabia confronts yet more stringent restrictions. In both cases, the enterprises must ensure that the data isn’t routed through a less restrictive jurisdiction, such as the United States—which is where many vendors are based.
“Your data might be in-region,” Chanda said. “But the model that is training on it could be somewhere else. Do you really know what’s happening and do you truly own that data?”

Compliance-driven data routing choices carry a computing cost. Already, the impact from the sovereignty illusion is showing up on corporate balance sheets.
Organizations with the greatest control over their AI stack protect [55%](https://www.ibm.com/thought-leadership/institute-business-value/report/ai-sovereignty) more operating profit from AI-driven disruptions than those operating with less control, the latest findings say. [Executives report](https://www.ibm.com/thought-leadership/institute-business-value/report/ai-sovereignty) that over the past 24 months, AI dependencies have caused price increases, usage restrictions, model phase-outs and other forms of volatility.
A misaligned platform strategy isn’t cheap. Organizations in [The Calculus of AI Sovereignty study](https://www.ibm.com/thought-leadership/institute-business-value/report/ai-sovereignty) pay nearly 3 times more in token processing when data location is out of line with model execution. What does that mean? For a USD 20 billion enterprise, AI that runs far from the data it relies on results in about an extra USD 50 million for the same AI capabilities.
The consequences of paying attention to digital sovereignty—or failing to do so—look set to increase rapidly.

##  What leading firms do differently 
Leading enterprises are moving beyond compliance toward operational control.
They’re redesigning platforms to increase:
  * Visibility into AI systems and data flows
  * Portability across models, vendors and environments
  * Interoperability through open standards


They’re also introducing abstraction layers to reduce dependency on any single provider.
“Interoperability has to be a top priority,” Chanda said. “You need to ensure you own not just your core data—but everything your AI systems generate.”
At the same time, organizations are rebalancing where workloads live, bringing critical systems closer to environments they can control, while still using cloud flexibility where it makes sense.
Most importantly, they’re adopting selective sovereignty.
“Not every system needs the same level of control,” Chanda explained. “The key is deciding where control really matters—and prioritizing accordingly.”
Leading firms segment their environments:
  * Tier 1: Mission-critical and regulated systems—maximum control
  * Tier 2: Important capabilities—balanced control
  * Tier 3: Commodity services—managed dependency


This aligns control with business value, protecting what matters most without slowing innovation.
For an example of sovereignty in an open hybrid cloud environment, look at IBM’s partnership with semiconductor company AMD. The two companies are collaborating on an AI-oriented sovereignty software platform that helps organizations implement continuous sovereignty controls across infrastructure, data, workloads, and operations.
Organizations are responding to the need for greater control over their AI. [Gartner research](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/5863580) forecasts that sovereign cloud investment will nearly quintuple to USD169 billion by 2028.
“Sovereignty and hybrid cloud go together,” said Chanda. “It almost feels like a full circle that the enterprises are starting to recognize now.”

##  What to do next 
For C-suite leaders, the moment for a new approach to sovereignty could hardly have come too soon.
Many executives have been burned too often by AI proofs of concept (POCs) that failed to deliver, according to Chanda. “They get very excited about a successful AI proof of concept,” he said. “But scaling it across a fragmented architecture is where the value realization slows to a trickle.”
After all those failed POCs, Chanda explains, leaders understand the need to move beyond compliance to engineering verifiable, real-time control into the business architecture itself.
To pierce the sovereignty illusion, C-suite leaders and board members are shifting from passive governance to active enforcement. This phased roadmap outlines the immediate, mid-term and long-term mandates for key enterprise stakeholders.
##  CEO's 
**The Mandate:** Set and enforce the enterprise’s strategy on selective sovereignty.  
| **In the next 30–60 days**  | **In the next 60–120 days**  | **Longer term**  |  
| --- | --- | --- |  
| Sign off on the organization’s three AI sovereignty tiers. Require Tier 1 owners to outline their system’s key dependencies.  | Mandate an AI outage simulation. Require immediate remediation plans and link executive goals directly to these resilience benchmarks.  | Audit executive understanding of Tier 1 exposure continuously and validate real optionality for critical systems.  |  
##  External Boards 
**The Mandate:** Enforce discipline by confirming that management is limiting structural exposure.  
| **In the next 30–60 days**  | **In the next 60–120 days**  | **Longer term**  |  
| --- | --- | --- |  
| Request a Tier 1 dependency map and documented evidence of switching capability.  | Appoint an enterprise owner for AI resilience and require regular trend reporting.  | Watch for quarterly drops in exposure metrics and decreasing system switching times.  |  
##  CFO's 
**The Mandate:** Allocate capital optionality, treating it like insurance.  
| **In the next 30–60 days**  | **In the next 60–120 days**  | **Longer term**  |  
| --- | --- | --- |  
| Require comprehensive cost-to-switch and time-to-switch reports for Tier 1 systems. Factor a control premium into high-stakes AI investments.  | Confirm that all contracts include enforceable portability guarantees and track switching metrics. Explore alternative strategies for any Tier 1 systems facing acute vendor lock-in.  | Make sure funding flows to the highest-impact investments first, optimizing for both flexibility and cost.  |

|  Map all Tier 1 AI dependencies and pinpoint where open-source models can reduce lock-in.
