# Tax credits power the shift to a circular economy
July 27, 2026 
As companies race to align innovation with environmental, social, and governance (ESG) priorities, a familiar tool is being reconsidered through a new lens. The federal research credit, long viewed as a mechanism for incentivizing technical advancement, is emerging as a potential bridge to one of today’s most influential sustainability frameworks: the circular economy.
In “Bridging Research Credits and the Circular Economy” published in _Tax Notes_ , Grant Thornton’s Abbie Therrow, Dennis St. Martin, Monica Bambury and Kevin Benton explore how these two concepts, often treated separately, are increasingly intertwined in practice.
The circular economy challenges the traditional “take, make, dispose” model by emphasizing reuse, regeneration and waste reduction. At the same time, businesses are under growing pressure to demonstrate both financial performance and environmental responsibility. This convergence has tangible implications for how companies design products, invest in research, and report outcomes.
From material science breakthroughs and recycling technologies to advanced manufacturing processes and data-driven supply chains, many activities central to circularity may also qualify for research credits. Yet the alignment is far from automatic. Complex qualification rules, evolving case law and heightened scrutiny from tax authorities introduce both opportunity and risk.
By examining regulatory frameworks, real-world case examples and emerging business practices, the _Tax Notes_ article reveals how companies can strategically connect innovation, tax planning, and sustainability.
