# Clayton Donahue
Senior Vice President, Data Platform & Infrastructure 
Clayton Donahue is the Senior Vice President of Data Platform and Infrastructure at Chartis. As SVP, he leads the firm’s data platform initiatives, building the foundational infrastructure and services necessary to advance Chartis’ AI capabilities and leverage the firm’s unique data assets. Clayton also serves as the firm’s HIPAA Privacy Officer. He has more than 20 years of experience designing, architecting, and developing data-driven analytics and applications, and is well-versed in agile development approaches and modern analytic and data stacks—particularly Snowflake, Python, dbt, Microsoft Azure, and Tableau. 
Prior to his role with Chartis, Clayton led engineering at iVantage Health Analytics, a healthcare performance improvement firm. Clayton served as the organization’s VP of Engineering, then later as CTO. At iVantage, he led the re-platforming of the iVantage Performance Manager application, overseeing technology operations for the engineering team and offshore development partners. 
Clayton holds a Bachelor of Arts from Colby College, where he majored in Computer Science with a minor in Russian Language and Literature.

###  Why healthcare? 
Because healthcare is a worthy cause. Because it’s personal. Because it needs help. Because it inspires the best in us. There are many answers to this question that ring equally true. For me, what started as circumstance—Boston is a terrific nexus of healthcare and technology—has evolved into a deeper understanding of the workings and needs of the healthcare industry. 
###  What inspires you? 
Simply put: Mountains. As an avid hiker, I’ve always been fascinated by the White Mountains of New Hampshire. There’s a mix of challenge, solitude, and space that always seems to bring a degree of relativity to the world. A mountainous landscape provides a grounding counterpoint to the ever-changing, fast-moving world of technology, and never ceases to inject a good measure of humility into my life. 
###  Do you have a motto or personal mantra? 
Plant more trees. I find it important to remind myself to take actions—even little ones—that won’t pay dividends until twenty, forty, or even one hundred years later. We may not see the fruits of these actions, but the near-term change we perceive often starts from small steps set in motion years ago. So, I’ve committed to begin planting the seeds for those outcomes whenever possible, whether it’s tending to a relationship, supporting a cause, or learning a new skill. Planting that one acorn in the yard.
