OUR PEOPLE
# Brett Pederson
Senior Partner 
Brett Pederson is a Senior Partner in Strategic Transformation and co-leads the Strategic Planning practice. Brett has 20 years of experience in strategic and financial healthcare consulting and specializes in enterprise strategy, academic medicine, and innovative partnerships/mergers and acquisitions. He has led engagements in the areas of enterprise strategy, mergers and acquisitions, organizational and physician/faculty enterprise design, service line strategy, academic funds flow design, new business development, and capital planning for many of the nation’s largest integrated delivery networks, leading academic medical centers, children’s hospitals, and community hospitals.
Brett has been published and has spoken at healthcare conferences across the country on topics including: building a winning playbook to guide health system partnership strategy, innovative approaches to commercial value-based contracting in pediatrics, the pace and future of consolidation in healthcare, the future of academic faculty in an era of provider consolidation, competitive positioning for children’s hospitals in response to Integrated Delivery Network convergence on pediatrics, and innovative approaches to addressing the behavioral health crisis in hospital emergency departments. 
Brett earned his Master of Business Administration from The University of Minnesota Carlson School of Management and a Bachelor of Arts in communication studies and philosophy from Gustavus Adolphus College.

###  Why did you pick Chartis? 
The people. Chartis attracts a certain type of professional, and some of the things that stood out to me (and continue to stand out) are: the collegiality and genuine concern for the development and wellbeing of colleagues, the drive toward leading edge and innovative thinking, the enduring commitment to improving healthcare, and the intentionality behind our culture. 
###  What have you found to be the most rewarding part of your career? 
This career creates compelling opportunities for impact—and seeing the impact we can have is the most rewarding part of it for me. It can take many forms: a patient may be healed or have a better experience because of the ideas our team brought to bear, a health system may be able to better serve its community because of the work we supported, or I may, in some small way, help a less experienced colleague develop into a next generation leader, extending their sphere of influence and achieving their goals. 
###  When you’re not working, what would we find you doing? 
Spending time with my two wonderful children, Crew and Collins, or sneaking away to play golf.
