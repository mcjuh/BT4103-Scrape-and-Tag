# ERM advises UK government on interventions to accelerate low-carbon innovation across key sectors 
23 July 2026
Share this page:
ERM, alongside partners Eigen Ventures, Scope 3 Peer Group and SRS Sustainable Business, has undertaken research funded by UK Research and Innovation (UKRI) and the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero (DESNZ), to identify potential market interventions to accelerate adoption of low-carbon and resource-efficient innovations across the UK’s automotive, built environment and chemicals sectors.
### **Unlocking net-zero progress by strengthening demand for innovation**
The UK has a strong pipeline of low‑carbon and resource‑efficient innovations and businesses, yet many struggle to scale beyond early deployment. Commercialization is held back by perceived investment and performance risks, fragmented value chains, and supply constraints, driving up cost premiums and weakening demand. 
These barriers limit private capital mobilization and slow progress towards net zero and circular economy goals. As a result, even technically mature and potentially viable innovations can fail to achieve widespread market adoption without targeted intervention.
Through this project, ERM and its partners identified priority innovations with significant potential to deliver lifecycle emissions reductions and improve resource efficiency, but which face persistent demand‑side barriers. Drawing on extensive stakeholder engagement and the [MFI Toolkit](https://www.erm.com/globalassets/news/market-forming-interventions-toolkit2.pdf), the team developed a series of interventions to help drive market uptake across each sector. While grounded in the specific sectors and geographies assessed, the MFI Toolkit provides a structured, transferable approach that can be applied to address similar demand‑side challenges in other markets globally.
Stafford Lloyd, Innovation Lead at UKRI said “Scaling innovation requires more than derisking technology – it requires markets that are ready to deploy. These interventions demonstrate how unlocking demand can accelerate the clean energy transition while strengthening UK supply chains and economic growth. The MFI Toolkit provides a practical framework to help policymakers and industry design and implement effective demand-side interventions, supporting the scale-up and integration of low-carbon solutions across key sectors.”
Richard Platt, Partner at ERM said: “We welcomed the opportunity to work with UKRI and DESNZ to research practical interventions that could advance low-carbon innovations, address market barriers unlocking investment, accelerating adoption, and strengthening the UK’s transition to a competitive, resilient, and low-carbon economy.”

**About Eigen Ventures**
Eigen Ventures are specialists in designing and delivering innovation acceleration programmes and business support services for clean tech and resource efficiency innovators. **About the Scope 3 Peer Group**
The Scope 3 Peer Group is a cross-industry, global collective of 3,400 sustainability and procurement practitioners tasked with supply chain emissions reduction. We host peer exchanges on best practices, relevant tools and facilitate collective action to drive faster, non-duplicative and credible impact. We also have Nature & Biodiversity and Human Rights & Workforce Resilience Peer Groups. We are a group of “those who do”, not “those who say”.

**About Innovate UK**
Innovate UK, part of UK Research and Innovation, is the UK’s innovation agency. It works to create a better future by inspiring, involving and investing in businesses developing life-changing innovations. Its mission is to help companies to grow through their development and commercialisation of new products, processes and services, supported by an outstanding innovation ecosystem that is agile, inclusive and easy to navigate.
**About DESNZ**
DESNZ is responsible for leading the government mission to achieve clean power by 2030 and accelerate to net zero. This government is committed to transitioning the UK to a circular economy. This is an ambitious goal, requiring major changes throughout the economy. Getting the transition right will generate major contributions to the Government’s Missions to kickstart economic growth and to make Britain a clean energy superpower, including accelerating to Net Zero.
