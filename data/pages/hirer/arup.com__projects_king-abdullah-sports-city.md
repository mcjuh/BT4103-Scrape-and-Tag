Saudi Arabia’s King Abdullah Sports City Stadium is an elegant, respectful and versatile sports complex that combines traditional Islamic architecture with innovative design to form a highly functional, sustainable and flexible building. 
Reflecting its location in two ways, the stadiumalso known as the Alinma Stadiumds both to the geology and climate of Jeddah and to the cultural context of a devout Muslim society. brought together architecture and engineering to translate these complex requirements into the stadium’s distinctive design.
Home to two Saudi Pro League football teams based in Jeddah, AL Ittihad and Al Ahli, the 60,000-seat stadium is far larger than any other in the country. It not only facilitates larger crowds, but also enables Saudi Arabia to host major events such as the 2027 AFC Asian Cup and the 2034 FIFA World Cup. The stadium therefore has been designed to comply with FIFA regulations, the Green Guide and the International Building Code.
## A sports complex encouraging participation at every level 
As well as providing a place to watch sport, the project aims to inspire wider participation in physical activity through state-of-the-art facilities. In addition to the stadium, the Sports City includes a 10,000-seat indoor sports hall that can be reconfigured for a wide range of sports, a 1,000-seat outdoor athletics stadium and track, three football pitches, four five-a-side pitches, six tennis courts, and athletics warm-up facilities. 
## Applying our sports architecture expertise 
Our architects used geometric patterns, a rich part of Islamic culture, to help define the stadium’s look. We drew on the tradition of openwork mashrabiya screens, providing privacy and keeping the sun out while still permitting ventilation. In a climate that is extremely hot for much of the year, getting the right approach to shading and ventilation was crucial.
The designers wanted to provide as much cover as possible to spectators, but not to shade the grass. Bermuda-type grass was used, the best of its kind to survive in the hot conditions.

## A stadium shaped by geometry and climate 
To achieve excellent sightlines throughout the bowl, while maintaining comfort through natural ventilation for much of the year, we developed an angled layout on the lower levels that becomes rounded towards the top. This solution provides a combination of intimacy at the lower tiers with excellent views across the stadium. 
The roof is formed from cantilevered trusses, creating a series of isosceles triangles that reference the tessellation of traditional Islamic designs. The building includes PTFE insertions, referring to the Bedouin tradition. This aesthetic and functional choice contributes to the stadium’s distinctive character.
The cladding plays a vital part in ventilation, allowing air to be drawn in and, as it heats up, rise and exit the bowl. This was carefully modelled to provide sufficient airflow during stadium use. Almost all matches are played at night when the perforations also allow light to escape through the façade.
Situated at the edge of Jeddah, the Sports City is in an area near the airport that is developing rapidly and easily accessible.
The 10,000-seat indoor arena is the largest of its type in the country and is designed to be used at all levels up to international competition.
It is flexible and can be reconfigured for a wide range of sports, cultural events and exhibitions.
## A jewel in the desert 
Combining innovative design with a strong response to climate, culture and tradition, King Abdullah Sports City has strengthened Saudi Arabia’s ability to host major sporting events while creating a destination for both elite competition and community sport. With its faceted geometry, formalist setting and harsh environment, it has been described as a ‘Jewel in the desert’.
