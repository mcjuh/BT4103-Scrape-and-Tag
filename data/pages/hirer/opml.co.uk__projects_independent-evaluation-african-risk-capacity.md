The African Risk Capacity (ARC) is an innovative African Union initiative which combines early warning, disaster risk management, and risk finance to create an African-owned disaster response system across the continent, which enables African governments to better meet the needs of people at risk of disasters. 
Oxford Policy Management (OPM) conducted an independent evaluation of ARC over the period 2015–2026, financed largely by the UK’s Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO). This work assessed the development of ARC as an organisation, the uptake of ARC products and services, and the impact ARC has in reducing vulnerability to disasters across Africa. The evaluation was steered by an Evaluation Steering Group and responded to the collective priorities of all stakeholders represented there. 
## ARC
The frequency and severity of disasters globally, and drought in Africa specifically, is intensifying. Against this backdrop, African governments require support in their disaster risk planning and financing to ensure that timely support reaches affected populations before they engage in negative coping strategies, such as the sale of assets.
ARC provides support to its member states in planning for climate-related disasters and index-based weather risk insurance based on a pooled regional insurance model. ARC is unique amongst the regional risk pools in requiring countries to have effective contingency plans before agreeing to provide insurance, and in learning from payouts made, and thus has great potential to improve the ability of governments to better anticipate, plan, and respond to disaster risk. Ultimately, vulnerable households should be more resilient to disasters through the receipt of timely support. 
## Our approach
Our evaluation supported lesson learning and improvements in effectiveness, helping to strengthen ARC and member states. It also informed the broader disaster risk financing sector, including other regional risk pools, and the global evidence base.
The focus of the evaluation was on ARC’s role in enabling a timely response following an extreme climate event such as drought before the livelihood situation of affected households becomes critical. It assessed the value of support to contingency planning, early responses and risk pooling to ensure rapid responses to drought and other extreme weather events. It was a theory-based evaluation, that used contribution analysis as our primary analytical framework, examining progress against the Theory of Change (ToC) from inputs to impact. This allowed us to identify both progress and any critical shortcomings, not only where ARC may or may not be achieving certain objectives (a critical assessment of performance), but where the ToC itself may or may not be holding (a critical assessment of strategy). 
The evaluation made use of country case studies, organisational reviews, global reviews and a wide range of other analysis to address the evaluation questions.
## Evaluation outputs 
We undertook two formative evaluations, to identify and share lessons learnt with the management of ARC and other stakeholders, as well as two impact assessments (one overall and a pilot in Senegal), which assessed the contribution of ARC’s support to improved disaster response. It focused on countries that had a payout from ARC in the period 2020–2023. Key findings are summarised in the briefing notes below accompanied by ARC management and FCDO responses.
