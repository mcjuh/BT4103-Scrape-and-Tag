Pakistan hosts over a million Afghans without a formal national refugee protection framework, creating conditions shaped by a fragile balance of humanitarian norms and immigration enforcement. With the Government of Pakistan’s announcement of the Illegal Foreign Refugee Plan (IFRP) in 2023 and the subsequent Special Regulatory Order (SRO) 2025, the refugee protection landscape has shifted as the large-scale deportation drive now restricts mobility and access to services in Pakistan. Afghan nationals in Pakistan, especially women and other vulnerable groups, now face heightened protection and livelihood risks.
The UNHCR programme was originally conceptualised to leverage the Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus, aiming to safeguard refugees, reduce burden on host communities, and empower refugees to achieve self-reliance. However, in the current contracting protection environment for refugees, a shift is needed from long-term integration towards survival, harm reduction, and resilience.
Our evaluation assesses how effectively UNHCR and its partners have adapted to this changing context; examining relevance, efficiency, and coherence of the programme. By generating evidence and practical recommendations, the evaluation helps refine strategy and strengthen protection and solutions for refugees in the country.
### Challenges
Afghan refugees and stateless persons in Pakistan face a complex set of protection, social, and economic challenges. They rely on temporary or de facto protection frameworks, leaving them vulnerable to policy shifts, enforcement campaigns, and uncertainty about their legal status. The implementation of the IFRP and SRO have further restricted mobility and access to services, generating fear and anxiety among refugees.The programme is operating in a precarious operational and political environment. While coordination with government counterparts and implementing partners is on-going, shifting enforcement priorities and limited humanitarian space intensify difficulties. The shrinking of international aid has added additional strain to the programme’s ability to carry out protection and advocacy efforts. Many refugees, whose informal livelihoods have been disrupted by restricted movement and limited access to markets, are confronted with additional economic vulnerabilities. These losses are further exacerbated by the loss or damage to limited assets endured during forced relocations and evictions. Women, children, and high-risk groups face compounded risks given their already existing vulnerabilities.
### Expertise
The evaluation applies a rigorous, theory-based, and utilisation-focused mixed-methods approach, grounded in UNHCR Evaluation Policy, UNEG Norms and Standards, and core humanitarian principles. It is designed to assess the project’s relevance, coherence, and efficiency in a context where protection space for Afghan refugees is shrinking.The evaluation integrates qualitative and quantitative methods to capture the perspectives of diverse stakeholders, including UNHCR staff, partners, government counterparts, refugee elders, and additionally vulnerable populations. Primary data collection includes Key Informant Interviews (KIIs), Focus Group Discussions (FGDs), and household quantitative data across Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan, covering visible and hard-to-reach refugee populations. In-depth case studies also provide granular insight into how UNHCR interventions affect beneficiaries and the practical challenges of implementation.
Our methodological approach emphasises ethical engagement, safety, and confidentiality, particularly for high-risk groups including undocumented refugees, religious and ethnic minorities, artists, students, human rights defenders etc. Participatory and trauma-informed approaches ensure meaningful beneficiary input while protecting respondents from exposure.The evaluation utilises triangulation of multiple data sources and applies a structured evaluation matrix linking questions, indicators, and analytical methods, aiming to provide evidence-based insights to inform programme adaptation, strengthen operational responses, and ensure interventions remain responsive to protection, livelihood, and social cohesion challenges.
### Impact
The evaluation of the UNHCR programme provides evidence-based insights into how interventions are addressing urgent protection and livelihood challenges for refugees in Pakistan. By systematically examining the relevance, coherence, and efficiency of programme activities, the evaluation identifies what has worked, where adaptations are needed, and how a strategic pivot can effectively contribute in safeguarding refugees and vulnerable populations in Pakistan.Our findings and recommendations are expected to guide UNHCR, government counterparts, and other strategic partners in Pakistan to refine programme design and delivery, ensuring that resources are targeted effectively to maintain protection space and support resilience, and support policy advocacy. By highlighting practical lessons on delivering assistance in a restrictive enforcement environment, the evaluation aims to contribute to informed decision-making for future UNHCR programming.Through this evaluation, we aim to uphold our vision of improving lives through sustainable change. By generating actionable recommendations grounded in evidence and local realities, the evaluation strengthens the ability of UNHCR and partners to deliver interventions that protect human rights, maintain social cohesion, and enable refugees and host communities to preserve dignity, safety, and opportunities for self-reliance.
