The GRASP programme was implemented in Pakistan, with a focus on Sindh and Balochistan, where rural poverty, limited market access, and vulnerability to economic and climate shocks remain significant development challenges. The programme aimed to support poverty reduction and sustainable, gender-inclusive economic growth by strengthening value chains, improving enabling environment for rural enterprises, and promoting market-based approaches. The programme was implemented from 2019 to 2025.
The European Union (EU) commissioned final evaluation of GRASP under a framework contract held by PROMAN, with OPM Europe sub-contracted to support its implementation. The evaluation applies a systematic, timely, and evidence-based approach to assess programme performance in line with evolving EU cooperation priorities, including results-oriented programming and contributions to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).
The global objective of the evaluation is two-fold: to provide EU services, key stakeholders, and the wider public with an independent assessment of GRASP’s performance and results against its intended objectives, and to identify lessons learned, conclusions, and recommendations to inform future interventions. The specific objectives include assessing the immediate and longer-term value of GRASP support, explaining the factors underpinning observed results, and strengthening accountability for the use of EU resources.
### Challenges
In Pakistan, poverty remains predominantly rural, with a large share of the population dependent on agriculture and related activities for livelihoods. Despite agriculture’s central role in employment, exports, and food security, many rural households continue to experience multidimensional poverty. Poverty levels are particularly acute in provinces such as Balochistan and Sindh, where deprivation remains significantly above the national average.
The agriculture sector itself faces deep structural constraints. Productivity is hampered by inadequate access to quality inputs, weak extension services, limited mechanisation, land tenure challenges, and insufficient access to finance. Post-harvest losses, poor infrastructure, and weak market linkages further reduce farmers’ ability to commercialise production and compete in domestic and international value chains. These challenges are compounded by policy and regulatory distortions discouraging private investment and limiting opportunities for small producers. External shocks such as climate-related disasters and market volatility further complicate reform efforts. 
The evaluation of the GRASP programme assesses whether programme resources were effectively targeted to address rural poverty and structural constraints in the agriculture sector, while supporting accountability, evidence-based learning, and informed decision-making for future EU programming in Pakistan and globally. 
### Expertise
The final evaluation of GRASP employs a theory-based, mixed-methods approach to generate robust and triangulated evidence on programme performance against the OECD-DAC and EU evaluation criteria: relevance, coherence, efficiency, effectiveness, sustainability, impact, and EU added value. This approach enables a comprehensive assessment of both results achieved and the causal pathways through which change has occurred.
Contribution Analysis forms the core of the evaluation methodology. It is used to test the programme’s intervention logic and underlying assumptions, assessing GRASP’s contribution to strengthening the enabling environment for micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs) in rural areas of Sindh and Balochistan.
To ensure evidence is credible and contextually grounded, the evaluation combines qualitative and quantitative data collection tools, including key informant interviews (KIIs), focus group discussions (FGDs), structured surveys, and an extensive desk review of programme documentation and secondary data. Stakeholders engaged included implementing partners, government counterparts, private sector actors, and beneficiaries.
### Impact
The final evaluation of the GRASP programme will generate clear, evidence-based insights into what has worked, what has not, and why, to support rural economic development in Pakistan. By systematically assessing achievements and results, the evaluation informs EU and government’s future decision-making on policies, programmes, and investments aimed at reducing rural poverty and promoting inclusive, climate-smart and sustainable growth.
By transparently assessing results and challenges, the evaluation contributes to improved decision-making and responsible use of development resources at the national and sub-national levels in Pakistan.
The evaluation objectives align well with our purpose of improving lives through sustainable change, ensuring that similar interventions in future are grounded in evidence, responsive to local contextual realities, and capable of delivering sustainable, inclusive impact.
