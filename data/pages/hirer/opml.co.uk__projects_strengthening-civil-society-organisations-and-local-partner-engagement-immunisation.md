Nigeria has one of the largest numbers of zero-dose and under-immunised children globally. While civil society organisations (CSOs) play a critical role in reaching underserved communities, engagement between governments, CSOs, and development partners is often fragmented, limiting the effectiveness and sustainability of immunisation efforts.
Gavi established a new global funding mechanism to strengthen collaboration with CSOs and local partners and improve vaccination coverage. Oxford Policy Management, working with Mannion Daniels as Fund Manager, supports the implementation of this mechanism in Nigeria. We provide technical assistance (TA) and in-country coordination to strengthen how CSOs are selected, supported, and integrated into national immunisation systems.
The project works closely with the Federal Ministry of Health, the Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI), Gavi Alliance partners, and CSOs across Nigeria. We aim to help build sustainable partnerships that improve routine immunisation delivery, strengthen health system governance, and increase access to vaccines for hard-to-reach communities.
### Challenges
Despite strong policy commitment to immunisation, many communities in Nigeria remain underserved due to geographic, social, and institutional barriers. CSOs often have deep community reach but face constraints in funding access, coordination with government systems, and capacity to manage results-based programmes.
At the system level, coordination between EPI teams and civil society remains fragmented, often with no shared frameworks for contracting, performance management, or accountability. Nigeria’s large size and federal structure add further complexity, necessitating national coordination while being responsive to local needs across diverse geographies.
Ensuring that CSO investments contribute directly to national immunisation priorities, while building long-term institutional capacity, requires strong technical support, trusted relationships, and continuous learning.
### Expertise
We provide embedded strategic and technical support to strengthen the full lifecycle of CSO engagement. This includes supporting mapping and outreach of CSOs and local partners, contributing to transparent sourcing and selection processes, and providing technical input to due diligence assessments to ensure CSOs are well-positioned to deliver immunisation activities.
During implementation, we work alongside government and partners to strengthen coordination, performance management, and learning. The project supports CSOs to align activities with national EPI priorities, improve data use, and demonstrate results. We also facilitate regular dialogue between CSOs, government stakeholders, and Gavi partners to strengthen accountability and shared ownership.
Our approach combines health systems governance expertise with practical delivery support. We focus on building sustainable systems, including coordination platforms, engagement frameworks, and capacity strengthening plans, so that governments can more effectively manage CSO partnerships beyond the life of the project.
### Impact
This project is expected to improve how immunisation programmes reach communities that are often missed by traditional health services. Stronger coordination and clearer accountability mechanisms will enable CSOs to contribute more effectively to national vaccination priorities.
The project is also expected to support increased immunisation coverage, particularly among zero-dose and under-immunised children, through closer alignment of CSO activities with national immunisation programmes. At the same time, it will strengthen the capacity of government immunisation teams, including the Ministry of Health and EPI, alongside CSOs to plan, manage, and monitor CSO-supported activities, improving collaboration and delivery of results. Over time, improved approaches to CSO engagement will support more sustainable and locally led immunisation delivery.
This work contributes to our mission to improve lives through sustainable policy change by strengthening health system governance, supporting localisation, and enabling inclusive partnerships that deliver better health outcomes for vulnerable populations.
