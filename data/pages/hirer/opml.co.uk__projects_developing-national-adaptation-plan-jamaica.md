Jamaica, a Small Island Developing State (SIDS) in the Caribbean Sea, is highly vulnerable to climate change and natural hazards. The government, through the Planning Institute of Jamaica, is dedicated to addressing these risks, evidenced by its United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) Paris Agreement commitments and Jamaica’s Vision 2030. Building on existing adaptation efforts, this project's core aim is to develop a comprehensive, gender-inclusive National Adaptation Plan (NAP) and implementation strategy for Jamaica.
Key stakeholders include government ministries, private and public sectors, academia, civil society organisations, and vulnerable communities. The initiative will update vulnerability assessments across critical sectors like health, water, agriculture, and coastal resources, and prioritise adaptation options. Central to the work are debates on ensuring effective, integrated, and nationally owned adaptation, supported by robust financing mechanisms and strong capacity-building, all aligned with UNFCCC NAP guidelines and Green Climate Fund requirements. 
### Challenges
This project primarily seeks to address Jamaica's profound vulnerability to climate change by developing a fully integrated, gender-responsive and financed NAP. The core problem lies in transitioning from existing, often fragmented, adaptation initiatives to a comprehensive, strategic framework that systematically builds resilience across all identified critical sectors and for all vulnerable populations. Key challenges include:
  * Data gaps and inconsistency: Updating vulnerability assessments requires robust, up-to-date climate scenarios and disaggregated data, which may be incomplete or inconsistent by sectors.
  * Coordination complexities: Integrating climate change adaptation into existing national and subnational development plans across diverse government agencies, private sector, and civil society requires intricate coordination.
  * Capacity building: Ensuring sufficient technical and institutional capacity at all levels for both planning and implementing adaptation strategies is a significant hurdle.
  * Financing mobilisation: A critical challenge in identifying, costing, and securing sustainable domestic and international financing for the NAP's implementation.
  * Inclusive engagement: Guaranteeing meaningful participation from all stakeholders, particularly vulnerable communities, to ensure the NAP is truly gender-responsive and socially inclusive requires careful facilitation and buy-in.


### Expertise
The project's methodology is a flexible, country-context-tailored approach that integrates both top-down (science-first) and bottom-up (policy-first) elements, firmly guided by the UNFCCC NAP Technical Guidelines. Key methods include:
  * Comprehensive document review: Stocktaking national documents like Vision 2030, the National Policy for Gender Equality, Climate Change Policy Framework, stakeholder consultation reports, and NDCs to identify adaptation needs, options, and emerging issues, aligning with GCF and national policies.
  * Inclusive stakeholder engagement: Multi-level consultations, including sensitisation sessions, five focus/working group sessions, and seven appraisal/prioritisation sessions, engaging public/private sectors, academia, civil society, municipal corporations, and vulnerable communities to build ownership and ensure gender and social inclusivity (GESI).
  * Updated vulnerability and adaptation (V&A) assessments: Revisiting existing V&A reports for Jamaica's five priority sectors (water, health, tourism, agriculture, and coastal resources) plus human settlements, incorporating gender and social inequality considerations and climate change scenarios.
  * Prioritisation toolkit: Utilising decision matrices and scoring to appraise and prioritise gender-specific adaptation options based on costs, benefits, effectiveness, and efficiency.
  * Monitoring, evaluation, and learning framework (MELF): Developing a robust MELF with a data collection plan and a sensitisation and communication strategy to track NAP effectiveness, facilitate iterative updates, and disseminate lessons learned.
  * Costing and financing strategy: A crucial element, estimating required funds for NAP implementation and outlining strategies for diversifying domestic and international funding sources.


The diverse team, including key experts Ruth Phillips Itty (Team Leader), Dainalyn Swaby (Social Analyst), and non-key experts covering V&A, adaptation finance, and GESI, brings extensive experience from LDCs and SIDS, ensuring world-class, evidence-based project delivery. 
### Impact
The primary outcome of this project will be a gender-inclusive NAP for Jamaica, complete with a robust implementation strategy, a Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learning Framework (MELF), and a concrete financing strategy. This NAP will be evidence-based, drawing on updated vulnerability assessments, and incorporate prioritised, gender-specific adaptation options validated by diverse stakeholders.
The positive impact will be significant: enhanced climate resilience across critical sectors (water, health, agriculture, coastal resources, and human settlements) and for vulnerable communities. This will lead to reduced social and economic disadvantage caused by climate change impacts. The project will also strengthen Jamaica's institutional and technical capacity for adaptation planning, implementation, and resource mobilisation, making future climate action more effective and sustainable.
By building Jamaica's resilience and integrating climate action into its development agenda, the project expects to strengthen public policy and contribute to long-term sustainable development.
