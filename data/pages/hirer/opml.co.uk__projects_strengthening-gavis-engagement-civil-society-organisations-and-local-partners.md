We provide technical assistance (TA) at national and sub-national levels to strengthen health systems and support the Government of Ethiopia and Gavi in ensuring equitable and sustainable access to immunisation under the GAVI Fund Manager contract. Through embedded TA and collaboration with civil society organisations across high-priority districts, we focus on reaching 250,000 zero-dose children and remote populations for immunisation coverage.

Oxford Policy Management and Mannion Daniels (jointly referred to as the fund manager) are working to expand and strengthen Gavi’s capacity to engage with Civil Society Organisations (CSOs) to increase demand for and access to immunisation services for zero-dose and under-vaccinated children and communities in hard-to-reach and underserved woredas (districts) in Ethiopia.
We strive to expand and strengthen Gavi’s capacity to engage with CSOs and local partners through five activities: a) sourcing and selection, b) due diligence and pre-award assessment, c) grant management, d) cross-country learning, and e) project closeout.
These activities enable progress towards the following country-level objectives in Ethiopia:
  * Extend immunisation services to reach zero-dose, under-immunised children and missed communities;
  * Increase capacity and quality of vaccine storage and distribution to improve vaccine availability, especially in the last mile;
  * Improve planning, coordination and monitoring of supply chain management at all levels;
  * Design and implement social, and behaviour change interventions;
  * Increase advocacy for social and political commitment as well as increased accountability for equitable immunisation at all levels; and
  * Strengthen partnerships with local and community actors to improve demand for immunisation.


After thorough review of 13 eligible proposals by an independent Technical Review Panel and pre-contracting due diligence, Gavi and Immunisation Services Desk of the Federal Ministry of Health of Ethiopia (ISD/MoH) - the responsible body for the [Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI)](https://www.moh.gov.et/en/Expanded_Program_on_Immunization_%28EPI%29?language_content_entity=en) - successfully approved five CSOs based in Ethiopia in December 2024. After signing contract, the five CSOs commenced implementation of project activities in January 2025. To facilitate timely and effective implementation of the project, Dr Ayana Yeneabat, the Strategic Advisor from Oxford Policy Management, provides strategic and technical oversight, facilitates planning, monitoring; and reporting, and supports alignment and coordination of CSO project with ISD/MoH, Regional Health Bureaus, Gavi and other key immunisation stakeholders. 
The project support targets eighty woredas (districts) which are among those identified as priority one with high zero-dose (ZD) and under-vaccinated (UV) children in the country. Underlying factors for the high ZD and UV children range from weak and disruption of services due to conflict and internal displacement, hard terrains, highly mobile populations in pastoralist communities to low awareness of takers about immunisation services. 
### Challenges
The main challenges that this project seeks to address include:
  * increasing demand for immunisation services through community awareness on the benefits of vaccines;
  * improving quality of services through training of healthcare workers; and
  * expanding access through periodic intensification of routine immunisation (PIRI) including by increasing outreaches and mobile services in hard-to-reach communities.


The project also aims to facilitate last mile delivery and strengthen immunisation supply chain by availing maintenance of refrigerators and other cold chain equipment and training of health workers on temperature monitoring. One key area of emphasis is addressing issues with target estimates through baseline assessment using headcounts of zero-dose and under-vaccinated children; and supporting timely and accurate recording and reporting of vaccinated children.
The project uses adaptable and tailored approaches to bridge gaps in health systems, including facilitating context-specific immunisation activities in conflict areas through negotiated access and ‘hit-run’ outreaches, and for accessing children among mobile populations, especially in pastoralist communities. 
**Risks and issues:**
  * Some project locations, e.g., Oromia and Amhara regions, are conflict-affected, posing significant risks to access and service delivery. To mitigate these challenges, CSOs are actively collaborating with local community leaders for negotiated access. The trusted figures play a crucial role in bridging access gaps, ensuring that immunisation services reach vulnerable populations. Additionally, CSOs will support EPI teams to deliver vaccines at ‘times of tranquillity’ through ‘hit and run’ operations, and where feasible negotiate for access.
  * Frequent movement of pastoralist communities hinders consistent contact and follow-up. To mitigate this, CSOs intend to deploy mobile health teams equipped with GPS tracking to monitor migration patterns and ensure continuous access to immunisation and other essential health services.
  * Vaccine hesitancy, resulting in lower acceptance, reduced uptake, and the persistence of zero-dose children. This risk requires intensifying targeted community engagement initiatives, including awareness campaigns, education programs, and involvement of religious and community leaders as well as faith actors, traditional healers, and healing centres to dispel myths and misconceptions.
  * Previous experiences in managing grants differ among the CSOs. While the webinars, pre-contracting due diligence and subsequent follow-ups have contributed to better understanding of the Gavi CSO project among the grantees, there is a need to continue mentorship and supportive supervision to ensure that financial expenditures are well aligned to project workbooks.


### Expertise
Significant and inclusive engagement with EPI, MoH, Gavi, Regional Health Bureaus (RHBs), and community actors has resulted in effective progress.
In March 2025 the project was formally launched with an event attended by the MoH and the EPI team, all contracted CSOs, Gavi, and the fund manager. All five CSOs presented to and deliberated on project activity plans with the respective RHBs for technical alignment and Regional Finance Bureaus for local accountability, conducted familiarisation of project at regional and zonal launches with participation of local counterparts, community leaders and immunisation partners (for local buy-in and improved coordination). All have recruited, oriented and deployed regional, zonal and woreda project staff who are embedded in the respective counterpart EPI teams for synergy and sustainability. Activities have commenced with baseline rapid assessments of cold chain status, review of data management system, and engagement with immunisation activities. All the CSOs were invited by MoH/EPI and are participating in Immunisation Technical Working Group (TWG) meetings. Likewise, a coordination platform between the fund manager and the five CSOs has been established and held its first meeting. The grantees will collaborate with regional, zonal and woreda EPI officers in project implementation.
A key element of ensuring timely and quality delivery of CSO project activities is the establishment of regular monitoring and coordination mechanisms complimented by field supportive supervision. As part of this, there are performance reports, monthly CSOs coordination meetings where EPI counteracts also participate, and joint performance review meetings with the respective RHBs and partners.
### Impact
Solid engagement with the national and regional EPI team right from developing and dissemination of call for proposals, selection of target woredas, to soliciting feedback in the selection of CSOs, project launch and in planning of activities, development of terms of reference and staff recruitment. The approach to embed project technical experts to existing EPI teams with government counterparts availing workstations in their premises demonstrates strong buy-in and adding value to existing systems. These engagements have contributed to the alignment of activities, fostered collaboration and buy-in of the project by government counterparts at all levels- an essential and critical enabler for success of new initiatives such as the Gavi CSO engagement and sub-national HSIS technical assistance projects. Initial targets have been set using data available from June 2024. These include vaccination of 250,622 zero-dose and under-immunised children, and 214,452 individuals from missed communities accessing community outreach and engagement activities.
**Lessons learnt**
Lesson 1: An inclusive engagement process involving key counterparts from national and regional government levels and collaborating institutions, including Gavi and the fund manager, has significantly enriched the design and selection process, ensuring robust technical and political buy-in across all levels.
Lesson 2: CSOs with local knowledge and understanding of context have established presence and are rapidly adapting to the changing dynamics. They can negotiate access in conflict situations to deliver much needed life-saving immunisation services.Lesson 3: Leveraging the process to build capacity among local CSOs adds significant value to the project selection and pre-contracting due diligence. This value is further enhanced through joint discussions, physical visits, online back-stopping calls and follow-up guidance on the purpose, approaches and requirements of the CSO fund mechanism. These efforts benefit implementation rollout and support the documentation of best practices and lessons learned by each CSO and, where applicable, their implementing partners.Lesson 4: Contextualised modalities have emerged as particularly important in Ethiopia. With the aim of increasing vaccination coverage among zero-dose and under-vaccinated children, each project must be designed to be context-specific and fit-for-purpose. For instance, in areas where vaccine hesitancy is a significant challenge, engagement with community leaders and women's groups is prioritised, whereas in conflict-affected regions, outreach efforts and hit-and-run strategies are implemented.Lesson 5: Linking Gavi HSIS with the CSO project adds value for harmonisation and equity in immunisation support. This was evident from the immunisation partners mapping conducted through HSIS TA, which revealed duplication of Gavi and other supported activities in few woredas/districts. Informed by the findings, Amhara RHB requested the MoH and CSO change one woreda with overlap of Gavi CIFF and CSO project activities to another priority woreda with no partner support- demonstrating data-driven alignment before roll-out of activities.Lesson 6: Coordination platforms play a crucial role in fostering partnership among different immunisation programme players- including government and non-government actors- for better immunisation outcome. Engagement of CSOs in coordination, planning and performance reviews both contributes to better outcome and visibility of Gavi’s CSO fund mechanism where experience and lessons from the initiative will inform its potential expansion.
